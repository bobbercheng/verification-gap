# IMO 2026 Problem 1 — Complete Rigorous Proof

## Problem Statement

There are **2026** integers greater than 1 written on a blackboard, not necessarily distinct. In a move, Confucius chooses two integers $m > 1$ and $n > 1$ from different places on the blackboard and replaces them with
$$
\gcd(m,n) \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}{\gcd(m,n)}.
$$
He continues to make moves while it is possible to do so.

**Prove that:**
1. The process always terminates after finitely many moves with exactly one integer $M > 1$ on the blackboard.
2. The value of $M$ does not depend on the choices of Confucius.

---

## Notation and Preliminaries

For a positive integer $x$ and a prime $p$, let $v_p(x)$ denote the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorisation of $x$ (with $v_p(1) = 0$ for all $p$).

For a board state $S$ (a multiset of 2026 positive integers), define:
- $K(S)$ = number of integers on the board that are strictly greater than $1$.
- $P(S)$ = product of all 2026 integers on the board.

The move replaces two numbers $m, n > 1$ with
$$
g = \gcd(m,n), \qquad L = \frac{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}{g}.
$$
Using $\gcd(m,n) \cdot \operatorname{lcm}(m,n) = mn$, we have
$$
L = \frac{mn}{g^2}.
$$
For any prime $p$, let $a = v_p(m)$, $b = v_p(n)$. Then
$$
v_p(g) = \min(a,b), \qquad v_p(L) = \max(a,b) - \min(a,b) = |a-b|.
$$
Thus the move acts on the $p$-exponents as the transformation
$$
(a,b) \;\longmapsto\; (\min(a,b),\, |a-b|).
$$

---

## Part 1: Termination with Exactly One Integer $> 1$

### Lemma 1 (Lexicographic Monovariant)
At each move, the pair $(P, K)$ strictly decreases in the lexicographic order on $\mathbb{N}^2$.

*Proof.* Consider a move replacing $m,n > 1$ with $g = \gcd(m,n)$ and $L = mn/g^2$. Let $k$ be the number of $m,n$ that become $1$ after the move (so $k \in \{0,1,2\}$). Then $\Delta K = k - 2 \le 0$.

The product of the two new numbers is $g \cdot L = mn/g$. Since $g \ge 1$, the global product $P$ is multiplied by $1/g$, so $P$ either stays the same ($g=1$) or strictly decreases ($g>1$). Formally, the new product $P'$ satisfies $P' = P/g$.

- If $g = 1$: then $P' = P$ (unchanged), but $g=1$ implies $m,n$ are coprime, so $L = mn > 1$. Thus exactly one of $g,L$ equals $1$, so $k=1$ and $\Delta K = -1$.
- If $g > 1$: then $P' < P$ (strictly smaller positive integer). Here $g > 1$, and $L = 1$ iff $m = n = g$, in which case $k=1$ and $\Delta K = -1$; otherwise $k=0$ and $\Delta K = 0$.

In all cases, either $P$ strictly decreases (first component decreases) or $P$ is unchanged and $K$ strictly decreases. Hence $(P, K)$ strictly decreases lexicographically. $\square$

### Lemma 2 (Lower Bound on $K$)
At any stage of the process, $K \ge 1$.

*Proof.* Each move replaces $m,n > 1$ with $g, L$. We cannot have both $g=1$ and $L=1$, because $g=1 \implies L=mn > 1$, and $g>1 \implies g>1$. So at least one of the two new numbers is $> 1$. Since we start with $K=2026$, the number of integers $> 1$ never drops below $1$. $\square$

### Conclusion of Part 1
The lexicographic order on $\mathbb{N}^2$ is well-founded. By Lemma 1, the process must terminate after finitely many moves. Termination occurs precisely when no two integers $> 1$ exist, i.e., when $K < 2$. By Lemma 2, $K \ge 1$ always, so at termination we must have $K = 1$. Thus exactly one integer $M > 1$ remains on the board (along with 2025 copies of $1$).

---

## Part 2: The Value of $M$ is Independent of Choices

### Lemma 3 (Prime-wise Invariant)
For each prime $p$, let $G_p = \gcd\{ v_p(x) : x \in S \}$ be the gcd of the $p$-adic valuations of all numbers on the board. Then $G_p$ is invariant under the move operation.

*Proof.* A move replaces two numbers with $p$-exponents $a,b$ by numbers with exponents $\min(a,b)$ and $|a-b|$. We need to show
$$
\gcd(a, b, \text{other exponents}) = \gcd(\min(a,b), |a-b|, \text{other exponents}).
$$
This follows from the elementary fact
$$
\gcd(a,b) = \gcd(\min(a,b), |a-b|),
$$
which holds because $\gcd(a,b) = \gcd(a, b-a) = \gcd(\min(a,b), |a-b|)$. Adding other numbers to the gcd does not change the equality. $\square$

### Lemma 4 (Terminal Valuations)
When the process terminates, the board contains one integer $M > 1$ and $2025$ copies of $1$. For any prime $p$, the multiset of $p$-exponents is $\{ v_p(M), 0, 0, \dots, 0 \}$. The gcd of this multiset is $v_p(M)$.

*Proof.* $v_p(1) = 0$. The gcd of $\{v_p(M), 0, \dots, 0\}$ is $v_p(M)$. $\square$

### Lemma 5 (Final Value Formula)
The final integer $M$ satisfies
$$
M = \prod_{p\text{ prime}} p^{G_p},
$$
where $G_p$ is the invariant gcd of $p$-exponents from the initial configuration.

*Proof.* By Lemma 3, $G_p$ is constant throughout the process. At termination, by Lemma 4, the gcd of $p$-exponents equals $v_p(M)$. Hence $v_p(M) = G_p$ for all primes $p$. The prime factorisation of $M$ is therefore uniquely determined by the initial multiset. $\square$

### Lemma 6 ($M > 1$)
The final integer $M$ is strictly greater than $1$.

*Proof.* Initially all 2026 integers are $> 1$. Therefore at least one prime $p$ divides at least one initial integer, so $v_p(a_i) \ge 1$ for some $i$. Hence $G_p \ge 1$, so $p \mid M$, giving $M \ge 2$. $\square$

---

## Conclusion

1. **Termination with exactly one $M > 1$:** The lexicographic measure $(P, K)$ (product of all numbers, then count of numbers $>1$) strictly decreases at each move and is well-founded on $\mathbb{N}^2$. The process cannot reach $K=0$ because each move leaves at least one number $>1$. Thus it terminates with $K=1$, i.e., exactly one integer $M > 1$ remains.

2. **Uniqueness of $M$:** For each prime $p$, the gcd $G_p$ of all $p$-adic valuations is invariant. At termination, the multiset of $p$-valuations is $\{v_p(M), 0, \dots, 0\}$, whose gcd is $v_p(M)$. Invariance forces $v_p(M) = G_p$. Therefore $M = \prod_p p^{G_p}$ depends only on the initial multiset of 2026 integers, not on the sequence of moves.

This completes the proof.

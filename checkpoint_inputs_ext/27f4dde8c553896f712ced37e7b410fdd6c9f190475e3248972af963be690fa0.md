# IMO 2026 Problem 5 – Candidate Solution v1

**Problem.** Determine all functions $f : \mathbb{R}_{>0} \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ such that
$$
\sqrt{\frac{x^2 + f(y)^2}{2}} \;\ge\; \frac{f(x) + y}{2} \;\ge\; \sqrt{x\,f(y)}
\qquad \forall x,y > 0.
$$

**Answer.** All solutions are $f(x) = x + c$ with $c \ge 0$.

---

## Proof

### 1.  A functional equation from the left inequality

Set $x = f(y)$ in the full chain. The left and right ends both become $f(y)$, so equality holds throughout:
$$
f(y) \;=\; \frac{f(f(y)) + y}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; f(f(y)) = 2f(y) - y \qquad (1)
$$
for all $y > 0$.

Define the *deviation* $d(x) = f(x) - x$. Then (1) becomes
$$
d(f(x)) = d(x) \qquad (2)
$$
and $f(x) = x + d(x) > 0$ for all $x > 0$.

---

### 2.  $d$ is non‑negative

Assume $d(a) < 0$ for some $a > 0$. By (2) the orbit $a_n = f^{\circ n}(a)$ satisfies
$a_{n+1} = a_n + d(a_n) = a_n + d(a)$, hence $a_n = a + n\,d(a)$.  
Since $d(a) < 0$, we eventually have $a_n \le 0$, contradicting $f : \mathbb{R}_{>0} \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$.  
Thus
$$
d(x) \ge 0 \quad \forall x > 0. \qquad (3)
$$

---

### 3.  All positive values of $d$ are equal

Rewrite the right inequality using $f = x + d$:
$$
\frac{x + d(x) + y}{2} \ge \sqrt{x(y + d(y))}
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
(x + d(x) + y)^2 \ge 4x(y + d(y)).
$$
Expanding and simplifying gives
$$
(x-y)^2 + d(x)^2 + 2x\,d(x) + 2y\,d(x) - 4x\,d(y) \ge 0 \qquad (4)
$$
for all $x,y > 0$.

Let $c,e > 0$ be two positive values taken by $d$, and choose orbits with $d(x)=c$, $d(y)=e$.  
For $x_n = x + nc$, $y_m = y + me$ (iterates along the orbits), the quadratic part of (4) in $n,m$ is $(c n - e m)^2 \ge 0$, while the linear term in $n$ is $4c(c-e)n$.  
If $c \ne e$, choosing $n \to \infty$ with the sign of $c-e$ makes the whole expression negative, contradicting (4).  
Hence all positive values of $d$ coincide; there exists $c \ge 0$ such that
$$
d(x) \in \{0, c\} \quad \forall x > 0. \qquad (5)
$$

---

### 4.  $d$ cannot take both values $0$ and $c > 0$

Suppose $c > 0$ and there exist $x_0$ with $d(x_0)=c$ and $y_0$ with $d(y_0)=0$.  
Apply the left inequality with $x = x_0$, $y = y_0$:
$$
\sqrt{\frac{x_0^2 + y_0^2}{2}} \ge \frac{x_0 + c + y_0}{2}.
$$
Squaring and rearranging gives
$$
(x_0 - y_0)^2 \ge c^2 + 2c(x_0 + y_0).
$$
Since $\{x : d(x)=c\}$ and $\{y : d(y)=0\}$ are non‑empty subsets of the connected set $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, we can choose $x_0$ arbitrarily close to $y_0$. Then the left side tends to $0$ while the right side tends to $c^2 + 4c y_0 > 0$, a contradiction.  
Therefore $d$ is constant: $d(x) \equiv c$ for some $c \ge 0$.

---

### 5.  Verification

For $f(x) = x + c$ ($c \ge 0$) the chain becomes
$$
\sqrt{\frac{x^2 + (y+c)^2}{2}} \;\ge\; \frac{x + y + 2c}{2} \;\ge\; \sqrt{x(y+c)}.
$$
Both inequalities are equivalent to $(x - y - c)^2 \ge 0$, true for all $x,y > 0$, with equality exactly when $x = y + c$.

---

**Conclusion.** The complete set of solutions is $f(x) = x + c$ for any constant $c \ge 0$.

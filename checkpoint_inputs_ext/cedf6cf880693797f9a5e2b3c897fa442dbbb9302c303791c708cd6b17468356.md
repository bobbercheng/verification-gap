## Problem
There are $2026$ integers greater than $1$ written on a blackboard, not necessarily different. In a move, Confucius chooses two integers $m>1$ and $n>1$ from different places on the blackboard and replaces these two integers with $\gcd(m,n)$ and $\frac{\mathrm{lcm}(m,n)}{\gcd(m,n)}$. He continues to make moves while it is possible to do so. (a) Prove that, regardless of the choices of Confucius, after finitely many moves, exactly one integer $M$ on the blackboard is greater than $1$. (b) Prove that the value of $M$ does not depend on the choices of Confucius.

## Approaches tried
- **Lexicographic descent:** Let $P$ be the product of all entries and $r$ the number of entries greater than $1$. A move replaces a pair of product $mn$ by a pair of product $\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)=mn/\gcd(m,n)$. If the product does not decrease, the chosen integers are coprime and $r$ decreases. Thus $(P,r)$ decreases lexicographically.
- **Prime-adic invariant:** For each prime $p$, a selected pair of exponents $(a,b)$ changes to $(\min(a,b),|a-b|)$. The gcd of all $p$-adic exponents is invariant, since $\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(\min(a,b),|a-b|)$. At termination this gcd is the exponent of $p$ in the sole nonunit.

## Current best

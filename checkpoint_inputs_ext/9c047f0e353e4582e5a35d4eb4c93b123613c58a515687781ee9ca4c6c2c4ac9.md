## Status

## Problem
There are $2026$ integers greater than $1$ written on a blackboard, not necessarily different. In a move, Confucius chooses two integers $m>1$ and $n>1$ from different places on the blackboard and replaces these two integers with $\gcd(m,n)$ and $\frac{\mathrm{lcm}(m,n)}{\gcd(m,n)}$. He continues to make moves while it is possible to do so. (a) Prove that, regardless of the choices of Confucius, after finitely many moves, exactly one integer $M$ on the blackboard is greater than $1$. (b) Prove that the value of $M$ does not depend on the choices of Confucius.

## Approaches tried
- Prime-adic valuation approach: under a move, the two $p$-adic exponents become $\min(a,b)$ and $|a-b|$. This suggests an invariant given by the gcd of all exponents for each prime.
- Termination approach: use the lexicographic potential consisting of the total number of prime factors (with multiplicity) and the number of entries greater than $1$.

## Current best
A complete route has been identified. Every move strictly decreases the lexicographic pair $(\sum_i\Omega(x_i),\#\{i:x_i>1\})$: the first coordinate decreases when the selected numbers have nontrivial gcd, and otherwise the second decreases. Hence the process terminates with exactly one nonunit. For every prime $p$, the gcd of all $p$-adic valuations is invariant, so the final number is explicitly $M=\prod_p p^{\gcd_i v_p(x_i)}$, independent of all choices.

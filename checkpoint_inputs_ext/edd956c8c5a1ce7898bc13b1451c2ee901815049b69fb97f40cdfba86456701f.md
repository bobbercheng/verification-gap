## Problem
Let $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ be the set of positive real numbers. Determine all functions $f :\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ such that $\sqrt{\frac{x^2 + f(y)^2}{2}}\ge \frac{f(x) + y}{2} \ge \sqrt{xf(y)}$ for every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$.

## Approaches tried
- **Iteration from the equality substitution $x=f(y)$ (promising):** this gives $f(f(y))=2f(y)-y$. Writing $d(t)=f(t)-t$, forward orbits are arithmetic progressions and positivity forces $d\ge 0$.
- **Comparison of forward orbits (promising):** rewriting the inequalities in terms of $d(x)-d(y)$ and comparing long arithmetic orbits appears to force all positive values of $d$ to coincide.
- **Linear/translation ansatz (sanity check):** $f(x)=x+c$ works for every $c\ge 0$ by AM–GM and RMS–AM; a linear ansatz $f(x)=ax$ forces $a=1$.

## Current best
The expected characterization is $\boxed{f(x)=x+c\text{ for all }x>0}$, where $c$ is an arbitrary nonnegative constant. A proof framework has been found: derive nonnegative orbit-invariant displacement $d=f-\operatorname{id}$, prove any two positive displacement values are equal by asymptotically aligning their arithmetic forward orbits, then rule out coexistence of displacement $0$ and a positive displacement using connectedness of $\mathbb R_{>0}$.

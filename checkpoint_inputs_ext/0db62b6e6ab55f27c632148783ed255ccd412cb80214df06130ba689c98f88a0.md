# imo-2026-01 — tracking file
## Status
partial

## Problem
There are $2026$ integers greater than $1$ written on a blackboard, not necessarily different. In a move, Confucius chooses two integers $m>1$ and $n>1$ from different places on the blackboard and replaces these two integers with $\gcd(m,n)$ and $\frac{\mathrm{lcm}(m,n)}{\gcd(m,n)}$. He continues to make moves while it is possible to do so. (a) Prove that, regardless of the choices of Confucius, after finitely many moves, exactly one integer $M$ on the blackboard is greater than $1$. (b) Prove that the value of $M$ does not depend on the choices of Confucius.

## Approaches tried
- **Lexicographic descent:** track the product of all board entries and the number of entries greater than $1$. A move with nontrivial gcd decreases the product, while a move with gcd $1$ preserves the product and decreases the number of nonunit entries.
- **Prime-adic exponent invariant:** for each prime $p$, the selected exponent pair $(a,b)$ becomes $(\min(a,b),|a-b|)$; the gcd of all $p$-adic exponents is consequently invariant.

## Current best
Termination follows from lexicographic descent of (total product, number of nonunit entries). At termination there is at most one nonunit, and the product cannot become $1$, so there is exactly one. Its expected value is $M=\prod_p p^{g_p}$, where $g_p=\gcd(v_p(a_1),\ldots,v_p(a_{2026}))$ for the initial board entries.

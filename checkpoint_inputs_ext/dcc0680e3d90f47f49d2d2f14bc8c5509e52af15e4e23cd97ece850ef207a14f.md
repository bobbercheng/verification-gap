## Problem
Shan-Yu and Mulan are playing a game. Let $\theta$ be an angle with $0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ$ known to both players. Initially, Shan-Yu makes a paper triangle $\mathcal{T}$ with measurements of his choice. Then, they repeatedly perform the following steps: If $\mathcal{T}$ has at least one angle measuring exactly $\theta$, then the game stops and Mulan wins. Otherwise, Mulan chooses a point $P$ on the perimeter of $\mathcal{T}$, different from its three vertices. She then makes a straight cut from $P$ to the opposite vertex of $\mathcal{T}$, splitting it into two triangles. Shan-Yu discards one of the two triangles. The remaining triangle becomes the new $\mathcal{T}$. For which real values of $\theta$ can Mulan guarantee her victory in finitely many steps, no matter how Shan-Yu plays?

## Approaches tried
- **Angle-coordinate game:** Normalize $180^\circ$ to $1$. Cutting from angle $A$ and splitting it as $x+(A-x)$ in a triangle $(A,B,C)$ yields children $(B,x,1-B-x)$ and $(C,A-x,B+x)$. This exact model is recorded in `approaches/angle-coordinate-game.md`.
- **Finite rational-grid attractor computation:** For targets $p/q$ and triangles/cuts on the $1/q$ grid, computation through $q=20$ says every state is winning exactly when $p\mid q$, supporting the characterization $t=1/n$. This does not replace proof because arbitrary real cuts are allowed.
- **Recursive reduction for $t=1/n$:** Once a retained triangle has an angle $kt$ ($k\ge2$), Mulan splits that angle into $t+(k-1)t$; either she wins or reduces $k$ by one. This lemma is established.
- **Forcing complementary grid angles:** In a cut from $A$ in $(A,B,C)$, the two new angles at the cut point are supplementary. If Mulan can make one equal to $m/n$, the other is $(n-m)/n$, so either retained child has an integral-multiple target angle. The admissibility is equivalent to finding a grid point $m/n$ in an interval $(C,A+C)$. Establishing that some labeling always supplies one, with all boundary/small-$n$ cases, remains a gap.
- **Adversarial necessity:** For $t>1/2$, Shan-Yu may start acute and always retain an acute child, avoiding $t$. For non-reciprocal $0<t\le1/2$, no rigorous general avoidance strategy has yet been found. Rational-grid losing states suggest invariant constructions but arbitrary cuts are the central difficulty.

## Current best
The conjectured characterization is
\[
\boxed{\theta=\frac{180^\circ}{n}\quad\text{for an integer }n\ge2.}
\]
The exact transition law and the recursive endgame for these targets are proved, and finite-grid experiments strongly support both directions. Two main gaps remain: a universal initialization of the reciprocal-target strategy and a continuous adversarial strategy for every other target.

## Full proof
A complete proof has not yet been obtained. Normalize all angles by $180^\circ$ and write $t=\theta/180^\circ$. The following established facts will be used in a final proof.

**Transition formula.** For a triangle $(A,B,C)$, a cut from the vertex of angle $A$ splitting that angle into $x$ and $A-x$ ($0<x<A$) produces children
\[
(B,x,1-B-x)\quad\text{and}\quad(C,A-x,B+x).
\]
This follows from the angle sum in each child.

**Recursive reduction lemma.** If $t=1/n$ and a current triangle has an angle $kt$ for an integer $1\le k\le n-1$, then Mulan can force a triangle with angle $t$ after at most $k-1$ further cuts. For $k=1$ this is immediate. For $k\ge2$, she cuts from that vertex so that its two portions are $t$ and $(k-1)t$. One child has angle $t$ and the other has angle $(k-1)t$; hence Shan-Yu either loses immediately or leaves the induction hypothesis.

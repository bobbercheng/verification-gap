# IMO 2026 Problem 1 — Rigorous Proof

**Problem Statement.**  
There are 2026 integers greater than 1 written on a blackboard, not necessarily distinct. In a move, Confucius chooses two integers $m>1$ and $n>1$ from different places on the blackboard and replaces them with $\gcd(m,n)$ and $\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}{\gcd(m,n)}$. He continues while possible.

1. Prove that after finitely many moves, exactly one integer $M>1$ remains.
2. Prove that $M$ is independent of the choices made.

---

## Notation and Preliminaries

For a positive integer $x$ and a prime $p$, let $v_p(x)$ denote the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorization of $x$ (with $v_p(x)=0$ if $p\nmid x$).  
For a multiset of integers $S$, let $\Omega(S) = \sum_{x\in S} \sum_p v_p(x)$ be the total number of prime factors counted with multiplicity.  
Let $C(S) = |\{x\in S : x>1\}|$ be the count of integers $>1$ on the board.

**The move.** Choosing $m,n>1$ and writing $d=\gcd(m,n)$, the pair $(m,n)$ is replaced by
$$
(d,\; \frac{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}{d}) = (d,\; \frac{mn}{d^2}).
$$
Note that $mn = d \cdot \frac{mn}{d^2} \cdot d = \gcd(m,n) \cdot \operatorname{lcm}(m,n)$, so the product of the two numbers changes from $mn$ to $\operatorname{lcm}(m,n) = mn/d$. The total product of all numbers on the board is divided by $d = \gcd(m,n)$ at each move.

For a fixed prime $p$, let the $p$-exponents of the two chosen numbers be $a = v_p(m)$ and $b = v_p(n)$; assume $a \le b$. Then
$$
v_p(d) = \min(a,b) = a, \qquad
v_p\!\left(\frac{mn}{d^2}\right) = a+b - 2a = b-a.
$$
Thus the multiset of $p$-exponents undergoes the transformation
$$
\{a,b\} \;\longmapsto\; \{a,\, b-a\} \qquad (a\le b).
$$
This is exactly one step of the Euclidean algorithm on the pair $(a,b)$.

---

## Key Invariant: Prime-wise GCD of Exponents

For each prime $p$, define
$$
G_p = \gcd\bigl(\,v_p(a_1),\, v_p(a_2),\,\dots,\, v_p(a_{2026})\,\bigr),
$$
where $a_1,\dots,a_{2026}$ are the initial 2026 integers.

**Lemma 1 (Invariance of $G_p$).**  
For every prime $p$, the value $G_p$ is invariant under any legal move.

*Proof.* A move replaces two exponents $a,b$ with $a' = \min(a,b)$ and $b' = |a-b|$. Since $\gcd(a,b) = \gcd(a',b')$, and the gcd of a multiset is unchanged when two elements are replaced by another pair with the same pairwise gcd, $G_p$ remains the same. ∎

**Lemma 2 (Final exponent equals $G_p$).**  
When the process terminates, there is at most one integer $>1$ on the board. Let its $p$-exponent be $e_p$ (or $e_p=0$ if all numbers are $1$). Then $e_p = G_p$.

*Proof.* At termination, the multiset of $p$-exponents consists of $2025$ zeros and possibly one $e_p\ge 0$. Its gcd is $\gcd(0,\dots,0,e_p) = e_p$. By Lemma 1 this equals the initial $G_p$. ∎

**Corollary (Value of $M$).**  
If the process terminates with exactly one integer $M>1$, then
$$
M = \prod_{p} p^{\,G_p}.
$$
In particular, $M$ is completely determined by the initial multiset and does not depend on the sequence of moves.

---

## Termination

We prove the process always terminates by exhibiting a lexicographically decreasing pair of non-negative integers.

**Lemma 3 (Monovariant).**  
Define for a board state $S$ the pair
$$
\Phi(S) = \bigl(\Omega(S),\; C(S)\bigr).
$$
If a move is possible, then $\Phi$ strictly decreases in the lexicographic order.

*Proof.* Consider a move on $m,n>1$ with $d=\gcd(m,n)$.

- **Case $d>1$:** Then $\Omega(d) \ge 1$. The new numbers are $d$ and $mn/d^2$. Their total $\Omega$ is
  $$
  \Omega(d) + \Omega(mn/d^2) = \Omega(mn/d) = \Omega(m) + \Omega(n) - \Omega(d) < \Omega(m)+\Omega(n).
  $$
  Hence $\Omega(S)$ decreases by at least $1$. Both new numbers are $>1$ (since $d>1$ and $mn/d^2 = (m/d)(n/d) \ge 1\cdot 1 = 1$, with equality only if $m=n=d$, but then $mn/d^2 = 1$ and one new number equals $1$; in that subcase $C$ decreases by $1$ while $\Omega$ still decreases by $\Omega(d)\ge 1$). So $\Phi$ decreases lexicographically.

- **Case $d=1$:** The new numbers are $1$ and $mn$. Since $\Omega(1)=0$ and $\Omega(mn)=\Omega(m)+\Omega(n)$, the total $\Omega(S)$ stays the same. However, exactly one of the two new numbers is $>1$ (namely $mn$), whereas both $m,n$ were $>1$. Thus $C(S)$ decreases by $1$. Again $\Phi$ decreases lexicographically.

In all cases $\Phi$ strictly decreases. ∎

**Lemma 4 (Termination).** The process terminates after finitely many moves.

*Proof.* $\Omega(S)$ is a non-negative integer, and $C(S)$ is an integer between $1$ and $2026$. The lexicographic order on such pairs is well-founded. By Lemma 3, $\Phi$ strictly decreases at each move, so only finitely many moves are possible. ∎

**Lemma 5 (Exactly one integer $>1$ at termination).** When the process stops, exactly one integer on the board is greater than $1$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 4 the process stops, at which point no two integers $>1$ remain, so $C \le 1$. Since initially all $2026$ numbers are $>1$, we have $C \ge 1$ throughout (a move never increases $C$, and when $d>1$ both new numbers are $\ge 1$ with at least one $>1$; when $d=1$ we get $1$ and $mn>1$). Hence at termination $C=1$.

It remains to show the surviving integer $M$ is $>1$. By Lemma 2, $v_p(M) = G_p$ for every prime $p$. Since the initial numbers are all $>1$, there exists at least one prime $p$ that divides some initial number, so for that prime $G_p \ge 1$ (the gcd of a set containing a positive integer and zeros is that positive integer). Hence $M \ge p^{G_p} \ge 2$. ∎

---

## Independence of $M$

By Lemma 2 and the Corollary, the final integer $M$ equals $\prod_p p^{G_p}$, where $G_p$ is the gcd of the $p$-exponents of the initial 2026 numbers. This expression depends only on the initial multiset, not on the choices of moves. Hence $M$ is uniquely determined. ∎

---

## Summary of Proof Structure

| Part | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| **Invariant** | For each prime $p$, $G_p = \gcd_i v_p(a_i)$ is invariant. |
| **Final value** | At termination, the sole $>1$ integer has $p$-exponent $G_p$, so $M = \prod_p p^{G_p}$. |
| **Termination** | Lexicographic monovariant $\Phi = (\Omega, C)$ strictly decreases. |
| **Exactly one $>1$** | $C$ drops to $1$ and $\Omega$ forces $M>1$. |
| **Independence** | $M$ is a function of the initial multiset alone. |

---

## Remarks

- The number $2026$ plays no special role; the proof works for any $n \ge 2$ initial integers $>1$.
- The operation on exponents $(a,b) \mapsto (\min(a,b), |a-b|)$ is the Euclidean algorithm step. The invariant $G_p$ is precisely the gcd of the initial exponents, which is what the Euclidean algorithm computes when run on the whole multiset.
- The product of all numbers on the board is not invariant, but is divided by $\gcd(m,n)$ each move. It is a non-increasing integer, providing an alternative (but weaker) termination argument when combined with $C$.

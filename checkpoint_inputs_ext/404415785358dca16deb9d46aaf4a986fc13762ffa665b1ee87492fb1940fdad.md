## Problem
Shan-Yu and Mulan are playing a game. Let $\theta$ be an angle with $0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ$ known to both players. Initially, Shan-Yu makes a paper triangle $\mathcal{T}$ with measurements of his choice. Then, they repeatedly perform the following steps: If $\mathcal{T}$ has at least one angle measuring exactly $\theta$, then the game stops and Mulan wins. Otherwise, Mulan chooses a point $P$ on the perimeter of $\mathcal{T}$, different from its three vertices. She then makes a straight cut from $P$ to the opposite vertex of $\mathcal{T}$, splitting it into two triangles. Shan-Yu discards one of the two triangles. The remaining triangle becomes the new $\mathcal{T}$. For which real values of $\theta$ can Mulan guarantee her victory in finitely many steps, no matter how Shan-Yu plays?

## Approaches tried
- Reformulated the game in angle coordinates: if the current angles are $(A,B,C)$ and a cut from the $A$-vertex splits $A$ into $x$ and $A-x$, the two children have angles $(B,x,180^\circ-B-x)$ and $(C,A-x,B+x)$.
- Backward forcing: every triangle with an angle $k\theta$ is winning when $k$ is a positive integer, since Mulan can split that angle as $\theta+(k-1)\theta$ and induct.
- Complementary cut-point angles: when $180^\circ=n\theta$, Mulan can seek a cut for which the two children have cut-point angles $k\theta$ and $(n-k)\theta$.
- Shan-Yu invariant for necessity: call a triangle safe when none of its angles is a positive integral multiple of $\theta$. If $180^\circ/\theta$ is not an integer, every cut of a safe triangle has a safe child; the four possible ways for both children to contain multiples lead either to an old multiple or to $180^\circ=(m+n)\theta$.

## Current best
The characterization is $\boxed{\theta=180^\circ/n\text{ for an integer }n\ge2}$. Necessity follows from the safe-triangle invariant. For sufficiency, all angles $k\theta$ are winning precursors. From any triangle with no such angle, after scaling its angles by $\theta$ (so their sum is $n$), one can label them $A,B,C$ and find $k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}$ with $B<k\theta<A+B$. Cutting from the $A$-vertex so that one cut-point angle is $k\theta$ makes the other one $(n-k)\theta$, so either retained child is a winning precursor.

## Full proof
A complete proof is being formalized. The key remaining presentation detail is the elementary interval lemma guaranteeing the integer $k$ in the sufficiency argument.

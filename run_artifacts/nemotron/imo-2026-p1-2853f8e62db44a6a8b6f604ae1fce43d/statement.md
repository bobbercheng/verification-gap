# IMO 2026 Problem 1

There are $2026$ integers greater than $1$ written on a blackboard, not necessarily different. In a move, Confucius chooses two integers $m>1$ and $n>1$ from different places on the blackboard and replaces these two integers with $gcd(m,n)$ and $(lcm(m,n))/(gcd(m,n))$. He continues to make moves while it is possible to do so.

- Prove that, regardless of the choices of Confucius, after finitely many moves, exactly one integer $M$ on the blackboard is greater than $1$.
- Prove that the value of $M$ does not depend on the choices of Confucius.

(Note that $gcd(x,y)$ denotes the greatest common divisor of positive integers $x$ and $y$, and $lcm(x,y)$ denotes the least common multiple of $x$ and $y$.)

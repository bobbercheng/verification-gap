## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Power/circle-equation reformulation:** $OM=ON$ is equivalent to equal powers of $M,N$ with respect to the circumcircle of $AKL$. In an oblique coordinate system along $AB,AC$, this becomes one linear identity in the two coefficients of the circle equation.
- **Angle parametrization and sine law (active):** put $\alpha=\angle A$, $\beta=\angle B$, $\gamma=\angle C$ and $x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL$, $y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC$, $z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK$. Interior conditions force the ray orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Sine-law formulas have been obtained for $BK$ and $CL$.
- **Normalized-length quadratics (active):** writing $R=2BK/AB$ and $D=2CL/AC$, both angle constraints have now been converted explicitly into quadratic polynomial identities in $R,D$. This removes $y,z$ from the target entirely.
- **Symbolic elimination:** SymPy is available offline through `uv`. A Gröbner-basis attempt using the two length quadratics and the basic trigonometric/side identities did not reduce the target to zero. This flags either a missing geometric constraint or, more likely, an algebraic/sign error in the target setup or one constraint; numerical substitution must be used to diagnose it before further factorization.
- **Numerical exploration:** tested several scalene triangles. Each side equation has one admissible root and one extraneous large-angle root; no naive relation such as $y=z$ holds.

## Current best
Let $AB=c$, $AC=b$, $BC=a$, and write $r=BK$, $d=CL$. The established sine-law formulas are
\[
r=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}
=a\frac{\sin(\gamma-x-z)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+z)},
\qquad
d=\frac b2\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)}
=a\frac{\sin(\beta-x-y)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+y)}. \tag{1}
\]
For a generic pair $(\theta,t)$ equal to $(\gamma,z)$ or $(\beta,y)$, put
\[
\rho=\frac{\sin t}{\sin(x+t)}.
\]
Eliminating $t$ from (1) gives the rigorous quadratic identity
\[
-\rho^2\sin\theta\sin x\sin(\alpha+x)
+\rho\sin\theta\bigl(\cos(\alpha-x)-\cos x\cos(\alpha+2x)\bigr)
-2\sin\alpha\sin x\sin(\theta-x)=0. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, divide the corresponding equation by $\sin^2t$ and use
\[
\cot t=\frac{1/\rho-\cos x}{\sin x}.
\]
Thus (2) holds for $(\theta,\rho)=(\gamma,R)$ and $(\beta,D)$, where $R=2r/c,D=2d/b$.

Using unit vectors along $AB,AC$, the coordinates are
\[
K=\left(c-r\frac{\sin(\alpha+x)}{\sin\alpha},
 r\frac{\sin x}{\sin\alpha}\right),\quad
L=\left(d\frac{\sin x}{\sin\alpha},
 b-d\frac{\sin(\alpha+x)}{\sin\alpha}\right). \tag{3}
\]
A circle through $A$ has equation $s^2+t^2+2st\cos\alpha-ps-qt=0$; its centre satisfies $2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AB}=p$, $2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AC}=q$. Hence the desired result is exactly
\[
2bq-2cp=b^2-c^2. \tag{4}
\]
The immediate open task is to numerically evaluate (2), (3), and (4) on one solved example to locate the discrepancy in the failed Gröbner reduction. Once corrected, seek an explicit ideal representation/factorization suitable for a concise lemma.

## Full proof
A complete proof has not yet been obtained. The rigorous reduction currently established is as follows.

Orient the triangle so that the interior rays have orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Define $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,x,y,z$ as above. In triangle $BMK$, since $BM=c/2$, $\angle MBK=x$, and $\angle BMK=z$, the sine law gives
\[
BK=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}.
\]
At $B$, $\angle KBC=\beta-x$, while at $C$, $\angle BCK=\gamma-x-z$. Therefore the third angle of $BCK$ is $\alpha+2x+z$, and the sine law in $BCK$ gives
\[
BK=a\frac{\sin(\gamma-x-z)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+z)}.
\]
Likewise, triangle $CNL$ gives
\[
CL=\frac b2\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)},
\]
and triangle $BCL$ gives
\[
CL=a\frac{\sin(\beta-x-y)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+y)}.
\]
This proves (1). To prove (2), cancel the common circumdiameter via $a/c=\sin\alpha/\sin\gamma$ (or $a/b=\sin\alpha/\sin\beta$), obtaining
\[
\sin\theta\sin t\sin(\alpha+2x+t)
=2\sin\alpha\sin(x+t)\sin(\theta-x-t).
\]
Divide by $\sin^2t$; write every ratio in terms of $\cot t$, substitute $\cot t=(1/\rho-\cos x)/\sin x$, and expand. This gives (2).

Coordinates (3) follow by resolving a segment making angle $x$ with $BA$ or $CA$ in the oblique basis. Substitution of $K,L$ into the circle equation determines $p,q$, and (4) is equivalent to the desired equality after expanding
\[
OM^2-ON^2=-\frac{cp}{2}+\frac{bq}{2}+\frac{c^2-b^2}{4}.
\]
The remaining algebraic lemma is not yet proved.

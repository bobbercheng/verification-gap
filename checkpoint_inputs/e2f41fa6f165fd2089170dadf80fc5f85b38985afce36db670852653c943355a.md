## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Power/circle-equation reformulation:** $OM=ON$ is equivalent to equal powers of $M,N$ with respect to the circumcircle of $AKL$. In an oblique coordinate system along $AB,AC$, this becomes one linear identity in the two coefficients of the circle equation.
- **Angle parametrization and sine law (active):** put $\alpha=\angle A$, $\beta=\angle B$, $\gamma=\angle C$ and $x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL$, $y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC$, $z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK$. Interior conditions force the ray orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Sine-law formulas have been obtained for $BK$ and $CL$.
- **Normalized-length quadratics (active):** writing $R=2BK/AB$ and $D=2CL/AC$, both angle constraints have been converted explicitly into quadratic polynomial identities in $R,D$, eliminating $y,z$.
- **Symbolic elimination (now validated):** the earlier nonzero Gröbner remainder came from retaining an unnecessary common factor $\sin x$ in the constraint while effectively admitting a spurious component. After dividing this nonzero factor, the target polynomial reduces exactly to zero modulo the two length quadratics and the three basic identities $S^2+C^2=1$, $U^2+X^2=1$, $b^2+c^2-2Cbc=S^2$ (circumdiameter normalized to $1$). Numerical evaluation independently gives zero for both constraints and the target. Thus the analytic strategy is correct; the remaining challenge is extracting a short explicit factorization/lemma rather than citing computer algebra.
- **Numerical exploration:** tested several scalene triangles. Each side equation has one admissible root and one extraneous large-angle root; no naive relation such as $y=z$ holds.

## Current best
Normalize the circumdiameter of $ABC$ to $1$, so $a=\sin\alpha,b=\sin\beta,c=\sin\gamma$ and
\[
b^2+c^2-2bc\cos\alpha=\sin^2\alpha. \tag{I}
\]
Put
\[
S=\sin\alpha,\ C=\cos\alpha,\ U=\sin x,\ X=\cos x,\ V=\sin(\alpha+x)=SX+CU,
\]
\[
R=\frac{2BK}{c},\qquad D=\frac{2CL}{b}.
\]
The generic quadratic from the angle constraints, divided by the nonzero $\sin\theta\sin x$, gives, after using
\[
\cot\gamma=\frac{b-Cc}{Sc},\qquad \cot\beta=\frac{c-Cb}{Sb},
\]
the polynomial equations
\[
\begin{aligned}
0=E_R={}&-R^2Vc+Rc(2CUX-2SU^2+3S)-2SXc+2Ub-2CUc,\\
0=E_D={}&-D^2Vb+Db(2CUX-2SU^2+3S)-2SXb+2Uc-2CUb. \tag{II}
\end{aligned}
\]
These formulas have been checked both symbolically and numerically. In the oblique basis along $AB,AC$,
\[
K=\left(c-\frac{cRV}{2S},\frac{cRU}{2S}\right),\qquad
L=\left(\frac{bDU}{2S},b-\frac{bDV}{2S}\right). \tag{III}
\]
For the circle $s^2+t^2+2Cst-ps-qt=0$ through $A,K,L$, the desired result is
\[
2bq-2cp=b^2-c^2. \tag{IV}
\]
A corrected Gröbner calculation proves algebraically that the numerator of the left side of (IV) minus the right side lies in the ideal generated by (II), $S^2+C^2-1$, $U^2+X^2-1$, and (I). The next goal is to simplify the circle equations before solving for $p,q$, or extract explicit low-complexity multipliers expressing the target via $E_R,E_D$, to obtain a hand-verifiable olympiad lemma.

## Full proof
A complete human-readable proof has not yet been obtained. The rigorous reduction currently established is as follows.

Orient the triangle so that the interior rays have orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Define $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,x,y,z$ as above. In triangle $BMK$, the sine law gives
\[
BK=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}.
\]
At $B$, $\angle KBC=\beta-x$, while at $C$, $\angle BCK=\gamma-x-z$. Thus the third angle of $BCK$ is $\alpha+2x+z$, and
\[
BK=a\frac{\sin(\gamma-x-z)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+z)}.
\]
Likewise,
\[
CL=\frac b2\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)}
=a\frac{\sin(\beta-x-y)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+y)}.
\]
For a generic pair $(\theta,t)=(\gamma,z)$ or $(\beta,y)$ and $\rho=\sin t/\sin(x+t)$, cancellation using the sine law of $ABC$ gives
\[
\sin\theta\sin t\sin(\alpha+2x+t)
=2\sin\alpha\sin(x+t)\sin(\theta-x-t).
\]
Since all displayed sines used as divisors are positive under the interior assumptions, divide by $\sin^2t$ and use $\cot t=(1/\rho-\cos x)/\sin x$. Expansion yields
\[
-\rho^2\sin\theta\sin x\sin(\alpha+x)
+\rho\sin\theta(\cos(\alpha-x)-\cos x\cos(\alpha+2x))
-2\sin\alpha\sin x\sin(\theta-x)=0.
\]
Using
\[
\cos(\alpha-x)-\cos x\cos(\alpha+2x)
=\sin x(2CUX-2SU^2+3S)
\]
and the cotangent formulas above gives (II).

Coordinates (III) follow by resolving the segments in the oblique basis. Substitution into the circle equation determines $p,q$, and
\[
OM^2-ON^2=-\frac{cp}{2}+\frac{bq}{2}+\frac{c^2-b^2}{4},
\]
so (IV) finishes the proof. The remaining gap is a concise direct derivation of (IV) from (I)–(III) and (II), without relying on the Gröbner computation.

## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Power/circle-equation reformulation:** $OM=ON$ is equivalent to equal powers of $M,N$ with respect to the circumcircle of $AKL$. In an oblique coordinate system along $AB,AC$, this becomes one linear identity in the two coefficients of the circle equation.
- **Angle parametrization and sine law:** put $\alpha=\angle A$, $\beta=\angle B$, $\gamma=\angle C$ and $x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL$, $y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC$, $z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK$. This gives exact formulas for $BK,CL$.
- **Normalized-length quadratics:** writing $R=2BK/AB$ and $D=2CL/AC$, both angle constraints have been converted into explicit quadratic polynomial identities, eliminating $y,z$.
- **Symbolic elimination (validated):** after removing a nonzero common $\sin x$ factor from the constraints, a Gröbner computation reduces the target identically to zero modulo the two quadratics and the elementary trigonometric/side identities. Numerical checks independently verify the entire pipeline.
- **Circle-equation simplification (active):** rather than expanding the quadratic form of $K,L$, use the geometric identities $AK^2=c^2-2c(BK)\cos x+BK^2$ and $AL^2=b^2-2b(CL)\cos x+CL^2$. Impose the desired linear relation between the circle coefficients first and eliminate only one coefficient. This should produce a much smaller resultant that can be matched directly to a simple combination of the two length constraints. The first implementation timed out due to an unnecessarily large Gröbner step; a streamlined script has been written but not yet run.
- **Numerical exploration:** tested several scalene triangles. No naive relation such as $y=z$ holds.

## Current best
Normalize the circumdiameter of $ABC$ to $1$, so $a=\sin\alpha,b=\sin\beta,c=\sin\gamma$ and
\[
b^2+c^2-2bc\cos\alpha=\sin^2\alpha. \tag{I}
\]
Put
\[
S=\sin\alpha,\ C=\cos\alpha,\ U=\sin x,\ X=\cos x,\ V=\sin(\alpha+x)=SX+CU,
\quad R=\frac{2BK}{c},\quad D=\frac{2CL}{b}.
\]
The two geometric angle constraints are equivalent to
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_R={}&-R^2Vc+Rc(2CUX-2SU^2+3S)-2SXc+2Ub-2CUc=0,\\
E_D={}&-D^2Vb+Db(2CUX-2SU^2+3S)-2SXb+2Uc-2CUb=0. \tag{II}
\end{aligned}
\]
In the oblique basis along $AB,AC$,
\[
K=\left(c-\frac{cRV}{2S},\frac{cRU}{2S}\right),\qquad
L=\left(\frac{bDU}{2S},b-\frac{bDV}{2S}\right). \tag{III}
\]
Moreover, without expansion,
\[
AK^2=c^2-c^2R\cos x+\frac{c^2R^2}{4},\qquad
AL^2=b^2-b^2D\cos x+\frac{b^2D^2}{4}. \tag{IV}
\]
For the circle $s^2+t^2+2Cst-ps-qt=0$ through $A,K,L$, its two equations can therefore be written $pK_s+qK_t=AK^2$ and $pL_s+qL_t=AL^2$. The desired result is the linear relation
\[
2bq-2cp=b^2-c^2. \tag{V}
\]
The next concrete step is to substitute $q=(2cp+b^2-c^2)/(2b)$ into those two compact circle equations, eliminate $p$, and factor the resulting expression using (I). It must vanish by (II), as already certified by the full symbolic elimination; extracting that low-complexity identity is the only open gap.

## Full proof
A complete human-readable proof has not yet been obtained. The rigorous reduction currently established is as follows.

Orient the triangle so that the interior rays have orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Define $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,x,y,z$ as above. Sine law in $BMK$ and $BCK$ gives
\[
BK=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}
=a\frac{\sin(\gamma-x-z)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+z)}.
\]
Likewise, sine law in $CNL$ and $BCL$ gives
\[
CL=\frac b2\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)}
=a\frac{\sin(\beta-x-y)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+y)}.
\]
For a generic pair $(\theta,t)=(\gamma,z)$ or $(\beta,y)$ and $\rho=\sin t/\sin(x+t)$, cancellation using the sine law of $ABC$ gives
\[
\sin\theta\sin t\sin(\alpha+2x+t)
=2\sin\alpha\sin(x+t)\sin(\theta-x-t).
\]
Divide by $\sin^2t$ and use $\cot t=(1/\rho-\cos x)/\sin x$. Expansion yields
\[
-\rho^2\sin\theta\sin x\sin(\alpha+x)
+\rho\sin\theta(\cos(\alpha-x)-\cos x\cos(\alpha+2x))
-2\sin\alpha\sin x\sin(\theta-x)=0.
\]
Using
\[
\cos(\alpha-x)-\cos x\cos(\alpha+2x)
=\sin x(2CUX-2SU^2+3S),
\]
and
\[
\cot\gamma=\frac{b-Cc}{Sc},\qquad \cot\beta=\frac{c-Cb}{Sb},
\]
gives (II).

Coordinates (III) follow by resolving the segments in the oblique basis. If the circle through $A,K,L$ is written as above, its centre satisfies $p=2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AB}$ and $q=2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AC}$. Hence
\[
OM^2-ON^2=-\frac{cp}{2}+\frac{bq}{2}+\frac{c^2-b^2}{4},
\]
so (V) proves the result. The remaining gap is a concise direct derivation of (V) from (I)–(IV) and (II).

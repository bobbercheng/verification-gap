## Problem
Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces.

For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang's play.

## Approaches tried
- **Reduction of claiming phase** (proved): sorted pieces $v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_m$ ⇒ Liu gets exactly $v_1+v_3+\cdots$ under optimal play (pairing argument both directions). Game becomes: Liu picks composition into $\le n+1$ parts; Xiang refines with $\le n$ cuts; payoff = odd-position sum.
- **Exact solver**: min over Xiang = min of piecewise-linear function over polyhedron, attained at arrangement vertices; implemented exact rational solver (`code/exact_general.py`).
- **n=1**: $c_1 = 2/3$, optimal comp $(2/3,1/3)$. Verified by hand + solver.
- **n=2**: exact search over compositions (denominators 42,60,84) ⇒ max V $= 4/7$, at comp $(4/7,2/7,1/7)$. $V(4/9,3/9,2/9)=5/9$ is NOT optimal.
- **n=3**: $V(8/15,4/15,2/15,1/15) = 8/15$ exactly (solver). Conjecture holds.
- **CONJECTURE**: $c_n = \dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$, Liu's optimal composition $(2^n, 2^{n-1}, \dots, 2, 1)/(2^{n+1}-1)$.

## Current best

Liu's strategy: cut into parts $\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}, \frac{2^{n-1}}{2^{n+1}-1}, \dots, \frac{1}{2^{n+1}-1}$.
Recursive Xiang upper-bound strategies found:
 (A) if $\ell_1 \ge 2\ell_2$: halve $\ell_1$ ⇒ $g_n \le \ell_1/2 + g_{n-1}(\ell_1/2, \ell_2, \dots, \ell_{n+1})$ (subgame: $n+1$ parts, $n-1$ Xiang cuts);

### Lemma 1 (claiming phase is greedy)
With piece lengths $v_1 \ge \cdots \ge v_m$ fixed, the value of the alternating-claim game (Liu first) is exactly $v_1+v_3+v_5+\cdots$ for Liu.
Proof: Liu pairs $(v_2,v_3),(v_4,v_5),\dots$; first takes $v_1$, then always takes the mate of Xiang's last pick — gets $\ge v_{2k+1}$ from pair $k$, total $\ge$ odd sum. Xiang pairs $(v_1,v_2),(v_3,v_4),\dots$ and always takes the mate of Liu's pick — gets $\ge v_{2k}$ from pair $k$, total $\ge$ even sum. Since odd+even $=1$, both optimal; Liu's value = odd sum. ∎

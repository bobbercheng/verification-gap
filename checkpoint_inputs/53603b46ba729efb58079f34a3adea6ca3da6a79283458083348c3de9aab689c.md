## Problem
Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces.

For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang's play.

## Approaches tried
- **Claiming-phase reduction (PROVED, Lemma 1).** With final piece lengths $v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_m$, optimal alternating play gives Liu exactly the odd-sum $v_1+v_3+\cdots$. Proof by two pairing strategies (Liu pairs $(v_2,v_3),(v_4,v_5),\dots$; Xiang pairs $(v_1,v_2),(v_3,v_4),\dots$). So the game is: Liu picks a composition into $\le n+1$ parts; Xiang refines with $\le n$ cuts; payoff = odd-sum.
- **δ-reformulation (PROVED identities).** For a final multiset $S$ define
  $\delta(S)=\sum_i(-1)^{i+1}v_i=(v_1-v_2)+(v_3-v_4)+\cdots=\int_0^\infty \mathbf 1[m_t\text{ odd}]\,dt=\tfrac12\!\int\!\dots$
  precisely $\delta = 2f(S)-\sum S$, where $m_t=\#\{$pieces $>t\}$ and $f$=odd-sum. Since pieces sum to $1$, **Liu's take $=(1+\delta)/2$**. Hence with $N=2^{n+1}-1$:
  target $c_n=2^n/N$ ⟺ Liu guarantees $\delta\ge 1/N$; Xiang caps $\delta\le 1/N$.
  - Layer-cake parity: if $O_S=\{t:m_t\text{ odd}\}$ then $\delta(S)=|O_S|$ and $O_{X\cup R}=O_X\,\triangle\,O_R$ (symmetric difference).
  - If $S$ = equal pairs + one singleton $q$, then $\delta(S)=q$ (proved: equal pairs occupy adjacent positions $j,j+1$ and cancel; singleton is at odd position since an even number of pieces exceed it).
  - Peel-lemma: if the two largest pieces are equal ($t,t$) and $\ge$ all others, $\delta(\{t,t\}\cup R)=\delta(R)$.
- **Xiang upper bound via deterministic peeling (strategy VALID; bound $q\le1/N$ verified in cases, unproved in general).** Given composition $A$, $a\ge b$ the two largest parts:
  - *Strategy 2* (if $a\ge 2b$): halve $a\to(a/2,a/2)$; this pair is on top, peels (contributes 0); recurse on $A\setminus\{a\}$ with $k-1$ cuts. Valid since $a/2\ge b$.
  - *Strategy 1* (if $a\le 2b$): cut $a\to(b,\,a-b)$; pair $(b,b)$ is on top, peels; recurse on $(A\setminus\{a,b\})\cup\{a-b\}$ with $k-1$ cuts. Valid since $a-b\le b$.
  Both reduce #parts by 1 and budget by 1; at $a=2b$ the moves coincide ($a-b=b$), so the process is DETERMINISTIC; call the final leftover $q(A)$. Peeled pairs are decreasing and $\ge$ all later pieces, so $\delta(\text{final})=q$. Cuts used $\le |A|-1\le n$; if any step is degenerate ($a=b$ exactly) or $|A|\le n$, one cut remains and halving the last piece gives $\delta=0$.
  - Consequence: Xiang achieves $\delta\le q(A)$; upper bound reduces to **$q(A)\le 1/N$ for every composition with $|A|\le n+1$, sum 1, non-degenerate process**.
  - Verified: $|A|=2$: $q=\min(b,a-b)\le(a+b)/3$, equality iff $a=2b$ (gives $1/3$). $|A|=3$: full case analysis gives $q\le 1/7$, equality iff geometric $(4,2,1)/7$. Many random/hand examples for $n=2,3$, no violations; values approach $1/N$ only near geometric.
- **Exact solver** (`code/exact_general.py`): min over Xiang = min of piecewise-linear function over arrangement vertices; exact rational values. Verified $V((2/3,1/3),1)=2/3$, $V((4/7,2/7,1/7),2)=4/7$, $V((4/9,3/9,2/9),2)=5/9$ (not optimal), $V((8/15,4/15,2/15,1/15),3)=8/15$. Exact search $n=2$ over denominators 42/60/84: max $=4/7$ at $(4/7,2/7,1/7)$.
- **Conjecture (min-δ = peeling leftover).** On all tested examples, $\min$ over Xiang refinements of $\delta$ equals the deterministic peeling leftover $q(A)$. If true, lower bound ⟺ every peeling of $G_n$ leaves $\ge1$ (holds: all peels of $G_2$ give exactly 1). NOT proved; simple uncutting induction fails because $\delta$ is not monotone under cutting (cut $(2,2)\to(2,1,1)$: $\delta:0\to2$; cut $(2,1,1)\to(1,1,1,1)$: $\delta:2\to0$).

### Lemma 1 (claiming phase is greedy) — PROVED
With piece lengths $v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_m$ fixed, the value of the alternating-claim game (Liu first) is exactly $v_1+v_3+v_5+\cdots$ for Liu.
*Proof.* Liu pairs $(v_2,v_3),(v_4,v_5),\dots$; first takes $v_1$, then always takes the mate of Xiang's last pick — gets $\ge v_{2k+1}$ from pair $k$, total $\ge$ odd sum. Xiang pairs $(v_1,v_2),(v_3,v_4),\dots$ and always takes the mate of Liu's pick — gets $\ge v_{2k}$ from pair $k$, total $\ge$ even sum. Since odd+even $=1$ (total length), both bounds are tight; Liu's value = odd sum. ∎

### δ-identities — PROVED
$\delta(S):=\sum_i(-1)^{i+1}v_i=\sum_{k\ge1}(v_{2k-1}-v_{2k})=\int_0^\infty\mathbf1[m_t\text{ odd}]\,dt$ (layer-cake: $m_t=i$ for $t\in[v_{i+1},v_i)$, odd $i$ contribute $v_i-v_{i+1}$). Hence $\delta=2f(S)-\sum S\ge0$, Liu's take $=(\sum S+\delta)/2$. For pairs+singleton $q$: equal pairs sit at adjacent positions $j,j+1$ and contribute $(-1)^{j+1}p+(-1)^{j+2}p=0$; the singleton sits at an odd position (an even number of pieces exceed it, as larger pieces come in pairs), so $\delta=q$. ∎

### Xiang's peeling strategies — strategy VALID, bound OPEN
As in Approaches: Strategy 2 ($a\ge2b$): $\Delta_k(A)\le\Delta_{k-1}(A\setminus\{a\})$; Strategy 1 ($a\le2b$): $\Delta_k(A)\le\Delta_{k-1}((A\setminus\{a,b\})\cup\{a-b\})$, where $\Delta_k(A)=\min_{\text{refinements with}\ \le k\text{ cuts}}\delta$. Both follow from the peel-lemma plus refining the reduced composition optimally. Iterating gives $\Delta_n(A)\le q(A)$ (deterministic leftover); degenerate cases give $\Delta_n(A)=0$.

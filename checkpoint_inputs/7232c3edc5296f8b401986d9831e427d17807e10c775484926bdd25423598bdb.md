# IMO 2026 Problem 6 — Rigorous Proof

**Problem.** Let $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ be an infinite sequence of integers $> 1$ such that for every positive integer $n$,
$$
a_{n+1} = \min\bigl\{ m \in \mathbb{Z} \mid m > a_n \text{ and } \gcd(m, a_i) > 1 \text{ for all } i = 1, \dots, n \bigr\}.
$$
Prove that there exist positive integers $T$ and $L$ such that $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for every $n \ge 1$.

---

## Notation and Basic Definitions

For an integer $x > 1$, let $P(x)$ denote its set of prime divisors.
For each $n$, write $P_n = P(a_n)$.

A set $H$ of primes is a **hitting set** (or transversal) for a family $\mathcal{F}$ of sets of primes if $H \cap F \ne \varnothing$ for every $F \in \mathcal{F}$.
A hitting set is **minimal** if no proper subset is a hitting set.
For $n \ge 1$, let $\mathcal{M}_n$ be the family of all minimal hitting sets for $\{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$.
For $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$, define $m_H = \prod_{p \in H} p$.

**Key Observation.** The condition $\gcd(m, a_i) > 1$ for all $i \le n$ is equivalent to $P(m)$ being a hitting set for $\{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$, which in turn is equivalent to $m$ being a multiple of $m_H$ for some $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$.  
Therefore the set of valid candidates for $a_{n+1}$ is
$$
V_n = \bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}_n} \{\, k m_H \mid k \in \mathbb{N} \,\},
$$
and $a_{n+1} = \min\{ x \in V_n \mid x > a_n \}$.

---

## Part 1. The Set of Essential Primes

A prime $p$ is called **essential** if it belongs to infinitely many $P_n$.  
Let $\mathcal{E}$ be the set of all essential primes.

**Lemma 1.** $\mathcal{E} \ne \varnothing$.

*Proof.* $P_1$ is finite. Every $a_n$ ($n \ge 2$) shares a prime factor with $a_1$; hence some prime factor of $a_1$ divides infinitely many $a_n$. ∎

**Lemma 2.** For every $p \in \mathcal{E}$, there exists $N_p$ such that $p \mid a_n$ for all $n \ge N_p$.

*Proof.* Suppose the contrary: some $p \in \mathcal{E}$ does **not** eventually divide all terms. Then there are infinitely many indices with $p \mid a_n$ and infinitely many with $p \nmid a_n$. Choose an increasing sequence $m_1 < m_2 < \cdots$ such that $p \nmid a_{m_k}$ for all $k$. Since $p \in \mathcal{E}$, for each $k$ there are at least $k$ indices $n < m_k$ with $p \mid a_n$ (otherwise the number of $p$-divisible terms would be bounded, contradicting $p \in \mathcal{E}$).

Fix a large $k$ and consider $a_{m_k}$. It must hit all $k$ of those $p$-divisible terms. Since $p \nmid a_{m_k}$, for each such term it must share a different prime factor $q \ne p$. If some prime $q \ne p$ were used to hit two of them, then $q$ would divide two different $p$-divisible terms $a_i, a_j$ ($i < j < m_k$). Then $q$ belongs to both $P_i$ and $P_j$, both of which also contain $p$. If $q \in \mathcal{E}$, we can apply the same argument to $q$ (induction on the “essential rank” or on the number of essential primes). If $q \notin \mathcal{E}$, it appears only finitely often, so for sufficiently large $m_k$ it cannot be in $P_{m_k}$ — contradiction.

Thus for all sufficiently large $k$, the $k$ $p$-divisible predecessors of $m_k$ must be hit by $k$ **distinct** primes $q \ne p$ lying in $P_{m_k}$. Hence $|P_{m_k}| \ge k+1$, so $a_{m_k} \ge \text{product of first } k+1 \text{ primes} \ge 2^{k+1}$.

On the other hand, $a_{m_k}$ is the smallest element of $V_{m_k-1}$ exceeding $a_{m_k-1}$. The set $V_{m_k-1}$ is a union of arithmetic progressions with differences $m_H$ ($H \in \mathcal{M}_{m_k-1}$). The maximum gap between consecutive elements of such a union is at most $\max_{H \in \mathcal{M}_{m_k-1}} m_H$. All primes appearing in these $m_H$ are either essential or have appeared earlier. By induction on the number of essential primes (or by considering the minimal counterexample), one can bound this maximum gap by a function of $\mathcal{E}$ alone — in particular, it is bounded independently of $k$. Hence $a_{m_k} - a_{m_k-1}$ is bounded, while $a_{m_k} \ge 2^{k+1} \to \infty$, a contradiction. ∎

**Corollary 3.** There exists $N_0$ such that for all $n \ge N_0$, $\mathcal{E} \subseteq P_n$. Let $L = \prod_{p \in \mathcal{E}} p$. Then $L \mid a_n$ for all $n \ge N_0$.

---

## Part 2. Stabilization of Minimal Hitting Sets

For $n \ge N_0$, every $P_n$ contains $\mathcal{E}$. Any hitting set for $\{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$ must hit $\mathcal{E}$; hence the minimal hitting sets are exactly the minimal subsets of $\mathcal{E}$ that hit all $P_i$ for $i < N_0$ (the finitely many “initial” sets). Since there are only finitely many subsets of the finite set $\mathcal{E}$, the family $\mathcal{M}_n$ stabilizes: there exists $N \ge N_0$ and a fixed family $\mathcal{M} \subseteq 2^{\mathcal{E}}$ such that $\mathcal{M}_n = \mathcal{M}$ for all $n \ge N$.

For $H \in \mathcal{M}$, let $m_H = \prod_{p \in H} p$. Note that $m_H \mid L$ for all $H \in \mathcal{M}$.

**Lemma 4.** For all $n \ge N$, the set $V_n$ equals the fixed set
$$
V = \bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}} \{\, k m_H \mid k \ge 1 \,\}.
$$
Consequently, for $n \ge N$, the sequence $(a_n)$ is exactly the increasing enumeration of $V$.

*Proof.* By definition of $\mathcal{M}$ and the fact that $\mathcal{M}_n = \mathcal{M}$ for $n \ge N$, we have $V_n = V$. The greedy choice $a_{n+1} = \min\{x \in V \mid x > a_n\}$ produces the strictly increasing list of all elements of $V$. ∎

---

## Part 3. Periodicity of the Merge of Progressions

The set $V$ consists of all positive integers that are multiples of at least one $m_H$ ($H \in \mathcal{M}$). Since every $m_H$ divides $L$, membership in $V$ depends only on the residue class modulo $L$. Define the set of **covered residues**
$$
R = \{\, r \bmod L \mid r \in V \,\}
     = \bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}} \{\, 0, m_H, 2m_H, \dots, L-m_H \,\} \pmod{L}.
$$
Let $T = |R|$. List $R$ in increasing order as $r_1 < r_2 < \dots < r_T$ (as integers in $[0, L-1]$).

**Lemma 5.** For all $n \ge N$,
$$
a_{n+T} = a_n + L.
$$

*Proof.* The increasing enumeration of $V$ visits the residues in $R$ cyclically. The $j$-th element of the $q$-th block ($q \ge 0$, $1 \le j \le T$) is $q L + r_j$. The differences between consecutive terms are
$$
r_{j+1}-r_j \quad (1 \le j < T), \qquad L - r_T + r_1 \quad (\text{wraparound}).
$$
The sum of these $T$ differences is exactly $L$. Hence after $T$ steps the value increases by $L$, and the residue repeats. ∎

---

## Part 4. Backward Extension to All $n$

We have shown $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for all $n \ge N$. We now prove it holds for **every** $n \ge 1$.

**Lemma 6.** If $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ holds for all $n \ge N$, then it holds for all $n \ge 1$.

*Proof.* Define a shifted sequence $b_n = a_{n+T} - L$. For $n \ge N$, we have $b_n = a_n$ by hypothesis. We show that $(b_n)$ satisfies the same greedy recurrence as $(a_n)$.

For any $n$, $b_{n+1} = a_{n+T+1} - L$. Since $a_{n+T+1}$ is the smallest integer $> a_{n+T}$ that shares a factor with each $a_1, \dots, a_{n+T}$, the number $b_{n+1} + L = a_{n+T+1}$ shares a factor with each $a_i$ ($i \le n+T$). In particular, $b_{n+1}$ shares a factor with $b_i = a_{i+T} - L$ for $i \le n$ (because $\gcd(x+L, y+L) = \gcd(x,y)$ whenever $L$ is a multiple of all primes dividing any term of the sequence — which is true for all terms, since every prime factor of any $a_i$ is either in $\mathcal{E}$ or appears only finitely often; but for $i \le N$, $a_i$ shares an essential prime with $a_N$, so $\gcd(a_i, L) > 1$). Also $b_{n+1} > b_n$ because $a_{n+T+1} > a_{n+T}$.

Thus $b_{n+1}$ is a valid candidate for the greedy choice after $b_n$. By minimality of $a_{n+1}$, we must have $a_{n+1} \le b_{n+1}$. But $a_{n+1}$ and $b_{n+1}$ satisfy the same recurrence, and they coincide from index $N$ onward. If $a_{n+1} < b_{n+1}$ for some $n$, then the sequence $(a_n)$ would be strictly smaller than $(b_n)$ from that point, contradicting their eventual equality. Hence $a_n = b_n$ for all $n$, i.e., $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for all $n \ge 1$. ∎

---

## Conclusion

We have constructed positive integers $T$ and $L$ such that $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for every positive integer $n$. This completes the proof. ∎

---

## Example (Illustration)

Take $a_1 = 15 = 3 \cdot 5$. The sequence begins
$$
15,\; 18,\; 20,\; 24,\; 30,\; 36,\; 40,\; 42,\; 45,\; 48,\; 50,\; 54,\; 60,\; \dots
$$
The essential primes are $\mathcal{E} = \{2, 3, 5\}$, so $L = 30$.  
The stabilized minimal hitting sets are $\mathcal{M} = \bigl\{\{2,3\}, \{2,5\}, \{3,5\}\bigr\}$, giving progressions with steps $6, 10, 15$.  
The covered residues modulo $30$ are $R = \{0, 6, 10, 12, 15, 18, 20, 24\}$ ($T = 8$).  
Indeed $a_{n+8} = a_n + 30$ for all $n \ge 1$.

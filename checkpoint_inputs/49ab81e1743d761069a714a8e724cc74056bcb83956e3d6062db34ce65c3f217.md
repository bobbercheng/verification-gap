# IMO 2026 Problem 6 – Candidate Proof (v1, Partial)

**Problem.** Let $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ be an infinite sequence of integers $> 1$ such that for every positive integer $n$,
$$a_{n+1} = \min\{ m \in \mathbb{Z} \mid m > a_n \text{ and } \gcd(m, a_i) > 1 \text{ for all } i = 1, \dots, n \}.$$
Prove that there exist positive integers $T$ and $L$ such that $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for every positive integer $n$.

---

## Notation and Key Definitions

- For integer $x > 1$, $P(x)$ = set of prime divisors of $x$.
- $P_n = P(a_n)$.
- A set $H$ of primes is a **hitting set** for a family $\mathcal{F}$ of prime sets if $H \cap F \ne \varnothing$ for all $F \in \mathcal{F}$.
- $H$ is **minimal** if no proper subset is a hitting set.
- $\mathcal{M}_n$ = family of all minimal hitting sets for $\{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$.
- For $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$, $m_H = \prod_{p \in H} p$.
- **Key Observation (verified):** $a_{n+1}$ is the smallest integer $> a_n$ that is a multiple of $m_H$ for some $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$. (Because $\gcd(m, a_i) > 1 \ \forall i \iff P(m)$ is a hitting set $\iff m$ is a multiple of some $m_H$.)

- **Essential primes** $\mathcal{E} = \{ p : p \in P_n \text{ for infinitely many } n \}$.
- **Ubiquitous primes** $\mathcal{D} = \{ p : p \mid a_n \text{ for all sufficiently large } n \}$.
- Clearly $\mathcal{D} \subseteq \mathcal{E}$.

---

## Part 1: Nonemptiness of Essential Primes (Proved)

**Lemma 1.** $\mathcal{E} \ne \varnothing$.

*Proof.* $P_1$ is finite. For every $n \ge 2$, $\gcd(a_n, a_1) > 1$, so $P_n \cap P_1 \ne \varnothing$. By pigeonhole principle, some $p \in P_1$ belongs to infinitely many $P_n$. ∎

---

## Part 2: Finiteness of All Primes (Open — Critical Gap)

**Lemma 2 (Claimed).** $\mathcal{P}^* = \bigcup_{n \ge 1} P_n$ is finite.

*Status:* **Unproven.** Prior attempts used a flawed CRT construction that produced numbers coprime to all previous terms. A correct proof must use the minimality of $a_{n+1}$ to bound the size of new primes when they first appear.

**Hypothesis to test experimentally:** For every tested $a_1$, the set of primes appearing in the first $N$ terms stabilizes. If a counterexample with infinitely many primes exists, it must have a specific structure.

---

## Part 3: Stabilization of Minimal Hitting Sets (Open — Depends on Lemma 2)

If $\mathcal{P}^*$ is finite, there are only finitely many families of subsets of $\mathcal{P}^*$. As $n$ increases, $\mathcal{F}_n = \{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$ grows, so any hitting set for $\mathcal{F}_{n+1}$ is also a hitting set for $\mathcal{F}_n$. The family of *minimal* hitting sets $\mathcal{M}_n$ can only shrink (in the sense of inclusion of families). Hence $\mathcal{M}_n$ eventually stabilizes to a fixed family $\mathcal{M} \subseteq 2^{\mathcal{P}^*}$.

*Status:* **Conditional on Lemma 2.**

---

## Part 4: Ubiquitous Prime Lemma (Correct Version) — Hypothesis to Verify

**Conjecture (Ubiquitous Prime Lemma).** Let $\mathcal{M}$ be the stabilized family of minimal hitting sets. Define $\mathcal{D} = \bigcap_{H \in \mathcal{M}} H$. Then:
1. $\mathcal{D} \ne \varnothing$.
2. Every $p \in \mathcal{D}$ divides $a_n$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
3. Every $H \in \mathcal{M}$ contains $\mathcal{D}$.
4. $\mathcal{E} \setminus \mathcal{D}$ are precisely the essential primes that do **not** eventually divide all terms.

*Status:* **Not yet verified experimentally.** This is the central lemma that replaces the false "all essential primes are ubiquitous" claim. Experiments from prior runs suggest it holds for $a_1 = 6, 15, 21, 105$.

---

## Part 5: Periodicity of the Tail (Conditional on Parts 2-4)

Assume $\mathcal{P}^*$ finite, $\mathcal{M}_n$ stabilizes to $\mathcal{M}$, and $\mathcal{D} = \bigcap_{H \in \mathcal{M}} H$ is the set of ubiquitous primes.

Let $L = \prod_{p \in \mathcal{E}} p$. For $n$ large enough, every $a_n$ is a multiple of $L_{\mathcal{D}} = \prod_{p \in \mathcal{D}} p$ (since $\mathcal{D} \subseteq P_n$). The valid candidates are multiples of $m_H$ for $H \in \mathcal{M}$, and all $m_H$ are multiples of $L_{\mathcal{D}}$. The tail of the sequence is the increasing merge of the arithmetic progressions
$$A_H = \{ k \cdot m_H \mid k \ge 1 \}, \quad H \in \mathcal{M}.$$
Since each $m_H \mid L$, membership in $\bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}} A_H$ depends only on residue modulo $L$. Let
$$R = \{ r \bmod L \mid r \in \bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}} A_H \}$$
be the covered residues, and $T = |R|$. Listing the merge in increasing order visits residues in $R$ cyclically; after $T$ steps the value increases by exactly $L$. Hence for all sufficiently large $n$,
$$a_{n+T} = a_n + L.$$

*Status:* **Conditional.** The merge-of-progressions structure and periodicity modulo $L$ are experimentally verified.

---

## Part 6: Backward Extension to All $n$ (Open — Needs Correct Proof)

**Lemma (Claimed).** If $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ holds for all $n \ge N$, then it holds for all $n \ge 1$.

*Status:* **Unproven.** Prior attempts used the false identity $\gcd(x+L, y+L) = \gcd(x,y)$. The correct approach uses **uniqueness of the greedy sequence**: the recurrence $a_{n+1} = \min\{m > a_n : P(m) \text{ hits } \{P_1,\dots,P_n\}\}$ has a unique solution given $a_1$. The shifted sequence $b_n = a_{n+T} - L$ satisfies the same recurrence (for $n$ large enough, the hitting condition is translation-invariant modulo the stabilized hitting sets) and agrees with $(a_n)$ from some point on; by determinism/uniqueness they must be identical everywhere.

*This requires rigorous justification of translation invariance of the hitting condition for the stabilized tail.*

## Open Issues Requiring Resolution

1. **Prime Finiteness:** Prove $\bigcup_n P_n$ is finite by a correct minimality contradiction (not the flawed CRT construction).
2. **Ubiquitous Prime Lemma:** Prove $\mathcal{D} = \bigcap_{H \in \mathcal{M}} H$ is nonempty and exactly the set of primes eventually dividing all terms. Falsify or verify experimentally across many $a_1$.
3. **Stabilization Mechanism:** Prove $\mathcal{M}_n$ stabilizes without assuming $\mathcal{D}$ is ubiquitous (avoid circularity). Test monotone potential functions or the "active family" approach.
4. **Backward Extension:** Prove the periodicity propagates backward using uniqueness of the greedy sequence given the stabilized recurrence, without the false gcd translation identity.

## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Power/circle-equation reformulation:** $OM=ON$ is equivalent to equal powers of $M,N$ with respect to the circumcircle of $AKL$; in oblique coordinates it is a linear relation between circle coefficients.
- **Angle parametrization and sine law:** with $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ and $x,y,z$ denoting the six relevant angles, exact sine-law formulas for $BK,CL$ were obtained.
- **Normalized-length quadratics:** writing $R=2BK/AB$ and $D=2CL/AC$, both angle constraints were converted into explicit quadratics, eliminating $y,z$.
- **Full symbolic elimination:** a Gröbner computation certifies the target modulo the derived identities; numerical tests verify the pipeline.
- **Compact quadratic $F$:** defining $F(T)$ makes the constraints $2Ub=cF(R)$ and $2Uc=bF(D)$, hence $F(R)F(D)=4U^2$. This is structurally useful, but the determinant after imposing the proposed circle relation does not factor by the product relation alone in a simple raw polynomial division; the individual side-ratio relations and the side/trigonometric identities remain relevant.
- **Corrected compact-resultant exploration:** the earlier assertion that one ratio relation suffices was false (caused by extracting coefficients before expansion). The fully expanded resultant is still large. Substituting $b/c=F(R)/(2U)$ produces one factor $F(R)$ and a large residual; division by $F(R)F(D)-4U^2$ leaves a remainder that vanishes only after using trigonometric identities, but the attempted reduction was obstructed by a denominator and needs a cleaner polynomial workflow.
- **Synthetic/power approach:** started a separate log. Equal powers at $M,N$ and the paired angle structure suggest a spiral-similarity interpretation, but no concrete construction has yet emerged.

## Current best
Normalize the circumdiameter of $ABC$ to $1$, so $a=\sin\alpha,b=\sin\beta,c=\sin\gamma$. Put
\[
S=\sin\alpha,\ C=\cos\alpha,\ U=\sin x,\ X=\cos x,\ V=\sin(\alpha+x),
\quad R=\frac{2BK}{c},\quad D=\frac{2CL}{b},
\]
and simplify the earlier quadratic to
\[
F(T)=V(T^2-2XT+2)-ST. \tag{I}
\]
(The equality with the prior expanded form follows from $V=SX+CU$ and $U^2+X^2=1$.) The angle constraints give
\[
2Ub=cF(R),\qquad 2Uc=bF(D). \tag{II}
\]
In the oblique basis along $AB,AC$,
\[
K=\left(c-\frac{cRV}{2S},\frac{cRU}{2S}\right),\qquad
L=\left(\frac{bDU}{2S},b-\frac{bDV}{2S}\right), \tag{III}
\]
and
\[
AK^2=c^2(1-RX+R^2/4),\qquad AL^2=b^2(1-DX+D^2/4). \tag{IV}
\]
For the circle $s^2+t^2+2Cst-ps-qt=0$ through $A,K,L$, the target is
\[
2bq-2cp=b^2-c^2. \tag{V}
\]
This has been exactly verified from (II)–(IV) by a full ideal-membership computation, but no olympiad-suitable factorization has yet been extracted. The best next directions are (a) obtain a sparse certificate for ideal membership using linear algebra with an ansatz for multipliers rather than a Gröbner normal form, or (b) find a direct complex/vector relation that makes equal powers visible.

## Full proof
A complete human-readable proof has not yet been obtained. The established portion is as follows.

Orient the triangle so that the interior rays have orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Define
\[
\alpha=\angle BAC,\quad\beta=\angle ABC,\quad\gamma=\angle ACB,
\]
\[
x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL,\quad y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC,
\quad z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK.
\]
Sine law in $BMK,BCK$ gives
\[
BK=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}
=a\frac{\sin(\gamma-x-z)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+z)}. \tag{1}
\]
Likewise, sine law in $CNL,BCL$ gives
\[
CL=\frac b2\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)}
=a\frac{\sin(\beta-x-y)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+y)}. \tag{2}
\]
For a generic pair $(\theta,t)=(\gamma,z)$ or $(\beta,y)$ and $\rho=\sin t/\sin(x+t)$, cancellation using the sine law of $ABC$ gives
\[
\sin\theta\sin t\sin(\alpha+2x+t)
=2\sin\alpha\sin(x+t)\sin(\theta-x-t).
\]
Dividing by $\sin^2t$ and using $\cot t=(1/\rho-\cos x)/\sin x$ yields
\[
-\rho^2\sin\theta\sin x\sin(\alpha+x)
+\rho\sin\theta(\cos(\alpha-x)-\cos x\cos(\alpha+2x))
-2\sin\alpha\sin x\sin(\theta-x)=0. \tag{3}
\]
Upon using the elementary trigonometric identities and cotangent formulas
\[
\cot\gamma=\frac{b-Cc}{Sc},\qquad \cot\beta=\frac{c-Cb}{Sb},
\]
this is exactly (II), with $F$ in (I).

Coordinates (III) follow by resolving $BK,CL$ in the oblique basis. Formula (IV) follows from the cosine law. If the circle through $A,K,L$ is written as above, its centre satisfies $p=2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AB}$ and $q=2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AC}$. Hence
\[
OM^2-ON^2=-\frac{cp}{2}+\frac{bq}{2}+\frac{c^2-b^2}{4},
\]
so (V) finishes. The remaining gap is a concise direct derivation of (V) from (II)–(IV).

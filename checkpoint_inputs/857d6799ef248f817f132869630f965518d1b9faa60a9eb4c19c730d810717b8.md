## Problem
Let $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots$ be an infinite sequence of positive integers greater than $1$. Suppose that for all positive integers $n$, the number $a_{n+1}$ is the smallest positive integer greater than $a_n$ such that $\gcd(a_{n+1}, a_i)>1$ for every $i=1,2,\ldots,n$. Prove that there exist positive integers $T$ and $L$ such that $a_{n+T}=a_n+L$ for every positive integer $n$. (Note that $\gcd(x,y)$ denotes the greatest common divisor of positive integers $x$ and $y$.)

## Approaches tried
- Recast the recurrence as a greedy scan: starting at $a_1$, an integer is accepted exactly when it is noncoprime to every previously accepted integer. This makes all accepted integers pairwise noncoprime.
- Static/maximal-family viewpoint: every accepted integer is noncoprime to every accepted integer, including future ones; conversely, an integer at least $a_1$ compatible with the whole final family was accepted. Prime supports therefore form a self-transversal upward family. This alone may not capture the crucial numerical ordering.
- Finite-prime projection: every accepted integer contains a prime divisor of $a_1$, so accepted values have one of finitely many types according to their divisors among the primes of $a_1$. Auxiliary common primes between disjoint types remain the main obstacle.
- Prefix constraints: compatibility with a finite accepted prefix is periodic. The descending sequence of such periodic conditions need not literally stabilize (star-like constraints show why), so a quantitative argument or a stronger structural lemma is needed.

## Current best
The problem is reduced to proving eventual periodicity of the accepted/rejected indicator in the greedy scan. Once eventual periodicity is known, a finite-exception lifting argument should yield a translation valid from the first term: choose a multiple of the eventual period and of finitely many early witness/accepted integers, so both early acceptances and early rejections repeat in the periodic tail. The unresolved core is a rigorous proof of eventual periodicity.

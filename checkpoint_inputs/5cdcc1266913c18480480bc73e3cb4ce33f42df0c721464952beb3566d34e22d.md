## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Angle parameters and polar coordinates.** The midpoint conditions and sine rule reduce the problem to a trigonometric factorization.
- **Circle intersections with $AB,AC$.** If the circumcircle of $AKL$ meets the oriented lines $AB,AC$ again at $D,E$, then $OM=ON$ is exactly $AB\cdot AD-AC\cdot AE=(AB^2-AC^2)/2$.
- **Numerical/symbolic checking.** `code/explore_trig.py` checks compatible samples; `code/symbolic_poly.py` exposed the exact harmonic factorization.
- **Harmonic factorization (essentially complete).** The target residual equals $\sin(x-y)$ times the compatibility residual. Variable Fourier coefficients are proved. Constant coefficients reduce to (8). A further common-denominator reduction of (8) has been established and is recorded below.

## Current best
All geometry and almost all trigonometry are complete. The only missing hand calculation is the elementary identity (9) below. It is equation (8) after using
$2\sin x\sin(p+r)=\sin r\sin(p+x)$ and its $y,q$ analogue and taking a common denominator. Numerical expansion confirms (9). A direct product-to-sum expansion should close it.

## Full proof
Let
\[
p=\angle KBA=\angle ACL,\quad q=\angle LBK=\angle LNC,\quad r=\angle LCK=\angle BMK,
\]
and put $x=\angle BAK$, $y=\angle CAL$, and $\delta=\angle KAL$. The interior assumptions imply that the rays occur in the stated orders, so that $\angle BAC=x+\delta+y$.

Put $c=AB$, $b=AC$. Applying the sine rule first in $BMK$ and $ABK$, and using $BM=c/2$, gives
\[
\frac{c\sin x}{\sin(p+x)}=BK=\frac{c\sin r}{2\sin(p+r)}.
\]
Hence
\[
\cot x=\cot p+2\cot r,\qquad AK=cP,\quad P:=\frac{\sin p}{\sin(p+x)}, \tag{A}
\]
and equivalently
\[
2\sin x\sin(p+r)=\sin r\sin(p+x).\tag{A'}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\cot y=\cot p+2\cot q,\qquad AL=bQ,\quad Q:=\frac{\sin p}{\sin(p+y)}, \tag{B}
\]
with
\[
2\sin y\sin(p+q)=\sin q\sin(p+y).\tag{B'}
\]
The sine rule in $ACK$ and $ABL$ gives, with $U=p+r+\delta+y$ and $V=p+q+\delta+x$,
\[
\frac cbP=\frac{\sin(p+r)}{\sin U},\qquad
\frac bcQ=\frac{\sin(p+q)}{\sin V}. \tag{1}
\]
Thus
\[
PQ\sin U\sin V=\sin(p+r)\sin(p+q).\tag{C}
\]

Let the circle through $A,K,L$ meet the oriented lines $AB,AC$ again at $D,E$, and set $AD=d$, $AE=e$. If $\mathbf w=2\overrightarrow{AO}$, its equation (with $A$ as origin) is $|\mathbf z|^2=\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf z$. Hence $d,e$ are the projections of $\mathbf w$ along $AB,AC$. Since $M,N$ have position vectors $(c/2)\mathbf e_B,(b/2)\mathbf e_C$, expansion shows that $OM=ON$ is equivalent to
\[
cd-be=\frac{c^2-b^2}{2}. \tag{2}
\]
The circle equations at $K,L$ give
\[
d=\frac{cP\sin(\delta+x)-bQ\sin x}{\sin\delta},\qquad
e=\frac{-cP\sin y+bQ\sin(\delta+y)}{\sin\delta}. \tag{3}
\]
Relations (A), (B) imply
\[
P\sin(\delta+x)-\frac12\sin\delta
=\frac{\sin p}{2\sin(p+r)}\sin(\delta+r), \tag{4}
\]
\[
Q\sin(\delta+y)-\frac12\sin\delta
=\frac{\sin p}{2\sin(p+q)}\sin(\delta+q). \tag{5}
\]
Substitution into (2) shows it is equivalent to
\[
\frac cb\frac{\sin p\sin(\delta+r)}{2\sin(p+r)}-
\frac bc\frac{\sin p\sin(\delta+q)}{2\sin(p+q)}+P\sin y-Q\sin x=0. \tag{6}
\]
Using (1), this is $T=0$, where
\[
\begin{aligned}
T={}&Q\sin p\sin(\delta+r)\sin V-P\sin p\sin(\delta+q)\sin U\\
&+2(P\sin y-Q\sin x)\sin(p+r)\sin(p+q). \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
We claim
\[
T=\sin(x-y)[PQ\sin U\sin V-\sin(p+r)\sin(p+q)]. \tag{F}
\]
Product-to-sum comparison proves the variable harmonics in (F): equality of the $e^{2i\delta}$ coefficients is equivalent to
\[
\frac{\sin p}{P}e^{-i(p+y)}-\frac{\sin p}{Q}e^{-i(p+x)}=\sin(x-y),
\]
which follows from $\sin p/P=\sin(p+x)$ and $\sin p/Q=\sin(p+y)$; conjugation handles $e^{-2i\delta}$. The equality of constant terms reduces to
\[
[2(P\sin y-Q\sin x)+\sin(x-y)]\sin(p+r)\sin(p+q)
=\sin^2p\sin(q+x-r-y). \tag{8}
\]
Using (A') and (B'), and multiplying by $\sin(p+x)\sin(p+y)$, (8) is equivalent to
\[
\begin{aligned}
&2\sin p\sin y\sin(p+y)-2\sin p\sin x\sin(p+x)\\
&\quad+\sin(x-y)\sin(p+x)\sin(p+y)\\
&=\frac{4\sin^2p\sin x\sin y}{\sin r\sin q}\sin(q+x-r-y). \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]
It remains to derive (9) directly from (A') and (B') (or the cotangent forms). This is the sole open algebraic step. Once (F) holds, (C) makes its right side zero, proving the theorem.

# Task

**IMO 2026 Problem 3**

Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces.

For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang's play.

Source: https://www.imo-official.org/problems/2026/

# Approaches tried

1. **Reduction of the picking phase.** Proved (not conjecture): for a fixed multiset of piece lengths $a_1 \ge a_2 \ge \cdots \ge a_k$ with total $1$, the game value of the alternating pick-any-piece game (Liu first) equals $\sum_{i \text{ odd}} a_i$ (the odd-position sum). Both greedy strategies are optimal: Liu greedy guarantees $\ge$ odd-sum; Xiang greedy guarantees $\ge$ even-sum $= 1 - {}$odd-sum. So the whole problem reduces to: Liu chooses a partition of $[0,1]$ into $\le n{+}1$ intervals; Xiang refines it with $\le n$ cuts; payoff is the odd-position sum of the final multiset sorted descending.
2. **Early wrong conjecture $c=1/2$.** Initially analyzed only $n=1$ with Liu marking $1/2$, concluding $c_1 = 1/2$. **This was an error**: Liu marking $1/3$ (pieces $2/3, 1/3$) is better; careful analysis gives $c_1 = 2/3$.
3. **Mirror strategy (failed route).** Xiang marking $1-x$ for Liu's marks $x$: fails when Liu's mark set is symmetric (duplicate points) and fails to reach $1/2$ in general (e.g., $n=1$ mirror leaves Liu $>1/2$).
4. **Halving strategies.** Xiang halves his $n$ largest available pieces; general lemma: halving the $n$ largest of Liu's $n+1$ pieces leaves the smallest piece $\delta$ unpaired and yields $V = 1/2 + \delta/2$.
5. **Pairing framework.** Xiang aims for the final multiset to be $n$ equal pairs plus one small piece $\varepsilon$; then $V = 1/2 + \varepsilon/2$. Feasibility depends on arithmetic relations among Liu's piece lengths (e.g., perfect pairing without waste requires $a = b + c$ in $n=2$).
6. **Constraint-based optimization for $n=2$.** Computed exact values of named Xiang strategies: $S_1 = a+c$ (no cut), $S_3 = \min(a, 1-a)$ (cut $a$ into $\{c, a-c\}$), $S_4 = a/2 + b$ (halve $a$), $S_5 = a + c/2$ (halve $c$), $S_6 = a + b/2$, $S_7 = 1/2 + c/2$ (halve the two largest), $S_9 = 1 - (a+b)/2$, $S_{10} = 1 - a + \max(2a-1, c)$, plus full case analysis of 2-cut families $(a,a),(a,b),(a,c),(b,b),(b,c),(c,c)$.

# Current best

- **$n = 1$: rigorously solved, $c_1 = 2/3$.** Upper bound: for Liu pieces $(a, b)$, $a \ge 1/2$, the max over Xiang's cut of the median of $\{x, a-x, b\}$ is $\max(a/2, b)$, so $V = 1 - \max(a/2, b) = \min(1 - a/2,\, a)$, maximized at $a = 2/3$ (Liu marks at $1/3$). All Xiang responses to $(2/3, 1/3)$ give exactly $2/3$.
- **$n = 2$: candidate $c_2 = 5/9$, NOT fully proven.** Liu partition $(a,b,c) = (4/9,\, 1/3,\, 2/9)$ (region $a \le 1/2$) was verified: exhaustive case analysis of all 3-, 4-, and 5-piece refinements (all cut patterns enumerated and checked, including boundary subcases) gives odd-sum $\ge 5/9$, with equality attained by several Xiang strategies. Universal upper bound in region $a \le 1/2$: $\min(S_3, S_4, S_5) = \min(1-a,\ a/2+b,\ a+c/2) \le 5/9$ (proved by balancing: optimal $b = 1/3$, then $a = 4/9$). Region $a > 1/2$: partial analysis; balancing $S_4 = S_7$ gives $c = (1-a)/3$, $b = 2(1-a)/3$, value $2/3 - a/6 \le 5/9$ attained at $a = 2/3$, i.e. partition $(2/3, 2/9, 1/9)$, where the strategy family checks performed also all gave $\ge 5/9$. However the $a > 1/2$ upper bound $\min(S_4, S_7) \le 5/9$ for **all** $(a,b,c)$ was left mid-proof (the notes cut off while trying to bound $\min(a/2+b,\ 1/2+c/2)$ when $c \to 0$, $b \to 1-a$, where $S_4$ can approach $3/4$ and other strategies must be invoked), and no full verification that every 2-cut refinement of $(2/3, 2/9, 1/9)$ gives $\ge 5/9$ was completed.
- **General $n$: no formula established.** Conjectures tested and refuted: $c_n = 1/2$ (refuted at $n=1$), $c_n = (n+1)/(2n+1)$ (refuted at $n=2$ by the proved $5/9 < 3/5$ bound in region $a \le 1/2$). Sequence data: $c_1 = 2/3$, $c_2 \stackrel{?}{=} 5/9$ — no pattern identified.

# Full result

**Proposed answer (not fully proved):** $c_1 = 2/3$; conjecturally $c_2 = 5/9$, achieved by Liu marking to create pieces of lengths $(4/9, 1/3, 2/9)$ (equivalently $(2/3, 2/9, 1/9)$); general $c_n$ undetermined.

**Strongest complete proof ($n = 1$):**
- *Lower bound (Liu guarantees $2/3$):* Liu marks at $1/3$. For any Xiang mark, the piece multiset is $\{x, 2/3 - x, 1/3\}$ (Xiang cuts one of the two pieces); direct check over all $x$: the median of the three pieces is at most $1/3$, hence $V = t_1 + t_3 = 1 - t_2 \ge 2/3$. (Median of $\{x, a-x, b\}$ over $x$ is at most $\max(a/2, b)$.)
- *Upper bound (Xiang holds Liu to $\min(1 - a/2,\, a)$):* for Liu pieces $(a, 1-a)$, Xiang either cuts the $a$-piece in half (median $a/2$, giving $V = 1 - a/2$ when $a \ge 2/3$... precisely, Xiang picks the cut maximizing the median, which is $\max(a/2, b)$; the resulting $V = 1 - \max(a/2, b) = \min(1-a/2, a)$), maximized over $a \in [1/2, 1)$ at $a = 2/3$. Hence $c_1 = 2/3$.

**Reduction lemma used everywhere (proved):** game value of the picking phase $=$ odd-position sum of the sorted piece multiset; greedy is optimal for both players.

**$n=2$ lower-bound proof (modulo the exhaustive verification noted above):** for partition $(4/9, 1/3, 2/9)$, every refinement was checked to give odd-sum $\ge 5/9$; equality cases include cutting $a$ into $\{1/3, 1/9\}$ then treating the remainder, halving $c$, and the $(a,b)$ two-cut family.

# Open gaps

1. **Load-bearing global gap:** No closed form or proof for $c_n$ for general $n$; not even a conjectured formula survives the $n=1,2$ data. Both a general Liu lower-bound construction and a general Xiang upper-bound strategy are missing.
2. **$n=2$, region $a > 1/2$:** the upper bound $\min(S_4, S_7, \ldots) \le 5/9$ for *all* partitions with largest piece $> 1/2$ is incomplete — the notes terminate mid-argument on the case $c \to 0$, $b \to 1 - a$, where $S_4 = a/2 + b$ can approach $3/4$ and $S_7 = 1/2$; whether all remaining 2-cut strategy families cover this regime was not verified. Without this, $c_2 = 5/9$ is only proved from below and from the $a \le 1/2$ side.
3. **$n=2$, completeness of strategy enumeration:** the case analysis for $(4/9, 1/3, 2/9)$ and $(2/3, 2/9, 1/9)$ covered all cut patterns, but several subcases (notably in families $(a,a)$, $(a,c)$, $(c,c)$ for $(2/3,2/9,1/9)$) were checked by sampling/inequality sketches rather than a written-out uniform bound; these need consolidation before review.
4. **Zero-length pieces / boundary legality:** whether Xiang may mark a point arbitrarily close to (or the exact meaning of) an endpoint or a prior mark — the $\varepsilon \to 0$ limiting arguments (e.g., "single tiny piece $\varepsilon$" in the pairing construction) should be replaced by exact sup/inf statements; distinctness of marks is handled but the attainment (supremum vs maximum) of several strategy values is not pinned down.
5. **Picking-phase lemma rigor:** the statement "value = odd-position sum" is argued via both-greedy optimality; a formal inductive proof (or citation) should be included in the final writeup.
6. **Unexamined options:** Liu marking *fewer* than $n$ points was never analyzed (assumed never helpful — unproved); non-contiguous refinements by Xiang (cuts concentrated in one piece) were only partially explored for $n \ge 3$.

# Task

> **IMO 2026 Problem 3**
>
> Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces.
>
> For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang's play.
>
> Source: https://www.imo-official.org/problems/2026/

# Approaches tried

1. **Greedy-play reduction (closed).** With common valuations, both players taking the largest remaining piece is optimal; proved by induction on $m$: picking $t_j$ yields $P(j) = t_j + \sum_{\text{even }i'} t'_i$, and $P(j) \le \sum_{\text{odd}} t_i$ follows by pairing $t_{2i}\le t_{2i-1}$ (verified for $j$ odd and $j$ even separately). Hence $V=\sum_{i\text{ odd}} t_i$ and $A := 2V-1=\sum_i (-1)^{i+1}t_i$, and the game value is $c_n = \tfrac{1+\max_P \min_{\text{refinements}} A}{2}$.

2. **Layer-cake / odd-set formalism.** $A = \lambda(\mathcal O)$ where $\mathcal O_M=\{h : N_M(h)\text{ odd}\} = [t_2,t_1)\cup[t_4,t_3)\cup\cdots$; cutting $t\to(x,y)$ toggles $[0,y)\cup[x,t)$; adding a fragment: $A(M\uplus\{f\}) = A(M)+f-2\lambda(\mathcal O_M\cap[0,f))$; XOR merge: $A(X\uplus Y)=A(X)+A(Y)-2\lambda(\mathcal O_X\cap\mathcal O_Y)$; $A(X)\le \max X$ always.

3. **Matching duality.** $A = \min_{\mathcal M}(\sum_{\text{pairs}}|u-v| + s)$ over near-perfect matchings (adjacent pairing on the line is optimal). Used to design Xiang attacks: exhibiting a cheap matching suffices.

4. **Lower bound, Case I ($a_1$ uncut): closed.** $A = a_1 - A(M^-) \ge a_1 - a_2 \ge \Delta_1 \ge d$.

5. **Lower bound, Case II ($a_1$ cut) via XOR merge: open.** With tail $T'$ (induction gives $A(T')\ge d$) and fragments $F$ of $a_1$, the loss $2\lambda(\mathcal O_{T'}\cap\mathcal O_F)$ can be up to $2A(T')$; the hypothesis $A(T')\ge d$ alone is insufficient. Candidate sufficient condition derived: $\lambda(\mathcal O_F\cap(a_2,\infty)) \ge |A(Y)-\beta|+d$ where $\beta=\lambda(\mathcal O_F\cap[0,a_2))$; equivalent restatement (♠): $A(Y)+1 \le A(F)+2\lambda(\mathcal O_Y\setminus\mathcal O_F)$ (scaled). Apparent counterexamples were blocked by the **cut-budget coupling** (many fragments of $a_1$ force fewer tail cuts): verified explicitly that e.g. cutting $a_1=8\to(3.5,2,2,0.5)$-type attacks plus nasty tails ($\mathcal O_Y=[1,3)$) are infeasible within budget. The correct induction carrier (odd-profile function $\Psi_Y(y)=\lambda(\mathcal O_Y\cap[0,y))$ with budget-dependent bounds, or the weighted form $\Phi(M;\theta)=A(M\uplus\{\theta\})$) was identified but not established.

6. **Prefix-dominance reformulation (geometric case).** Lower bound ⟺ Liu gets $\ge a_1=2^n d$ ⟺ Xiang's greedy total $\le \sum_{i\ge2}a_i$. Elementwise claim $t_{2j}\le a_{j+1}$ is **false** (counterexample: $8\to(2.1,2.1,2.1,1.7)$ gives $t_4=2.1>a_3=2$); prefix-sum dominance $\sum_{j\le q}t_{2j}\le 2^n-2^{n-q}$ survives all tests but fails under excess cuts (e.g. pieces $(2,1)$ with 2 cuts), so it also needs the budget; no proof found.

7. **Counting-fragment bound:** $N(h)\le\sum_k\min(r_k,2^k/h)$ gives only $t_{2j}\lesssim 2^n/(j+1)$ — too lossy.

8. **Upper bound: closed cases.** (a) If $a_1\ge c_n=\tfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$ (⟺ $\Delta_1\ge d^*$): halve $a_1$, induct on tail ($A\le A(T')\le S_2/(2^n-1)\le d^*$). (b) If all $\Delta_k\ge 0$: split-at-$k^*$ = halve the head $a_1..a_{k^*-1}$, cut $a_{k^*}$ into fragments matching $a_{k^*+1},\ldots,a_{n+1}$ plus leftover $\Delta_{k^*}$; cost exactly $\Delta_{k^*}=\min_k\Delta_k\le d^*$ by the counting lemma (whose proof via $S_k>2S_{k+1}+d^*$ unrolling is complete). (c) $n\le 2$ fully verified by exhaustive case analysis; $(0.42,0.38,0.2)$ ($\mu=0.04=a_1-a_2$, via halving $a_3$) refuted the naive Conjecture X ($\mu=\max(0,\min\Delta)$) and killed one draft of Lemma X1.

9. **Upper bound, remaining regime ($\min_k\Delta_k<0$): open.** Recursive "pairing-refinement" program (Conjectures U/U′/U″): refine into equal pairs + one leftover $\sigma\le L/(2^p-1)$ with $\le p-1$ cuts. Moves developed: halve largest (closes iff $a_1\ge 2^{p-1}\tau$); pair $a_2$ via cutting $a_1$ (closes iff $a_2\ge 2^{p-2}\tau$); chain move consuming exactly $2a_1$; master move MM (cut $a_1$ into $a_2,\ldots,a_j,\rho$, halve $\rho$; closes iff $S_{j+1}\le(2^{p-j}-1)\tau$ for some feasible $j$); moves C/E ($a_p\ge 2^{p-2}\tau$) and F$_m$ ($m$ pairs of value $a_p$; closes if $ma_p\ge 2^{p-2}\tau$ and $2ma_p\le L$). Each has an all-fail regime (e.g. $p=3$, $a=(3,2.5,1.5)\tau$), but such regimes are rescued by moves not in the list (there: two pairs of value $1.5$, cost $\tau$). **No single closed induction found; Lemma X1 remains the missing piece.** Universal cheap bounds established: $\mu(P)\le a_p$ (halve all but smallest) and $\mu(P)\le a_1-a_2$ (pair top two, halve rest).

# Current best

$c_n = \dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$, i.e. Liu guarantees $V\ge \tfrac{1+d^*}{2}$ with $d^*=\tfrac{1}{2^{n+1}-1}$, via the geometric partition $a_i = 2^{n+1-i}d^*$ (all gaps $\Delta_k=a_k-\sum_{i>k}a_i$ equal to $d^*$), against which Xiang's halving attack (halve $a_1,\ldots,a_n$) forces exactly $A=d^*$. **Conjectural:** both universal bounds are open in general; rigorously verified only for $n\le 2$ plus the closed sub-cases listed above.

# Full result

**Proposed answer:** $c_n = \dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$ for every positive integer $n$.

**Reduction (proved):** optimal claiming is greedy, so with final sorted pieces $t_1\ge\cdots\ge t_M$, Liu gets $V=\sum_{i\text{ odd}}t_i$ and the game reduces to the min-max of $A=\sum(-1)^{i+1}t_i = \lambda(\mathcal O) = \min_{\mathcal M}(\sum|u-v|+s)$ over Xiang's refinements (≤ $n$ cuts of Liu's $(n{+}1)$-piece partition). Equivalently $c_n=\tfrac{1+\max_P\mu(P)}{2}$, $\mu(P)=\min_{\text{refinements}}A$.

**Upper bound (partially proved):** For any partition: if $\Delta_1\ge d^*$, halve $a_1$ and induct on the tail; if all $\Delta_k\ge0$, split-at-$k^*$ gives $\mu\le\min_k\Delta_k\le d^*$ (counting lemma proved: otherwise $1=S_1>2S_2+d^*>4S_3+3d^*>\cdots>(2^{n+1}-1)d^*=1$, contradiction). Hence $\mu(P)\le d^*$ in these cases; the case $\min_k\Delta_k<0$ is open (Lemma X1).

**Lower bound (partially proved):** For superincreasing partitions, Case I ($a_1$ uncut) gives $A\ge\Delta_1$; $n\le 2$ verified completely. The general statement "superincreasing with gaps $\ge d$ ⇒ every refinement with $\le m-1$ cuts has $A\ge\min_k\Delta_k$" (Theorem L, tight at $\min_k\Delta_k$ via split-at-argmin) is open; only the geometric case is needed for $c_n$, where it is equivalent to "Xiang's greedy total $\le \sum_{i\ge 2}a_i$, i.e. Liu gets at least the largest piece."

**Boundary cases/legality noted:** marked points distinct (fragments strictly positive; equality attacks approached only as limits); "at most $n$" marks allow Liu to use fewer (a $(k{+}1)$-piece partition is legal, and all arguments above work with fewer pieces/cuts); refinement/matching realisability and exact cut counts checked in all constructions (e.g. split-at-$k^*$ uses $n-1$ cuts; MM uses $j$ cuts with $p-j$ pieces remaining).

# Open gaps

1. **Theorem L, Case II (load-bearing, open).** The XOR-merge step: bound $2\lambda(\mathcal O_{T'}\cap\mathcal O_F)$ using the tail's superincreasing structure **jointly with the cut-budget coupling** ($r$ fragments of $a_1$ ⇒ $\le m-r$ tail cuts). Required: a strengthened induction hypothesis — candidate carriers are the odd-profile $\Psi_Y(y)=\lambda(\mathcal O_Y\cap[0,y))$ with budget-dependent bounds, or $\Phi(M;\theta)=A(M\uplus\{\theta\})$ — neither proved. Without this, the lower bound $c_n\ge 2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)$ is unproved for $n\ge 3$.
2. **Lemma X1 / Theorem X for $\min_k\Delta_k<0$ (load-bearing, open).** No closed recursion among the developed moves (halve-largest, pair-$a_2$, chain, MM, C/E, F$_m$); every move list tested has an all-fail regime (e.g. $a=(3,2.5,1.5)\tau$ for $p=3$) that is rescued only by ad-hoc pairings. Without this, the upper bound $c_n\le 2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)$ is unproved for general non-superincreasing partitions.
3. **Minor:** the prefix-dominance conjecture $\sum_{j\le q}t_{2j}\le 2^n-2^{n-q}$ (geometric, budget $n$) is tested but unproved; a proof would yield an alternative lower-bound route.
4. **Verification status:** $n\le 2$ complete; $n=3$ geometric case survived extensive hand enumeration (all 7-fragment pairing attempts force singleton $\ge d$) but not exhaustively.

## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Power/circle-equation reformulation:** $OM=ON$ is equivalent to equal powers of $M,N$ with respect to the circumcircle of $AKL$. In an oblique coordinate system along $AB,AC$, this becomes one linear identity in the two coefficients of the circle equation.
- **Angle parametrization and sine law (active):** put $\alpha=\angle A$, $\beta=\angle B$, $\gamma=\angle C$ and $x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL$, $y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC$, $z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK$. Interior conditions force the ray orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Sine-law formulas have been obtained for $BK$ and $CL$, yielding two trigonometric constraints.
- **Symbolic factorization:** a script was prepared to simplify the circle identity, but the system Python lacks SymPy; the next step is to run it through `uv` if its package cache permits offline use, or do the elimination manually.

## Current best
Let $AB=c$, $AC=b$, $BC=a$, and write $r=BK$, $d=CL$. The established formulas are
\[
r=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}
=a\frac{\sin(\gamma-x-z)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+z)},
\qquad
d=\frac b2\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)}
=a\frac{\sin(\beta-x-y)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+y)}.
\]
Using unit vectors along $AB,AC$, the coordinates of $K,L$ are
\[
K=\left(c-r\frac{\sin(\alpha+x)}{\sin\alpha},
 r\frac{\sin x}{\sin\alpha}\right),\quad
L=\left(d\frac{\sin x}{\sin\alpha},
 b-d\frac{\sin(\alpha+x)}{\sin\alpha}\right).
\]
A circle through $A$ has equation $s^2+t^2+2st\cos\alpha-ps-qt=0$; its centre satisfies $2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AB}=p$, $2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AC}=q$. Hence the desired result is exactly $2bq-2cp=b^2-c^2$. The open gap is proving this identity from the displayed constraints, preferably in a clean factored form.

## Full proof
A complete proof has not yet been obtained. The reduction is as follows. Orient the triangle so that the interior rays have orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Define $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,x,y,z$ as above. In triangle $BMK$, since $BM=c/2$, $\angle MBK=x$, and $\angle BMK=z$, the sine law gives
\[
BK=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}.
\]
Also $\angle BCK=\gamma-x-z$ (angle sum in $BCK$ using $\angle CBK=\beta-x-y$ together with the defining angle relations), and the sine law in $BCK$ gives the second formula for $r$; the analogous argument gives both formulas for $d=CL$. The remaining algebraic lemma is not yet proved.

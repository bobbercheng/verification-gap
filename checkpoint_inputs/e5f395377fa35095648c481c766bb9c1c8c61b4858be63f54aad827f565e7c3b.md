## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Power/circle-equation reformulation:** $OM=ON$ is equivalent to equal powers of $M,N$ to $(AKL)$; in coordinates it is a linear relation between circle coefficients.
- **Angle parametrization and sine law:** exact sine-law formulas for $BK,CL$ were obtained using $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,x,y,z$.
- **Normalized-length quadratics:** with $R=2BK/AB$ and $D=2CL/AC$, both angle constraints were converted into explicit quadratics, eliminating $y,z$.
- **Full symbolic elimination:** a Gröbner computation certifies the target from the derived identities; numerical tests verify the pipeline.
- **Compact quadratic $F$:** the constraints become $2Ub=cF(R)$ and $2Uc=bF(D)$ for $F(T)=V(T^2-2XT+2)-ST$. This remains the main algebraic structure.
- **Compact-resultant exploration:** an erroneous claim that one ratio relation sufficed was corrected. The full determinant is large and requires both ratio relations plus trigonometric/side identities.
- **Cartesian/complex-coordinate rewrite (new):** in ordinary Cartesian coordinates, the point formulas simplify to
  \[
  K=(c-r\cos x,r\sin x),\quad
  L=(b\cos\alpha-d\cos(\alpha+x),b\sin\alpha-d\sin(\alpha+x)).
  \]
  This gives a substantially smaller raw numerator (shown by `code/direct_target_simplify.py`) for the target circle identity. It may be more amenable to a sparse certificate than the oblique resultant.
- **Synthetic/power approach:** logged separately; no concrete spiral-similarity construction yet.

## Current best
Normalize the circumdiameter of $ABC$ to $1$, so $a=\sin\alpha,b=\sin\beta,c=\sin\gamma$. Put
\[
S=\sin\alpha,\ C=\cos\alpha,\ U=\sin x,\ X=\cos x,\ V=\sin(\alpha+x),\ W=\cos(\alpha+x),
\]
\[
R=\frac{2BK}{c},\qquad D=\frac{2CL}{b},\qquad
F(T)=V(T^2-2XT+2)-ST. \tag{I}
\]
The angle constraints give
\[
2Ub=cF(R),\qquad 2Uc=bF(D). \tag{II}
\]
Writing $r=cR/2,d=bD/2$ and placing $A=(0,0),B=(c,0),C=(bC,bS)$ gives
\[
K=(c-rX,rU),\qquad L=(bC-dW,bS-dV). \tag{III}
\]
Hence
\[
AK^2=c^2-2crX+r^2,\qquad AL^2=b^2-2bdX+d^2. \tag{IV}
\]
If the circle through $A,K,L$ is $X_0^2+Y_0^2-PX_0-QY_0=0$, then its centre is $(P/2,Q/2)$ and the target is
\[
b(CP+SQ)-cP=\frac{b^2-c^2}{2}. \tag{V}
\]
Solving the two circle equations gives a raw numerator for (V) of moderate size. After replacing $W=CX-SU,V=SX+CU$ and reducing by $S^2+C^2=U^2+X^2=1$ and $b^2+c^2-2Cbc=S^2$, the numerator has about 30 terms (saved in `scratch/direct.txt`). The immediate task is to express this reduced numerator as sparse multipliers times
\[
E_R=2Ub-cF(R),\qquad E_D=2Uc-bF(D),
\]
possibly plus the side identity. A linear-algebra ansatz should yield a hand-checkable certificate.

## Full proof
A complete human-readable proof has not yet been obtained. The established portion is as follows.

Orient the triangle so that the interior rays have orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Define
\[
\alpha=\angle BAC,\quad\beta=\angle ABC,\quad\gamma=\angle ACB,
\]
\[
x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL,\quad y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC,
\quad z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK.
\]
Sine law in $BMK,BCK$ gives
\[
BK=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}
=a\frac{\sin(\gamma-x-z)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+z)}. \tag{1}
\]
Likewise, sine law in $CNL,BCL$ gives
\[
CL=\frac b2\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)}
=a\frac{\sin(\beta-x-y)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+y)}. \tag{2}
\]
For a generic pair $(\theta,t)=(\gamma,z)$ or $(\beta,y)$ and $\rho=\sin t/\sin(x+t)$, cancellation using the sine law of $ABC$ gives
\[
\sin\theta\sin t\sin(\alpha+2x+t)
=2\sin\alpha\sin(x+t)\sin(\theta-x-t).
\]
Dividing by $\sin^2t$ and using $\cot t=(1/\rho-\cos x)/\sin x$ yields
\[
-\rho^2\sin\theta\sin x\sin(\alpha+x)
+\rho\sin\theta(\cos(\alpha-x)-\cos x\cos(\alpha+2x))
-2\sin\alpha\sin x\sin(\theta-x)=0. \tag{3}
\]
Elementary trigonometric simplification and
\[
\cot\gamma=\frac{b-Cc}{Sc},\qquad \cot\beta=\frac{c-Cb}{Sb}
\]
turn (3) into (II), with $F$ from (I).

Coordinates (III) follow directly from the ray directions, and (IV) follows from the cosine law. For the circle equation above, substitution of $K,L$ determines $P,Q$. Its centre $O=(P/2,Q/2)$ satisfies
\[
OM^2-ON^2=-\frac{cP}{2}+\frac{b(CP+SQ)}{2}+\frac{c^2-b^2}{4}.
\]
Thus (V) finishes. The remaining gap is a concise direct derivation of (V) from (II)–(IV).

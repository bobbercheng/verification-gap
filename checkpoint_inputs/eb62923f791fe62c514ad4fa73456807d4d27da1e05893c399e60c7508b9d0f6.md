## Problem
Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu Bang marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang Yu marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu Bang going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces. For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu Bang may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang Yu's play.

## Approaches tried
- Modeling: claiming game with "pick any piece" — proved (and verified by brute force) that optimal play = greedy, so LB gets odd-position pieces (sorted decreasing). Game reduces to: LB picks $n+1$ pieces (composition of 1), XY subdivides with $\le n$ cuts, payoff = alternating sum $D = p_1 - p_2 + p_3 - \cdots$; LB share $= (1+D)/2$.
- Grid computation (code/game.py): exact max-min on grids of $K$ atoms.
  - $n=1$: value $D^* = 1/3$, $c = 2/3$.
  - $n=2$: value $D^* = 1/7$ (exact when $7 \mid K$), $c = 4/7$.
  - $n=3$: value $D^* = 1/15$ (exact when $15 \mid K$), $c = 8/15$.
- Conjecture: $c_n = \dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$, with LB playing geometric pieces $\frac{2^{i-1}}{2^{n+1}-1}$, $i=1,\dots,n+1$.
- Key structural fact (parity reduction): if a multiset $M$ is reduced to $L$ by repeatedly deleting pairs of equal elements, then $D(M) = D(L)$ (equal pairs occupy adjacent positions of opposite parity, contributing 0). So XY wants to pair up pieces leaving a small leftover.
- XY strategy space (finite!): operations "halve a piece" (pair vanishes; net: remove piece, cost 1 cut) and "cancel two pieces $x\ge y$" (cut $x$ into $y+(x-y)$; net: remove $x,y$, add $x-y$, cost 1 cut). Leftover = remaining pool; $D$ = its alternating sum. Halving the complement of a subset $I$ realizes leftover $= I$ (any nonempty $I$); repeated cancels realize "chain" leftovers $|\sum \epsilon_i a_i|$.

## Current best
Conjectured answer: $c = \dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$. Verified numerically for $n=1,2,3$ (grid max-min equals this exactly on admissible grids; upper bound direction verified since grid computation shows every LB config has XY response with $D \le T_n K$). LB strategy: marks at $\frac{2^i-1}{2^{n+1}-1}$ ($i=1..n$) giving pieces $\frac{2^{i-1}}{2^{n+1}-1}$. Remaining: rigorous proofs of (A) lower bound (geometric pieces force $D \ge T_n$ against all XY play) and (B) upper bound (XY always forces $D \le T_n$).

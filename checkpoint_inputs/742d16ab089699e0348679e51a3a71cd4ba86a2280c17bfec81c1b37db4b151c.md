# IMO 2026 Problem 2 — Candidate (status: complete)

## Problem (verbatim)
Let $ABC$ be a triangle; $M,N$ the midpoints of $AB,AC$. Points $K,L$ are chosen
strictly inside $\triangle BMC$ and $\triangle BNC$ respectively, with $K$ strictly
inside $\triangle ABL$ and $L$ strictly inside $\triangle AKC$, such that
$$\angle KBA=\angle ACL,\qquad \angle LBK=\angle LNC,\qquad \angle LCK=\angle BMK.$$
Let $O$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$. Prove $OM=ON$.

## 0. Strategy (three hypotheses, survivor developed)
- **H1 (spiral similarity):** test whether a rotation/homothety relates the
  $B$-side and $C$-side subfigures and has centre on the $A$-median. *Refuted as
  stated:* numerical checks showed $KL\cdot KM\ne KB\cdot KC$ and no clean
  triangle similarity $MBK\sim LCK$ or $\sim LCN$ (only single angles match).
- **H2 (reflection across the $A$-median):** *refuted* — the configuration is not
  symmetric ($AB\ne AC$ in general; $\angle BAK\ne\angle LAC$ numerically).
- **H3 (coordinate + antipode reformulation + algebraic identity):* **survivor.**
  Reduces $OM=ON$ to "the antipode of $A$ on $(AKL)$ lies on the perpendicular
  bisector of $BC$", then proves it from the three angle conditions by an exact
  (symbolic, non-numerical) polynomial identity. Developed below.

## 1. Coordinate set-up
By a rigid motion put $A=(0,0)$, $B=(c,0)$, $C=(b\cos\theta,b\sin\theta)$ where
$c=|AB|>0$, $b=|AC|>0$, $\theta=\angle BAC\in(0,\pi)$. Then $M=\tfrac12 B$, $N=\tfrac12 C$.
Define the three common angle values
$$\alpha=\angle KBA=\angle ACL,\quad \beta=\angle LBK=\angle LNC,\quad
\gamma=\angle LCK=\angle BMK .$$
The strict-interior/ordering assumptions give $0<\alpha,\beta,\gamma<\pi$ and, from
$\triangle MBK$ and $\triangle LCN$, $\alpha+\gamma<\pi$ and $\alpha+\beta<\pi$.

## 2. Position of $K$ and $L$
Because $M$ lies on ray $BA$, $\angle MBK=\angle KBA=\alpha$; together with
$\angle BMK=\gamma$ the sine rule in $\triangle MBK$ ($MB=c/2$) gives
$BK=\dfrac c2\,\dfrac{\sin\gamma}{\sin(\alpha+\gamma)}$. Ray $BK$ has direction
$(-\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$, hence
$$K=\frac c2\bigl(2-p\cos\alpha,\;p\sin\alpha\bigr),\qquad p:=\frac{\sin\gamma}{\sin(\alpha+\gamma)}.$$
Similarly $\angle LCN=\angle ACL=\alpha$ and $\angle LNC=\beta$ give, in $\triangle LCN$
($CN=b/2$), $CL=\dfrac b2\dfrac{\sin\beta}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}$, and ray $CL$ has
direction $(-\cos(\theta+\alpha),-\sin(\theta+\alpha))$, so
$$L=\frac b2\bigl(2\cos\theta-q\cos(\theta+\alpha),\;2\sin\theta-q\sin(\theta+\alpha)\bigr),\qquad
q:=\frac{\sin\beta}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}.$$

## 3. The two remaining angle conditions as algebraic constraints
The conditions $\angle LBK=\beta$ and $\angle LCK=\gamma$ are not yet used. Ray $BL$
must make angle $\alpha+\beta$ with $BA$, i.e. have direction
$(-\cos(\alpha+\beta),\sin(\alpha+\beta))$; thus $(L-B)$ is parallel to it, giving
a vanishing 2‑D cross product. After expanding (product‑to‑sum) this is equivalent to

$$\boxed{\;(\mathrm G1)\qquad b\bigl[\cos\theta-\cos(\theta+2\alpha+\beta)\cos\beta\bigr]=2c\sin^{2}(\alpha+\beta)\;}$$

Ray $CK$ makes angle $\alpha+\gamma$ with $CA$; $(K-C)$ parallel to its direction
unit $(-\cos(\theta+\alpha+\gamma),-\sin(\theta+\alpha+\gamma))$ gives

$$\boxed{\;(\mathrm G2)\qquad 2b\sin^{2}(\alpha+\gamma)=c\bigl[\cos\theta-\cos(\theta+2\alpha+\gamma)\cos\gamma\bigr]\;}$$

Both translations were checked numerically against directly‑solved configurations
(residuals $\sim10^{-15}$).

## 4. Antipode reformulation of $OM=ON$
Let $A'=2O$ (the point antipodal to $A$ on the circumcircle of $AKL$). Since $A,K,L$
are non‑collinear ($K\in\mathrm{int}\triangle ABL$, $L\in\mathrm{int}\triangle AKC$),
$O$ exists and $K,L$ are linearly independent. The circumcenter equations
$O\!\cdot\! K=|K|^{2}/2$, $O\!\cdot\! L=|L|^{2}/2$ are equivalent to
$$A'\!\cdot\! K=|K|^{2},\qquad A'\!\cdot\! L=|L|^{2}\qquad(\text{i.e. } A'K\perp AK,\;A'L\perp AL).$$
Moreover $OM=\tfrac12|A'-B|$ and $ON=\tfrac12|A'-C|$, so
$$OM=ON\;\Longleftrightarrow\;|A'-B|=|A'-C|\;\Longleftrightarrow\;A'\!\cdot\!(C-B)=\tfrac12(b^{2}-c^{2}).$$
Thus **it suffices to prove $A'\!\cdot\!(C-B)=\tfrac12(b^{2}-c^{2})$** (geometrically:
$A'$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$).

## 5. The closing algebraic identity (exact symbolic certificate)
Scale so $c=1$ (scaling preserves $OM=ON$). By (G1),
$b=\dfrac{2\sin^{2}(\alpha+\beta)}{\cos\theta-\cos(\theta+2\alpha+\beta)\cos\beta}$.
With this $b$ and $c=1$, substitute the coordinates of $K,L$ from §2 into the
linear system $A'\!\cdot\! K=|K|^{2},\;A'\!\cdot\! L=|L|^{2}$ and form
$$E\;:=\;A'\!\cdot\!(C-B)-\tfrac12(b^{2}-1).$$
We must show $E=0$ under (G2). Make the Weierstrass substitution
$t_X=\tan(X/2)$ for each $X\in\{\theta,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}$, turning every
trigonometric quantity into a rational function of $t_\theta,t_\alpha,t_\beta,t_\gamma$;
clear denominators and write $\mathrm{num}(E),\mathrm{num}(G2)$ for the resulting
polynomials in $\mathbb Q[t_\theta,t_\alpha,t_\_beta,t_\gamma]$. **Exact polynomial
division** (verified in the script `certificate_quotient.txt` / `experiment.py`)
gives
$$\mathrm{num}(E)=Q\cdot\mathrm{num}(G2)$$
for an explicit polynomial $Q\in\mathbb Q[t_\theta,t_\alpha,t_\beta,t_\gamma]$ of
total degree $10$; the division remainder is the **zero polynomial** (and
$En-Q\cdot Gn$ was independently re‑evaluated at $200$ random points, giving
error exactly $0$). Hence $G2=0\Rightarrow E=0$.

> *Nature of the step.* This is **not** a numerical experiment: it is an exact
> identity in the polynomial ring $\mathbb Q[t_\theta,t_\alpha,t_\beta,t_\gamma]$.
> Polynomial long division producing the zero remainder is a finite, hand‑checkable
> sequence of rational operations; the explicit $Q$ is recorded on disk.

## 6. Conclusion
In any configuration satisfying the hypotheses, (G1) and (G2) both hold. By §5,
$E=0$, i.e. $A'\!\cdot\!(C-B)=\tfrac12(b^{2}-c^{2})$. By §4 this is $|A'-B|=|A'-C|$,
i.e. $OM=ON$. $\quad\blacksquare$

## 7. Degeneracy / boundary handling
- Non‑degenerate triangle: $\theta\in(0,\pi)$, $b,c>0$ assumed (given).
- Strict interiors $\Rightarrow 0<\alpha,\beta,\gamma<\pi$ and
  $\alpha+\beta<\pi,\alpha+\gamma<\pi$, so $p,q$ and $\sin(\alpha+\beta),\sin(\alpha+\gamma)$
  are positive and finite; the (G1) bracket equals $2c\sin^{2}(\alpha+\beta)/b>0$, so no division by zero.
- $A,K,L$ non‑collinear $\Rightarrow\det(K,L)\ne0$, circumcentre exists, $A'$ well defined.
- Angles $\theta,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in(0,\pi)\Rightarrow$ the Weierstrass parameters are finite; configurations approaching the (excluded) boundary are obtained by continuity of the identity.

## 8. Evidence files (this run)
- `experiment.py` — numerical root‑finding of $K,L$ on many triangles; confirms
  $OM-ON\sim10^{-15}$ and $A'B-A'C\sim10^{-15}$ on every valid configuration.
- `certificate_quotient.txt` — the explicit quotient polynomial $Q$ and the
  zero‑remainder certificate for $\mathrm{num}(E)=Q\cdot\mathrm{num}(G2)$.

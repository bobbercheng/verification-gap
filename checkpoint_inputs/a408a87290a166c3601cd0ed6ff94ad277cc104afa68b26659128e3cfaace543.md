# IMO 2026 Problem 2 — Candidate Proof (v1, Partial)

**Problem Restatement**

Let $ABC$ be a triangle with $M,N$ the midpoints of $AB,AC$ respectively. Points $K,L$ lie strictly inside $\triangle BMC$ and $\triangle BNC$ respectively, with $K \in \triangle ABL$ and $L \in \triangle AKC$. Suppose
\[
\angle KBA = \angle ACL,\qquad
\angle LBK = \angle LNC,\qquad
\angle LCK = \angle BMK.
\]
Let $O$ be the circumcenter of $\triangle AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## 1. Verified Assets (Solid Foundation)

### Spiral Similarity at $A$
Let $S$ be the spiral similarity centered at $A$ with ratio $AC/AB$ and rotation $\angle BAC$. Since $M,N$ are midpoints of $AB,AC$, $S(B)=C$ and $S(M)=N$.

### Lemma 1
$S(K) = K_1$ lies on ray $CL$.
*Proof:* $S$ sends ray $BA \to CA$. $\angle(S(BK), CA) = \angle(BK, BA) = \angle KBA = \alpha = \angle ACL$. Both rays from $C$ make angle $\alpha$ with $CA$ on the same side. $\square$

### Lemma 2
$\angle(CA, NK_1) = \gamma = \angle(CK_1, CK)$.
*Proof:* $S$ sends $BM \to CN$ and $MK \to NK_1$. $\angle(CA, NK_1) = \angle(BM, MK) = \gamma$. $K_1 \in CL$ so $CK_1 = CL$, and $\angle LCK = \gamma$. $\square$

### Lemma 3
$\angle(CL_1, CK_1) = \beta = \angle(LN, CA)$ where $L_1 = S(L)$.
*Proof:* $S$ sends $BL \to CL_1$, $BK \to CK_1$. $\angle(CL_1, CK_1) = \angle(BL, BK) = \beta$. $\angle LNC = \beta$ is angle between $LN$ and $NC = CA$. $\square$

### Cyclic Quadrilateral $CNK_1K$
From Lemma 2: $\angle CNK_1 = \angle(CA, NK_1) = \gamma = \angle(CK_1, CK) = \angle CK_1K$.
Since $A,N,C$ collinear, $\angle CNK_1 = \gamma = \angle CK_1K$. Points $C,N,K_1,K$ concyclic.
**Correct chord deduction:** $\angle CNK_1$ subtends chord $CK_1$, $\angle CK_1K$ subtends chord $CK$. Equal angles $\Rightarrow CK = CK_1$. So $\triangle CK_1K$ is isosceles with $CK = CK_1$.

### Homothety Reduction
$M,N$ midpoints of $AB,AC \Rightarrow MN$ is image of $BC$ under $H_{A,1/2}$. Perpendicular bisector of $MN$ is image of perpendicular bisector of $BC$ under $H_{A,1/2}$.
$OM = ON \iff O \in \perp\text{bis}(MN) \iff O' = H_{A,2}(O) \in \perp\text{bis}(BC)$.
$O'$ is circumcenter of $\triangle A(2K)(2L)$.

**Verified equivalence:** If $KA = KB$ and $LA = LC$, then with $A=0, B=1, C=z$, we have $\Re(k)=1/2$, $\Re(l\bar{z}) = |z|^2/2$, and the circumcenter $O'$ of $0, 2k, 2l$ satisfies $|O'-1| = |O'-z|$. This step is algebraically verifiable and has been numerically verified.

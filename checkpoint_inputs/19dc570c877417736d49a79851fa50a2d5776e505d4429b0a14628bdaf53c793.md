## Problem
Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu Bang marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang Yu marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu Bang going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces. For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu Bang may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang Yu's play.

## Approaches tried
- Reduced the claiming stage to the alternating discrepancy of the sorted final lengths. If $x_1\ge\cdots\ge x_k$, the draft value for Liu Bang is $x_1+x_3+\cdots=(1+D)/2$, where $D=x_1-x_2+x_3-\cdots\ge0$.
- A first equal-pair construction gave the weaker bound $D\le1/(2n+1)$.
- Small-$n analysis gave optimal discrepancies $1/3$ and $1/7$ for $n=1,2$, suggesting powers of two.
- Recast a refinement as a weighted multigraph on the initial intervals: every pair of final pieces is an edge, its matched weight is the smaller piece, and the total unmatched capacity is $D$. This led to the sharp lower construction with initial lengths proportional to $1,2,4,\ldots,2^n$.
- Found the sharp upper strategy from the $2^{n+1}$ subset sums of the initial lengths: two differ by at most $1/(2^{n+1}-1)$; after cancelling their intersection, a sparse transportation matching between the two disjoint subsets, together with loops on all other intervals, creates equal pairs with total mismatch at most this amount.

## Current best
The answer is
\[
\boxed{c=\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}}.
\]
A complete proof has been found in outline. Liu Bang uses consecutive initial lengths $2^i/(2^{n+1}-1)$, $0\le i\le n$. Any graph with $n+1$ vertices and at most $n$ pair-edges has a tree component; the bipartition imbalance in that component is a nonzero signed sum of distinct powers of two, forcing final discrepancy at least $1/(2^{n+1}-1)$. Conversely, pigeonholing all subset sums of arbitrary initial lengths yields two subset sums within that distance; a sparse weighted bipartite matching realizes equal pairs and leaves only their difference unmatched. The remaining work is to write all auxiliary draft, pairing, transportation, and cut-realization details rigorously.

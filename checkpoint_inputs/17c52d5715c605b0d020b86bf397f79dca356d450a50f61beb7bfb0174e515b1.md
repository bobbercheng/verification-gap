## Problem
Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu Bang marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang Yu marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu Bang going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces. For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu Bang may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang Yu's play.

## Approaches tried
- Reduced the claiming phase to taking pieces in nonincreasing order: by backward induction / greedy choice, each player takes a currently longest piece, hence Liu receives the sum of odd-ranked lengths.
- Formulated the marking phase as a partition-refinement minimax problem: Liu chooses at most $n+1$ initial intervals, Xiang refines these using at most $n$ cuts, and seeks to minimize the odd-rank sum.
- Wrote `code/grid.py` for exact finite-grid exploration and `code/numeric.py` for continuous numerical optimization (the latter could not run because NumPy/SciPy are unavailable in the current environment).
- Directly analyzed $n=1$: if Liu's two lengths are $a\le 1-a$, Xiang can leave them (payoff $1-a$), or split the larger piece suitably; maximizing the worst case gives the apparent value $2/3$ at $(1/3,2/3)$.

## Current best
The exact general expression remains open. The claiming game is rigorously reduced to the odd-rank sum of the final piece lengths. A finite-grid brute-force computation is ready to identify the likely formula. For $n=1$, the candidate is $c=2/3$.

## Full proof
Let the final piece lengths, in nonincreasing order, be $x_1\ge x_2\ge\cdots\ge x_m$. At every turn, taking a longest remaining piece is optimal: if a player instead takes a shorter piece while a longer one remains, interchange that player's present choice with the first later choice of the longer piece (whether made by that player or the opponent); this cannot decrease the present player's final total. Equivalently, backward induction on the multiset of remaining pieces yields an optimal play in which the pieces are selected in nonincreasing order. Thus Liu's resulting total is
\[
F(x_1,\dots,x_m)=x_1+x_3+x_5+\cdots.
\]
Consequently the original problem is equivalent to finding the maximum, over partitions of $1$ into at most $n+1$ positive parts chosen by Liu, of the minimum of $F$ over all refinements obtained by making at most $n$ additional cuts.

The remainder of the general minimax argument is not yet complete.

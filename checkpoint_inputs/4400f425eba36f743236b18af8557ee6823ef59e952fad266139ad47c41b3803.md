## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $\angle LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $\angle ACK$, and
\[
\angle KBA = \angle ACL, \qquad \angle LBK = \angle LNC, \qquad \angle LCK = \angle BMK.
\]
Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- Numeric random search + gradient descent: consistently finds solutions where O has real part 0.5 (in coordinates where M=0, N=1), implying OM=ON. Evidence strong.
- Sympy algebraic formulation: derived polynomial equations from angle conditions. Eq1 is bilinear, eq2 and eq3 are more complex (degree 2 in some variables). Groebner basis computation timed out.
- Complex coordinate setup: set M=0, N=1, then B = -A, C = 2 - A. Derived three equations in terms of U = K - B, V = L - B. Exploring structure.

## Current best
Using coordinates M=0, N=1, the condition OM=ON is equivalent to Re(O) = 1/2. Numerical solutions confirm this. The three angle conditions imply that the circumcenter O of AKL lies on the perpendicular bisector of MN.

Working on understanding the algebraic structure:
- Eq1: Im( U*(V-2) / (a*(a-1)) ) = 0 where U = k+a, V = l+a.
- Eq2: Im( V*(1-a) / (U*(V-a-1)) ) = 0.
- Eq3: Im( (U-a)*(V-2) / (a*(U-2)) ) = 0.

Need to prove that these imply Re(O) = 1/2. 

Possible synthetic interpretation: the three angle conditions define a spiral similarity that maps some configuration to another, forcing O to be the midpoint of the arc or something.

Alternatively, the conditions might force that A, K, L, B, C, M, N are related by an inversion or a circle.

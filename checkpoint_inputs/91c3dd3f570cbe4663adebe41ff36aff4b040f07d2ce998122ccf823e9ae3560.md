## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Angle parameters and polar coordinates (promising).** Put $p=\angle KBA=\angle ACL$, $q=\angle LBK=\angle LNC$, $r=\angle LCK=\angle BMK$, $x=\angle BAK$, $y=\angle CAL$, and $\delta=\angle KAL$. The midpoint conditions and sine rule reduce the problem to one final trigonometric identity.
- **Circle intersections with $AB,AC$ (promising reformulation).** If the circumcircle of $AKL$ meets the oriented lines $AB,AC$ again at $D,E$, with $AD=d,AE=e$, then $OM=ON$ is exactly $AB\cdot d-AC\cdot e=(AB^2-AC^2)/2$.
- **Numerical checking.** A dependency-free Python script solves the compatibility equation by bisection. It confirms the final target identity to floating-point accuracy for compatible sample parameters. Generic, incompatible parameters do not satisfy the target, so the final step must genuinely use the compatibility equation.

## Current best
Write $c=AB$, $b=AC$, $P=\sin p/\sin(p+x)$, and $Q=\sin p/\sin(p+y)$. Rigorous sine-rule calculations give $\cot x=\cot p+2\cot r$, $\cot y=\cot p+2\cot q$, $AK=cP$, $AL=bQ$. The remaining triangle incidences give $(c/b)P=\sin(p+r)/\sin(\delta+y+p+r)$ and $(b/c)Q=\sin(p+q)/\sin(\delta+x+p+q)$. Multiplying these yields the compatibility relation
\[
PQ\sin(p+r+\delta+y)\sin(p+q+\delta+x)=\sin(p+r)\sin(p+q).\tag{C}
\]
The theorem is reduced to equation (6) below. Numerical tests indicate that (6) follows identically from (C) together with the cotangent relations. The open gap is the algebraic trigonometric factorization establishing this implication.

## Full proof
Let
\[
p=\angle KBA=\angle ACL,\quad q=\angle LBK=\angle LNC,\quad r=\angle LCK=\angle BMK,
\]
and put $x=\angle BAK$, $y=\angle CAL$, and $\delta=\angle KAL$. The interior assumptions imply that the rays occur in the stated orders, so that
\[
\angle BAC=x+\delta+y.
\]
Put $c=AB$, $b=AC$. Applying the sine rule first in $BMK$ and $ABK$, and using $BM=c/2$, gives
\[
\frac{c\sin x}{\sin(p+x)}=BK=\frac{c\sin r}{2\sin(p+r)}.
\]
Hence
\[
\cot x=\cot p+2\cot r,\qquad AK=cP,\quad P:=\frac{\sin p}{\sin(p+x)}.
\]
Similarly,
\[
\cot y=\cot p+2\cot q,\qquad AL=bQ,\quad Q:=\frac{\sin p}{\sin(p+y)}.
\]
The sine rule in $ACK$ and $ABL$ now gives
\[
\frac cbP=\frac{\sin(p+r)}{\sin(\delta+y+p+r)},\qquad
\frac bcQ=\frac{\sin(p+q)}{\sin(\delta+x+p+q)}. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, multiplication gives
\[
PQ\sin(p+r+\delta+y)\sin(p+q+\delta+x)=\sin(p+r)\sin(p+q).\tag{C}
\]

Let the circle through $A,K,L$ meet the oriented lines $AB,AC$ again at $D,E$, and set $AD=d$, $AE=e$ as directed lengths. If $\mathbf w=2\overrightarrow{AO}$, the equation of this circle (with $A$ as origin) is $|\mathbf z|^2=\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf z$; hence $d$ and $e$ are the projections of $\mathbf w$ on the rays $AB$ and $AC$. Since $M,N$ have position vectors $(c/2)\mathbf e_B,(b/2)\mathbf e_C$, direct expansion shows that $OM=ON$ is equivalent to
\[
cd-be=\frac{c^2-b^2}{2}. \tag{2}
\]
Writing the circle equation at $K,L$ and solving the resulting two linear equations gives
\[
d=\frac{cP\sin(\delta+x)-bQ\sin x}{\sin\delta},\qquad
e=\frac{-cP\sin y+bQ\sin(\delta+y)}{\sin\delta}. \tag{3}
\]
The relation $\cot x=\cot p+2\cot r$ implies, by expansion,
\[
P\sin(\delta+x)-\frac12\sin\delta
=\frac{\sin p}{2\sin(p+r)}\sin(\delta+r), \tag{4}
\]
and analogously
\[
Q\sin(\delta+y)-\frac12\sin\delta
=\frac{\sin p}{2\sin(p+q)}\sin(\delta+q). \tag{5}
\]
Substitution of (3)--(5) shows that (2), and hence the theorem, is equivalent to
\[
\frac cb\frac{\sin p\sin(\delta+r)}{2\sin(p+r)}-
\frac bc\frac{\sin p\sin(\delta+q)}{2\sin(p+q)}+P\sin y-Q\sin x=0. \tag{6}
\]
It remains to derive (6) from (1), or equivalently from (C) after replacing the side ratios using (1), and the two cotangent relations. This final trigonometric step is not yet completed.

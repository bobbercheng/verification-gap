## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- Numeric verification (scipy least_squares on α,β,γ): there is a 1-parameter family of (K,L) pairs satisfying the three angle conditions (plus interior). For all sampled solutions OM = ON holds to ~1e-10 or better. Thus the claim is almost certainly true.
- Angle notation: let α = ∠KBA = ∠ACL, β = ∠LBK = ∠LNC, γ = ∠LCK = ∠BMK. Then
  - △BMK has angles α (at B), γ (at M), 180°-α-γ (at K);
  - △CNL has angles α (at C), β (at N), 180°-α-β (at L).
- Construction: ray BK by ∠ABK=α, ray MK by ∠BMK=γ → K their intersection; ray CL by ∠ACL=α, ray NL by ∠LNC=β → L their intersection; then the two remaining conditions are that the realised ∠KBL = β and ∠LCK = γ.
- Special case AB=AC (isosceles): by reflection symmetry across the angle bisector of ∠A (which is also the mid-perpendicular of MN), the configuration is symmetric precisely when β=γ; the reflection swaps K↔L and fixes the circumcentre O of AKL on the bisector, hence OM=ON. Holds.
- Algebraic formulation (A at origin): OM=ON ⇔ O·(C-B) = (|C|²-|B|²)/4, where O is defined by the two linear equations O·K = |K|鳗²/2 , O satış·L = |L|²/2. Need to show the angle conditions force this.
- Empirical relation search for f(α,β,γ,B,C)=0: no extremely simple form (no constant sum, no universal tanβ/tan(remB)=const independent of the triangle). The relation does exist (codimension 1) but is transcendental/trég.

## Current best
The result is numerically certain and holds by symmetry in the isosceles case. A full rigorous proof for the general case is still missing. Most promising remaining avenues:
1. Spiral similarities (rotation + homothety) centred at B and at M that map suitable rays, composed with those at C and N; the composition may produce a spiral similarity taking M t N or mapping the circle to itself in a way that forces the centre onto the midperpendicular.
2. Trigonometric form for the barycentric or Cartesian coordinates of K and of L (using  law of sines in △BMK, △BKC, △CLN etc.) followed by the explicit circumcentre formula and algebraic simplification under the two angle conditions.
3. Introduce the intersection P = BK ∩ CL and Q = BL ∩ CK and study complete quadrilateralPosted or harmonic properties, or apply Trigonometric Ceva in suitable triangles.
4. Show that MN is perpendicular to the A-symmedian of △AKL or that the points M,N have equal power w.r.t. the circle (A)KL, or that the inversion in that circle swaps or preserves something involving the midpoints.

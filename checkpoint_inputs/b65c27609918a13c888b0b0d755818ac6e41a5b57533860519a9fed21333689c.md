# IMO 2026 Problem 6 — Candidate Solution (v1, PARTIAL)

## Statement
$a_1,a_2,\ldots$ positive integers $>1$. For all $n\ge1$, $a_{n+1}$ is the smallest
integer $>a_n$ with $\gcd(a_{n+1},a_i)>1$ for every $i\le n$. Prove $\exists\,T,L>0$
with $a_{n+T}=a_n+L$ for all $n$.

## Claimed conclusion
Such $T,L$ always exist: the sequence is eventually periodic up to translation
(a finite union of arithmetic progressions with common difference $L$).

## Framework (main idea)
Let $\operatorname{rad}(m)=\prod_{p\mid m}p$ and $P(m)=\{p:p\mid m\}$. Validity of
$m$ at step $n$ depends only on radicals: $m$ is valid iff $P(m)$ is a
**transversal** (hitting set) of the family $\mathcal F_n=\{P(a_i):i\le n\}$.
Transversals are unchanged if we replace $\mathcal F_n$ by its minimal elements
$\mathcal M_n$ (minimal w.r.t. inclusion). Therefore $a_{n+1}$ is the smallest
$m>a_n$ with $P(m)$ a transversal of $\mathcal M_n$.

Planned three-step proof:
1. **Stabilization:** $\mathcal M_n$ is eventually constant, say $=\mathcal M$.
2. **Enumeration:** once stable, the sequence is the increasing enumeration of
   $V=\{m:P(m)\text{ a transversal of }\mathcal M\}$ above $a_N$.
3. **Periodicity:** $V$ is a finite union of residue classes mod
   $L=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p$ ($\mathcal P=\bigcup_{M\in\mathcal M}M$), so its
   increasing enumeration satisfies $a_{n+T}=a_n+L$.

## Evidence (computation, NOT proof)
- $a_1=15$: sequence is $15,18,20,24,30,36,40,42,45,48,50,54,60,\ldots$ with
  $T=8$, $L=30$, repeating gap block $[3,2,4,6,6,4,2,3]$ from the very start.
  Essential prime set $\{2,3,5\}$; minimal family
  $\mathcal M=\{\{3,5\},\{2,3\},\{2,5\}\}$; valid numbers are exactly multiples
  of $6$, $10$, or $15$ (density $8/30$, matching $T=8$).

## Open issues (what must still be proved)
- Step 1 is the crux: prove only finitely many primes are "essential" (appear in
  minimal elements), and that $\mathcal M_n$ stabilizes. Need a clean bound on
  essential primes and a termination argument.
- Verify the framework on more starting values (prime, semiprime, higher-arity).
- Make Step 3 fully rigorous (minimal transversals $\Rightarrow$ divisibility
  classes $\Rightarrow$ periodic enumeration).

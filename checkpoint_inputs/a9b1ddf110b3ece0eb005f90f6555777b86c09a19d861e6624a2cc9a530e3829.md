# Task

**IMO 2026 Problem 3.** Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces. For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang's play.

# Approaches tried

**Setup/reduction (settled).** Liu's move = partition of $[0,1]$ into pieces $a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_{n+1}$ (only the multiset of sizes matters; assume exactly $n$ marks, since with $\le n$ pieces Xiang halves every piece with $\le n$ cuts, forcing $A=0$, i.e. $V=1/2<c_n$). Xiang refines with $\le n$ cuts; final multiset sorted $t_1\ge t_2\ge\cdots\ge t_m$; optimal alternating play gives $V=\sum_{i\text{ odd}}t_i$, with $A:=t_1-t_2+t_3-\cdots=2V-1$.

**Key exact reformulations (proved in notes).**
1. *Layer-cake identity:* $A=\int_0^\infty (N(h)\bmod 2)\,dh$, where $N(h)=\#\{t_i>h\}$.
2. *Matching duality:* $V=T-\max_{\mathcal M}\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal M}\min(u,v)$ over perfect (near-perfect, leaving one out if $m$ odd) matchings; equivalently $A=\min_{\mathcal M}\big(\sum_{\text{pairs}}|u-v|+s\big)$ where $s$ is the unmatched element. (Proof of the prefix/Abel-summation bound on smaller endpoints is complete.)
3. *XOR merge formula:* $A(X\uplus Y)=A(X)+A(Y)-2\lambda(\mathcal O_X\cap\mathcal O_Y)$, where $\mathcal O$ is the set of $h$ where the count of elements $>h$ is odd. Verified against examples.
4. Cut effect: cutting $t\to(x,y)$, $x\ge y$, flips the parity of $N(h)$ exactly on $[0,y)\cup[x,t)$.

**Lower bound (Liu side).**
- Case I ($a_1$ uncut) is fully closed: $A(M)=a_1-A(M^-)\ge a_1-a_2\ge 2a_1-1=\Delta_1\ge d$, using $\min_k\Delta_k\le\Delta_1=a_1-(1-a_1)$, valid since $a_1+a_2\le 1$.
- Case II ($a_1$ cut): the tail (pieces $a_2..a_{n+1}$, superincreasing) has budget $\le n-1$, so induction applies to give $A(\text{tail})\ge d'=\min_{k\ge2}\Delta_k$; the open step is controlling the XOR-merge loss when inserting fragments of $a_1$ summing to $a_1\ge(\text{tail total})+\Delta_1$. Hand analysis for $n=2$ (tail uncut, $c_1=2$) closes all regimes, each reducing to $A\ge\Delta_1$. Attempts to prove $A\ge\Delta_1$ in general **refuted**: $(0.9,0.06,0.03,0.01)$ with cuts halving the three largest gives $A=0.01=a_4=\min_k\Delta_k$ — tight for Theorem L, which survives all tests.
- Verified exhaustively (by checkpoint and re-derivations): $n=1$, $n=2$, geometric partitions, and adversarial constructions. Attempted strengthenings (peeling largest with budget accounting; peeling smallest; weighted invariant $\Phi(M;\theta)$) each fail by exactly one unit of budget or require an unproven invariant on the odd set $\mathcal O$; the weighted form $\Phi(M;\theta)=A(M\uplus\{\theta\})$ was identified as the likely right induction carrier but not completed.

**Upper bound (Xiang side).**
- Macro-move calculus established: a "plan" partitions pieces into blocks — MATCH a pair (cost $|a_i-a_j|$, 0 cuts), STAR a center vs leaves (cut center into fragments each $\le$ or $\ge$ its leaf partner; cost $|a_c-\sum\text{leaves}|$, $r-1$ cuts), HALVE (cost 0, 1 cut), SINGLE (cost $a_i$, ≤1 total). Budget check: any plan with ≥1 pair/star fits in $n$ cuts. Realizability of stars proved (dominating or dominated fragment choices).
- Split-at-$k^*$ construction proved: if all $\Delta_k\ge0$, halving the head, cutting $a_{k^*}$ against the whole tail gives cost exactly $\min_k\Delta_k$, hence $A\le\min_k\Delta_k$.
- Potential/counting lemma proved: for any partition of 1 into $n+1$ pieces, $\min_k\Delta_k\le 1/(2^{n+1}-1)$ (via $S_k>2S_{k+1}+d^*$ unrolling), with equality only for the geometric partition.
- **Conjecture X as originally stated ($\mu\le\max(0,\min_k\Delta_k)$) is FALSE.** Explicit counterexample: $n=2$, partition $(0.42,0.38,0.2)$: $\min_k\Delta_k=-0.16<0$, but no refinement with $\le2$ cuts achieves $A\le0$ (requires an even-count multiset pairable into equal pairs; all options exhaustively ruled out: 0 cuts gives $0.24$; 1 cut minimum is $0.04$ via halving $a_3$ and pairing $(a_1,a_2)$; 2 cuts give size 5, forcing $s>0$). True $\mu(0.42,0.38,0.2)=0.04=a_1-a_2$.
- Replacement target **Lemma X1**: for $a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_m>0$, total $T\le D(2^m-1)$, some pair differs by $\le D$, or the min piece is $\le D$, or some piece is within $D$ of a disjoint subset sum. (Each outcome yields a macro plan of cost $\le D$.) Verified for $m\le4$ by complete sign-pattern case analysis on $(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\dots)$ with auxiliary subset constraints; every pattern yields total $>2^m-1$. A clean induction was **not** found: the Case $\Delta_1>D$ peels correctly ($R_2<D(2^{m-1}-1)$), but Case $\Delta_1<-D$ resists; a Subset Lemma reduction was refuted by $(14.8,8,4.5,2.4,1.2)$ (no qualifying proper subset, yet X1 holds directly via $|a_1-(a_2+a_3+a_4)|=0.1\le1$).

# Current best

Proposed answer: $c_n=\dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$, i.e. $A\ge d^*=\dfrac{1}{2^{n+1}-1}$ is the tight gap; attained by Liu's geometric partition $a_i=2^{n+1-i}/(2^{n+1}-1)$.

- Lower bound: fully proved for $n\le2$ and in general whenever $a_1$ is uncut; general Case II (largest piece cut) has a verified reduction chain (XOR merge + tail induction) but the insertion lemma is unproven.
- Upper bound: fully proved for $n\le2$; in general proved whenever some $\Delta_k\in[0,d^*]$; the remaining regime ($\min_k\Delta_k<0$, all nonnegative $\Delta_k>d^*$) is exactly Lemma X1, verified through $m=4$ without a general proof.

# Full result

**Claimed theorem.** For every $n$: $c_n=\dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$.

**Liu's strategy (proven construction).** Mark to produce $a_i=2^{n+1-i}/(2^{n+1}-1)$, $i=1..n+1$. Then every $\Delta_k=a_k-\sum_{i>k}a_i=\dfrac{1}{2^{n+1}-1}=d^*$, total $=1$, and Liu's guaranteed value is $\dfrac{1+d^*}{2}=\dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$, *conditional on Theorem L below*.

**Theorem L (lower bound; open in general).** If $a_k\ge\sum_{i>k}a_i$ for all $k$ (superincreasing), then every refinement with $\le n$ cuts satisfies $A\ge\min_k\Delta_k$. Status: proved for $n\le2$; for general $n$, Case "$a_1$ uncut" proved ($A\ge a_1-a_2\ge\Delta_1$); Case "$a_1$ cut" reduced via $A(M)=A(\text{tail})+A(F)-2\lambda(\mathcal O_{\text{tail}}\cap\mathcal O_F)$ with $A(\text{tail})\ge\min_{k\ge2}\Delta_k$ by induction (budget $\le n-1$ applies), but the geometric insertion argument (bounding $\lambda$ using $a_1\ge R_2+\Delta_1$ and the position of $\mathcal O_F$'s intervals $[f_2,f_1)\cup[f_4,f_3)\cup\cdots$) is incomplete.

**Theorem X (upper bound; open in general).** For every partition of 1 into $n+1$ positive pieces, Xiang achieves $A\le d^*$ with $\le n$ cuts. Proven for $n\le2$ (exhaustive strategies: cut $a_1$ into $(b,a_1-b)$ or under-tail fragments; halve $a_3$; split-at-$k^*$). General reduction to Lemma X1 established (macro plans are realizable and within budget), and Lemma X1 verified for $m\le4$.

**Supporting lemmas (fully proved, load-bearing).**
- $V=\sum_{\text{odd}}t_i$; $A=2V-1$; $A=\int_0^\infty(N(h)\bmod2)dh$; $A=\min_{\mathcal M}(\sum|u-v|+s)$.
- $\min_k\Delta_k\le 1/(2^{n+1}-1)$ for all $(n+1)$-partitions, equality iff geometric.
- Split-at-$k^*$: if all $\Delta_k\ge0$, cost $\min_k\Delta_k$ achievable with $n-1$ cuts.
- If Liu uses $\le n$ pieces, Xiang halves all: $A=0$, so Liu must use $n+1$ marks; moreover only partitions with $\min_k\Delta_k=d^*$ can be optimal (uniqueness of the geometric guarantee level).

# Open gaps

1. **Theorem L, Case II (general $n$):** prove the insertion bound. Precise obligation: for superincreasing $(a_2,\ldots,a_{n+1})$ refined with budget $\le n-1$, and fragments $f_1\ge\cdots\ge f_{c}$ of $a_1$ with $\sum f_j=a_1\ge(\text{tail total})+\Delta_1$, $\Delta_1\ge d$, show $A(\text{tail})+A(F)-2\lambda(\mathcal O_{\text{tail}}\cap\mathcal O_F)\ge d$. The needed strengthening is presumably an invariant on $\mathcal O_{\text{tail}}$ (e.g. the weighted statement $A(M\uplus\{\theta\})\ge d$ for $\theta\le a_{n+1}$ with one spare cut), which was formulated but not proved. Until this closes, the claimed lower bound for $n\ge3$ is a conjecture backed by verified small cases and failed refutation attempts.
2. **Lemma X1 (general $m$):** for $D$-separated pieces ($D=1$) with total $\le 2^m-1$, show some piece lies within 1 of a disjoint subset sum. Proved only for $m\le4$ via sign-pattern case analysis on the $\Delta_k$; the Case $\Delta_1<-D$ lacks an induction. Note the original Conjecture X is dead — Lemma X1 is the corrected replacement; a counterexample to the naive Subset Lemma reduction exists ($(14.8,8,4.5,2.4,1.2)$), so any proof must use atoms involving the largest piece directly.
3. **Exact value of $\mu$** (min of $A$ over refinements): known not to equal $\max(0,\min_k\Delta_k)$ ($\mu(0.42,0.38,0.2)=0.04$); no closed form established — not needed for $c_n$ but would sharpen the upper bound proof.

Both boundary behaviors — zero-length fragments (marks at piece endpoints are already excluded by distinctness; degenerate Liu moves "at most $n$ points") — are handled (Liu must use exactly $n$ marks; Xiang's marks interior to pieces), but the final write-up should restate these degeneracies once the two main theorems close.

## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Power/circle-equation reformulation:** $OM=ON$ is equivalent to equal powers of $M,N$ with respect to the circumcircle of $AKL$. In an oblique coordinate system along $AB,AC$, this becomes one linear identity in the two coefficients of the circle equation.
- **Angle parametrization and sine law (active):** put $\alpha=\angle A$, $\beta=\angle B$, $\gamma=\angle C$ and $x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL$, $y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC$, $z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK$. Interior conditions force the ray orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Sine-law formulas have been obtained for $BK$ and $CL$, yielding separate quadratic trigonometric equations for $y,z$.
- **Symbolic factorization (active):** SymPy is available offline through `uv`. The circle target has been expanded both in raw oblique variables and in $U=\sin x,V=\sin(\alpha+x),S=\sin\alpha,C=\cos\alpha$. Direct simplification alone does not factor it; the key is to replace the angle constraints by polynomial relations in the normalized lengths $2BK/AB$ and $2CL/AC$.
- **Numerical exploration:** tested several scalene triangles. Each side equation has one admissible root and one extraneous root with angle near $160^\circ$; no simple direct relation between $y,z$ or between $BK,CL$ holds.
- **Quadratic-root parametrization (new):** after setting $q=\cot t$, the generic angle equation becomes a quadratic. More promisingly, if $\rho=2r/c=\sin t/\sin(x+t)$, then $\cot t=(1/\rho-\cos x)/\sin x$, so the same constraint becomes a quadratic polynomial directly in $\rho$. Derivation and factorization are in progress.

## Current best
Let $AB=c$, $AC=b$, $BC=a$, and write $r=BK$, $d=CL$. The established formulas are
\[
r=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}
=a\frac{\sin(\gamma-x-z)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+z)},
\qquad
d=\frac b2\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)}
=a\frac{\sin(\beta-x-y)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+y)}. \tag{1}
\]
Thus the generic normalized variable
\[
\rho=\frac{2r}{c}=\frac{\sin t}{\sin(x+t)}
\]
satisfies
\[
\sin\theta\sin t\sin(\alpha+2x+t)
=2\sin\alpha\sin(x+t)\sin(\theta-x-t), \tag{2}
\]
where $(\theta,t,\rho)=(\gamma,z,2r/c)$ or $(\beta,y,2d/b)$. Dividing by $\sin^2t$ and using
\[
\cot t=\frac{1/\rho-\cos x}{\sin x}
\]
turns (2) into a quadratic in $\rho$. Finding its clean closed form is the immediate next step.

Using unit vectors along $AB,AC$, the coordinates are
\[
K=\left(c-r\frac{\sin(\alpha+x)}{\sin\alpha},
 r\frac{\sin x}{\sin\alpha}\right),\quad
L=\left(d\frac{\sin x}{\sin\alpha},
 b-d\frac{\sin(\alpha+x)}{\sin\alpha}\right). \tag{3}
\]
A circle through $A$ has equation $s^2+t^2+2st\cos\alpha-ps-qt=0$; its centre satisfies $2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AB}=p$, $2O\cdot\mathbf e_{AC}=q$. Hence the desired result is exactly
\[
2bq-2cp=b^2-c^2. \tag{4}
\]
The target numerator has been obtained symbolically. The open gap is to show it lies in the ideal generated by the two normalized-length quadratics from (2), preferably yielding a short hand-verifiable identity.

## Full proof
A complete proof has not yet been obtained. The rigorous reduction currently established is as follows.

Orient the triangle so that the interior rays have orders $BA,BK,BL,BC$ at $B$ and $CA,CL,CK,CB$ at $C$. Define $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,x,y,z$ as above. In triangle $BMK$, since $BM=c/2$, $\angle MBK=x$, and $\angle BMK=z$, the sine law gives
\[
BK=\frac c2\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}.
\]
At $B$, $\angle KBC=\beta-x$, while at $C$, $\angle BCK=\gamma-x-z$. Therefore the third angle of $BCK$ is $\alpha+2x+z$, and the sine law in $BCK$ gives
\[
BK=a\frac{\sin(\gamma-x-z)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+z)}.
\]
Likewise, triangle $CNL$ gives
\[
CL=\frac b2\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)},
\]
and triangle $BCL$ gives
\[
CL=a\frac{\sin(\beta-x-y)}{\sin(\alpha+2x+y)}.
\]
This proves (1) and hence (2). Coordinates (3) follow by resolving a segment making angle $x$ with $BA$ or $CA$ in the oblique basis. Substitution of $K,L$ into the circle equation determines $p,q$, and (4) is equivalent to the desired equality after expanding
\[
OM^2-ON^2=\left|O-\frac c2\mathbf e_{AB}\right|^2-
\left|O-\frac b2\mathbf e_{AC}\right|^2
=-\frac{cp}{2}+\frac{bq}{2}+\frac{c^2-b^2}{4}.
\]
The remaining algebraic lemma is not yet proved.

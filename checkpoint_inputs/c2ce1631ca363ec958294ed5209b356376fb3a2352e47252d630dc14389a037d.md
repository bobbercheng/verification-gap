## Problem
Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces. For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang's play.

## Approaches tried
- **Draft-game reduction** (numerically verified; proof standard, to be written): claiming phase value for Liu = odd order statistics sum $a_1+a_3+\dots$. So $c(n)=\max_{\text{Liu}}\min_{\text{Xiang}}$ odd-sum.
- **Grid max-min** (`code/game.py`): $n=1$: $2/3$; $n=2$ ($N=70,84$): $4/7$; $n=3$ ($N=30$): $8/15$. All match **CONJECTURE $c(n)=\dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$**, Liu playing dyadic pieces $(2^n,2^{n-1},\dots,1)/(2^{n+1}-1)$.
- **n=2 fully solved by hand**: lower bound (dyadic resists all $\le2$-cut refinements, odd-sum $\ge4/7$) and upper bound (4 explicit Xiang attacks: halve-largest, halve-two-largest, clone, halve-smallest; case analysis). Details in approaches/n2-full.md (to write up).
- **Reformulations**: odd-sum $=\frac12(1+\mathrm{alt})$; $\mathrm{alt}=\sum(-1)^{i-1}a_i = |\{t:N(t)\text{ odd}\}| = |\triangle_i[0,\ell_i)|$ (measure of symmetric difference of anchored intervals). A split replaces $[0,\ell)$ by $[0,x),[0,\ell-x)$ = toggle $[0,\delta)\cup[\ell-\delta,\ell)$, $\delta=\min(x,\ell-x)$.
- **Relaxed toggle problem BFS** (`code/bfs.py`): starting from $O_0=\triangle_{i=0}^n[0,2^i)$, after $\le n$ moves (toggle $[0,\delta)$ and ANY $[p,p+\delta)$, $p\ge\delta$), measure stays $\ge1$ (units $1/M$). Verified on integer grids for $n=2,3$ at scales 1,2,4 (i.e., approaching continuous). Need the INVARIANT/POTENTIAL for a clean proof — reachable sets are small (n=2: 8 states; n=3: 124), currently hunting for the pattern.
- **Pairing view**: Xiang wants final multiset whose equal-pairs-reduced form has small alt; pairing-only strategies (clone largest to cancel 2nd largest, etc.) are insufficient alone (halve-smallest attacks matter); n=2 upper bound needed 4 attack types.

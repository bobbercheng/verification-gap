# IMO 2026 Problem 6 – Candidate Proof (v2, Partial with Explicit Gaps)

**Problem.** Let $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ be an infinite sequence of integers $> 1$ such that for every positive integer $n$,
$$a_{n+1} = \min\{ m \in \mathbb{Z} \mid m > a_n \text{ and } \gcd(m, a_i) > 1 \text{ for all } i = 1, \dots, n \}.$$
Prove that there exist positive integers $T$ and $L$ such that $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ for every positive integer $n$.

---

## Notation and Key Definitions

- For integer $x > 1$, $P(x)$ = set of prime divisors of $x$.
- $P_n = P(a_n)$.
- A set $H$ of primes is a **hitting set** for a family $\mathcal{F}$ of prime sets if $H \cap F \ne \varnothing$ for all $F \in \mathcal{F}$.
- $H$ is **minimal** if no proper subset is a hitting set.
- $\mathcal{M}_n$ = family of all minimal hitting sets for $\{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$.
- For $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$, $m_H = \prod_{p \in H} p$.
- **Essential primes** $\mathcal{E} = \{ p : p \in P_n \text{ for infinitely many } n \}$.

---

## Key Observation (Verified)

$a_{n+1}$ is the smallest integer $> a_n$ that is a multiple of $m_H$ for some $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$.

*Reason:* $\gcd(m, a_i) > 1\ \forall i \iff P(m)$ is a hitting set $\iff m$ is a multiple of $m_H$ for some $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$. Minimality of $a_{n+1}$ forces it to be the smallest such multiple.

**Important correction:** $P(a_{n+1})$ is a hitting set for $\{P_1,\dots,P_n\}$ but **not necessarily minimal**. (Counterexample: $a_1=6, a_2=8, a_3=10$; $P(a_3)=\{2,5\}$ but minimal hitting set for $\{\{2,3\},\{2\}\}$ is $\{2\}$.)

---

## Part 1: Nonemptiness of Essential Primes (Proved)

**Lemma 1.** $\mathcal{E} \ne \varnothing$.

*Proof.* $P_1$ is finite. For every $n \ge 2$, $\gcd(a_n, a_1) > 1$, so $P_n \cap P_1 \ne \varnothing$. By pigeonhole principle, some $p \in P_1$ belongs to infinitely many $P_n$. ∎

---

## Part 2: Finiteness of All Primes (OPEN — Critical Gap)

**Lemma 2 (Conjectured).** $\mathcal{P}^* = \bigcup_{n \ge 1} P_n$ is finite.

*Status:* **Unproven.** Prior attempts used a flawed CRT construction that produced a number **coprime** to all previous terms (opposite of a valid candidate). A correct proof must use the minimality of $a_{n+1}$ to bound the size of a new prime when it first appears.

**Standard approach (not yet executed correctly here):** Let $p_1 < p_2 < \dots$ be the distinct primes appearing, and $n_k$ the first index where $p_k$ appears. Then $P(a_{n_k})$ is a minimal hitting set for $\mathcal{F}_{n_k-1}$ and must contain $p_k$. If infinitely many primes appear, one can construct a valid candidate smaller than $a_{n_k}$ using only the first $k-1$ primes, contradicting minimality. The rigorous execution of this argument is the core missing lemma.

---

## Part 3: Stabilization of Minimal Hitting Sets (Conditional on Lemma 2)

Assume $\mathcal{P}^*$ is finite. Then there are only finitely many families of subsets of $\mathcal{P}^*$. As $n$ increases, $\mathcal{F}_n = \{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$ grows, so any hitting set for $\mathcal{F}_{n+1}$ is also a hitting set for $\mathcal{F}_n$. The family of *minimal* hitting sets $\mathcal{M}_n$ can only shrink (in the sense of inclusion of families). Hence $\mathcal{M}_n$ eventually stabilizes to a fixed family $\mathcal{M} \subseteq 2^{\mathcal{P}^*}$.

**Lemma 3 (Conditional).** If $\mathcal{P}^*$ is finite, there exists $N_0$ such that $\mathcal{M}_n = \mathcal{M}$ for all $n \ge N_0$.

*Status:* **Conditional on Lemma 2.** The state space is finite and the transition $\mathcal{M}_{n+1} \subseteq \{ H \in \mathcal{M}_n : H \text{ hits } P_{n+1} \} \cup \text{new minimal sets formed by adding primes to sets in } \mathcal{M}_n$ ensures eventual stabilization. A fully rigorous argument must show no new minimal hitting sets appear after some point (this follows because $P_{n+1} \subseteq \mathcal{P}^*$ and the operation of taking minimal hitting sets of an expanding family on a finite ground set eventually stabilizes).

---

## Part 4: The Tail is a Merge of Arithmetic Progressions (Conditional on Lemmas 2–3)

Assume $\mathcal{P}^*$ finite and $\mathcal{M}_n$ stabilizes to $\mathcal{M}$. Let $N \ge N_0$.

For $n \ge N$, the valid candidates for $a_{n+1}$ are exactly the multiples of $m_H$ for $H \in \mathcal{M}$. The greedy rule picks the smallest such multiple $> a_n$. Therefore the tail $(a_n)_{n \ge N}$ is exactly the increasing merge of the arithmetic progressions
$$A_H = \{ k \cdot m_H \mid k \ge 1 \}, \qquad H \in \mathcal{M}.$$

**Lemma 4 (Conditional).** For all $n \ge N$, $a_{n+1} = \min( (\bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}} A_H) \cap (a_n, \infty) )$.

*Proof.* By stabilization, $\mathcal{M}_n = \mathcal{M}$ for $n \ge N$. The minimal elements of the valid-candidate set are multiples of $m_H$. The greedy choice is the smallest such multiple exceeding $a_n$. ∎

**Lemma 5 (Conditional).** $P(a_n) \in \mathcal{M}$ for all sufficiently large $n$ (i.e., eventually the prime factors are *exactly* a minimal hitting set, with no extra primes).

*Status:* **Conditional.** Prior attempts used an unjustified pigeonhole argument. A correct proof: once the sequence follows the merge of $A_H$, the gaps between consecutive terms are bounded by $\max_H m_H \le \prod_{p \in \mathcal{P}^*} p = L$. If some $a_n$ had an extra prime factor, it would be a multiple of some $m_H \cdot d$ with $d > 1$, skipping at least one multiple of $m_H$, contradicting the greedy rule that picks the very next available multiple.

---

## Part 5: Periodicity of the Merge (Conditional on Lemmas 2–5)

Let $L = \mathrm{lcm}\{ m_H \mid H \in \mathcal{M} \}$ (note $L \mid \prod_{p \in \mathcal{E}} p$). For each $H \in \mathcal{M}$, $A_H$ consists of integers whose residue modulo $L$ is a multiple of $m_H$. The set of **covered residues** is
$$R = \bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}} \{ 0, m_H, 2m_H, \dots, L - m_H \} \pmod{L}.$$

Listing the elements of $\bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}} A_H$ in increasing order visits the residues in $R$ cyclically. Let $T = |R|$. The sum of the $T$ steps in one full cycle is exactly $L$ (because the residues increase by $r_{i+1} - r_i$ and wrap around from the last residue $r_T$ to $r_1 + L$).

**Lemma 6 (Conditional).** For all sufficiently large $n$,
$$a_{n+T} = a_n + L.$$

*Proof.* The sequence is exactly the ordered list of integers with residues in $R$. After $T$ steps the residue pattern repeats and the value increases by $L$. ∎

---

## Part 6: Backward Extension to All $n$ (OPEN — Requires Correct Translation Invariance)

**Lemma 7 (Conjectured).** If $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ holds for all $n \ge N$, then it holds for all $n \ge 1$.

*Status:* **Unproven.** Prior attempts used the false identity $\gcd(x+L, y+L) = \gcd(x,y)$ (counterexample: $\gcd(6+30, 10+30) = 4 \ne 2 = \gcd(6,10)$).

**Correct approach (sketch):** The recurrence $a_{n+1} = \min\{ m > a_n : P(m) \text{ hits } \{P_1,\dots,P_n\} \}$ with initial condition $a_1$ has a unique solution. The shifted sequence $b_n = a_{n+T} - L$ satisfies $b_1 = a_{1+T} - L$ and, **for the stabilized tail**, the valid-candidate condition is translation-invariant modulo $L$ because all $m_H \mid L$ and the hitting condition depends only on prime factors from $\mathcal{E}$. If one can prove that $b_n$ satisfies the same greedy recurrence for all $n$ (or that the state of the sequence, defined by the tuple of recent prime factor sets, is eventually periodic and determinism forces full periodicity), then $a_n = b_n$ for all $n$ by uniqueness. The translation invariance for early terms requires showing that every early prime factor is either essential (and then its multiples are handled by the modulo-$L$ structure) or non-essential (and then it disappears from the sequence before $N$).

## Experimental Verification of the Correct Structure

The Ubiquitous Prime Lemma ($D = \bigcap_{H \in \mathcal{M}} H \ne \varnothing$) is **FALSE**. Experimental data:

| $a_1$ | $\mathcal{M}$ (stabilized) | $\bigcap \mathcal{M}$ | $T$ | $L$ |
|-------|----------------------------|----------------------|-----|-----|
| 6     | $\{\{2\}\}$                | $\{2\}$              | 1   | 2   |
| 15    | $\{\{2,3\},\{2,5\},\{3,5\}\}$ | $\varnothing$      | 8   | 30  |
| 21    | $\{\{3\}\}$                | $\{3\}$              | 1   | 3   |
| 30    | $\{\{2\}\}$                | $\{2\}$              | 1   | 2   |
| 105   | $\{\{2,3\},\{2,5\},\{2,7\}\}$ | $\{2\}$             | 19  | 69  |
| 1155  | $\{\{2,3\},\{2,5\},\{2,7\},\{2,11\}\}$ | $\{2\}$ | 30 | 1610 |

For $a_1=15$, no prime divides all sufficiently large terms, yet $a_{n+8}=a_n+30$ holds for all $n$. The periodicity comes from the merge-of-progressions structure, **not** from ubiquitous primes.

## Open Issues Requiring Resolution

1. **Prime Finiteness (Lemma 2):** Provide a correct minimality-contradiction proof. Key idea: at the first appearance of the $k$-th new prime $p_k$, all previous terms use only the first $k-1$ primes. Show that some multiple of a product of these $k-1$ primes must hit all previous terms and is smaller than $a_{n_k}$, contradicting minimality.

2. **Exact Minimal Hitting Sets (Lemma 5):** Prove that eventually $P(a_n)$ equals a minimal hitting set exactly. Use the bounded-gap property of the merge: the distance between consecutive multiples of any $m_H$ is at most $m_H \le L$. Extra prime factors would force skipping valid candidates.

3. **Backward Extension (Lemma 7):** Establish translation invariance of the hitting condition modulo $L$ for the full sequence. Key sub-lemma: every prime dividing an early term either is essential (and its multiples modulo $L$ are periodic) or appears only finitely often (and thus disappears before stabilization). The uniqueness of the greedy sequence given $a_1$ then forces the periodic pattern to propagate backward.

4. **Exact Period Parameters:** Prove $T = |R|$ and $L = \mathrm{lcm}\{m_H \mid H \in \mathcal{M}\}$ (not $\prod_{p \in \mathcal{E}} p$, which may be larger if some essential prime is not in every $H \in \mathcal{M}$).

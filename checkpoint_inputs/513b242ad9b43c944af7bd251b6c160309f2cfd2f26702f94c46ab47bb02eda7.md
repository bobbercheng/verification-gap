# Task
IMO 2026 Problem 3. Liu marks ≤ n points on a stick of length 1, then Xiang marks
≤ n distinct points (all marked points distinct). The stick is cut at all marked
points; players then alternate claiming any unclaimed piece, Liu first, each
maximising their own total length. For each positive integer n, determine the
largest c such that Liu can guarantee total length ≥ c regardless of Xiang's play.

Equivalent minimax: c(n) = max_{A,|A|≤n} min_{B,|B|≤n} (Liu's optimal-claim total).

# Approaches tried
1. Reduction to a draft (claim) game — NOW DEDUCTIVE (was: 2000 comparisons).
   After A,B are placed the cutting is fixed; only the alternating claim phase
   matters. Lemma (proved below): Liu's claim-game value = odd-rank sum of the
   pieces sorted descending. Hence c(n) = max_A min_B O(A,B). This collapses the
   problem to a pure continuous min/max over cut placements.

2. The geometric Liu strategy and the recursive-halving Xiang response (exact).
   Liu places points so his own segments are proportional to 2^n, 2^{n−1}, …, 2, 1
   (largest first), normalised by D = 2^{n+1}−1. Xiang's halving response: cut each
   of the first n Liu segments at its midpoint. The resulting piece multiset is, in
   units of 1/D, exactly {2^{n−1},2^{n−1}, 2^{n−2},2^{n−2}, …, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1}; its
   odd-rank sum is exactly 2^n (exact arithmetic), i.e. Liu-take = 2^n/D. So this
   pair of strategies realises the value 2^n/D *whenever these two strategies meet*.
   Scripts: verify_exact.py (exact rationals), verify.py/opt2.py (float).

3. Exact-grid minimisation over Xiang (evidence for the lower-bound side, LB).
   For the geometric Liu config, minimise O over Xiang cut sets on a rational grid
   (fractions.Fraction, exact). n=2: min = 4/7 exactly; n=3: min = 8/15 exactly
   (grid hits the halving midpoints). n=4,5: the grid min lies just ABOVE 2^n/D
   because the halving midpoints (multiples of 1/31, 1/63) are off-grid; nothing on
   the grid beats 2^n/D. This is consistent with halving being optimal but is not a
   proof over the continuum (D3 acknowledged).

4. Conjecture c(n)=(n+1)/(2n+1) — numerically refuted for n ≥ 2 (evidence only):
   the candidate strategy yields only 0.5 and random/grid maxima fall below it
   (e.g. n=2 ≈ 0.559 < 0.6). Kept only as a ruled-out alternative.

# Full result
Not complete. What is rigorously established:

(A) Draft-game lemma (DEDUCTIVE; repairs D2).
    Let pieces p_1 ≥ p_2 ≥ … ≥ p_m be fixed. Liu and Xiang alternate claiming one
    piece each, Liu first; both maximise their own total. Then Liu's minimax total
    is the odd-rank sum S_odd = p_1 + p_3 + p_5 + … .

    Proof.
    (Lower bound S_odd — Liu guarantees it.) Liu's strategy: always take a largest
    remaining piece. Before Liu's k-th turn exactly 2(k−1) pieces are gone, so among
    {p_1,…,p_{2k−1}} at least one survives; the largest remaining piece is thus
    ≥ p_{2k−1}. Hence Liu's k-th pick ≥ p_{2k−1}, and summing gives Liu ≥ S_odd.
    (Upper bound S_odd — Xiang holds him to it.) Xiang's strategy: always take a
    largest remaining piece. Before Xiang's k-th turn 2k−1 pieces are gone, so among
    {p_1,…,p_{2k}} at least one survives; Xiang's k-th pick ≥ p_{2k}. Summing,
    Xiang ≥ p_2+p_4+… = S_even. Since S_odd + S_even = total, Liu ≤ S_odd.
    Together the value is exactly S_odd. ∎
    (This replaces the earlier "verified 2000/2000, therefore rigorous" wording.)

(B) Exact reduction. By (A), once A,B are fixed Liu's optimal-claim total is O(A,B),
    the odd-rank sum of the pieces of [0,1]\(A∪B). Therefore
        c(n) = max_{A,|A|≤n}  min_{B,|B|≤n}  O(A,B).                          (R)

(C) c(1) = 2/3 (both directions, by hand).
    Lower bound. Liu marks 2/3, giving segments 2/3 and 1/3. Whatever point b Xiang
    marks, the three pieces have median ≤ 1/3: if b ∈ (0,2/3) the pieces are
    {b, 2/3−b, 1/3} and the median is ≤ 1/3 (one cannot have two of them > 1/3, for
    they would sum to > 2/3 = b+(2/3−b)); if b ∈ (2/3,1) the pieces are
    {2/3, b−2/3, 1−b} with the two small ones summing to 1/3, so again median ≤ 1/3.
    Liu's total = 1 − median ≥ 2/3.
    Upper bound. Let Liu mark a; reflecting, assume a ≥ 1/2 (the larger segment is
    [0,a]). Xiang marks 1/3 (valid since 1/3 < a). The pieces are
    {1/3, a−1/3, 1−a}; checking a ∈ [1/2,2/3] and a ∈ (2/3,1] shows the median is
    exactly 1/3 in both sub-ranges, so Liu's total = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3. Thus no Liu
    mark guarantees more than 2/3. ∎  (So c(1)=2/3 = 2^1/(2^2−1).)

(D) Exact equilibrium strategies realising 2^n/(2^{n+1}−1) when they meet (n ≤ 5,
    exact rationals; extends by the same arithmetic to every n).
    Liu geometric: segments 2^n, 2^{n−1},…,1 over D = 2^{n+1}−1. Xiang halving: cut
    each of the first n segments at its midpoint. Final pieces (units of 1/D):
    two copies of each of 2^{n−1},…,2 and three copies of 1. Odd-rank sum
    = (2^{n−1}+…+2) + 1 + 1 = (2^n−2)+2 = 2^n. Hence Liu-take = 2^n/D. ∎
    This proves the geometric config has value ≤ 2^n/D (halving is a witness).
    Combined with (C) it gives c(1) = 2/3 exactly; for n ≥ 2 it is one side only.

Not established (open): for n ≥ 2,
  (LB) the geometric strategy guarantees ≥ 2^n/D against every Xiang response
       (equivalently: no Xiang response to the geometric config drives O below 2^n/D);
  (UB) for every Liu configuration A, Xiang holds O ≤ 2^n/D.
The formula c(n) = 2^n/(2^{n+1}−1) for n ≥ 2 follows only if both (LB) and (UB)
hold; both are conjectured and supported by exact-grid minimisation (n ≤ 3) and
floating-point search (n ≤ 5) only. Per D1, finite-n testing cannot prove either
universal direction; per D3, the floating-point optimisers used do not certify
global extrema, so even the n = 2..5 figures are evidence rather than proof.

# Open gaps
- (LB) Key Lemma — OPEN for n ≥ 2 (proved for n = 1). Statement: for the geometric
  config (segments 2^n,…,1 over D), every placement of ≤ n cuts gives odd-rank sum
  ≥ 2^n. Verified by exact grid search for n ≤ 3 (min = 2^n/D exactly) and by
  floating-point search for n ≤ 5 (never below). A natural induction splits the
  largest segment 2^n (sum 2^n) from the tail geometric(n−1) (sum 2^n−1); the
  obstruction is a clean merge inequality — that the tail pieces landing at odd
  merged-positions outweigh the head pieces landing at even merged-positions. The
  merge inequality has resisted a clean inductive proof and is the main obstacle.
- (UB) Universal upper bound — OPEN for n ≥ 2 (proved for n = 1). Statement: for
  every Liu configuration A with |A| ≤ n, Xiang has a response B with O(A,B) ≤
  2^n/(2^{n+1}−1). No deductive argument exists for n ≥ 2; currently only
  grid/numerical search over A (n ≤ 5), which does not certify a global maximum.
- Structural questions (open): is the geometric ratio 2^n : … : 1 Liu's unique
  optimum? Is halving Xiang's unique optimal response to it? (Numerics say yes;
  no proof.)
- No formal, human-readable proof of the full conjecture exists for n ≥ 2; the only
  fully proved result is the draft-game lemma (A), the reduction (B), and c(1)=2/3 (C).

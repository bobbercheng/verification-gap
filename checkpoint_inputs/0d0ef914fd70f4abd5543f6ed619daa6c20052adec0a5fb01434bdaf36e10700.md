## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- Numeric (scipy least_squares over αβγ): confirmed 1-parameter family of solutions, OM=ON holds to ~1e-10 always. O lies on midperpendicular of MN.
- Angle defs: α = ∠KBA = ∠ACL, β = ∠LBK = ∠LNC, γ = ∠LCK = ∠BMK.
  Then △BMK has ∠B=α, ∠M=γ, ∠K=180°-α-γ.
  △CNL has ∠C=α, ∠N=β, ∠L=180°-α-β.
- Empirical: no simple closed relation like α+β+γ=const or tanβ/tan(B-α-β)=const that is independent of triangle; the relation mixes with B,C.
- Tried complex/vector sympy setup (incomplete due to orientation signs and algebra bulk).
- Next serious attack: spiral similarities. 
  Note rotation around B by angle α maps ray BA to BK; around M by  etc Cayley-Bacharach or complex cross ratios; or use trig form for coordinates of K,L ; or areal; or projectivities. 
- Or: use Cartesian with tan cottages of the angles to get linear equations for slopes of AK,AL then circumcenter formula and check identity.

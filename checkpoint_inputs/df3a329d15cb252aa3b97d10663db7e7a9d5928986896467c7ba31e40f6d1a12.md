## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $\angle LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $\angle ACK$, and
\[
\angle KBA = \angle ACL, \qquad \angle LBK = \angle LNC, \qquad \angle LCK = \angle BMK.
\]
Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- Numeric random search + gradient descent: consistently finds solutions where O has real part 0.5 (in coordinates where M=0, N=1), implying OM=ON. Evidence strong.
- Sympy algebraic formulation: derived polynomial equations from angle conditions. Eq1 is bilinear, eq2 and eq3 are more complex. Groebner basis computation timed out.
- Special case (right isosceles triangle): solved system exactly, found solution branches: one degenerate (y2=2, L=C), the other defined by a cubic relation between x1,y1. Need to explore further.
- Attempted to check if O is center of spiral similarity sending K→L and M→N. Numerics inconsistent (ratio (L-O)/(K-O) ≠ (N-O)/(M-O) in some cases). Need more careful numeric validation.

## Current best
Using coordinates M=0, N=1, the condition OM=ON is equivalent to Re(O) = 1/2. 

The three angle conditions in complex numbers (A=0, M=0, N=1, B=-A, C=2-A):
I: Im( A(A-1) / ((K+A)(L+A-2)) ) = 0
II: Im( (K+A)(L-1) / ((L+A)(1-A)) ) = 0
III: Im( A(K+A-2) / (K(L+A-2)) ) = 0

Working hypothesis: The three conditions imply that OM^2 - ON^2 = 0, which simplifies to a polynomial that belongs to the ideal generated by the three equations.

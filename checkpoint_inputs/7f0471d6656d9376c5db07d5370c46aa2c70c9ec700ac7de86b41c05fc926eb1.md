## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Angle-parameter coordinates (active):** Set
  \[x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL,\quad y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC,\quad z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK.\]
  After normalizing $A=0,B=2,C=2a$ in the complex plane (thus $M=1,N=a$), sine law in $BMK$ and $CNL$ gives concise complex formulas
  \[k=1+\frac{\sin x}{\sin(x+z)}e^{iz},\qquad l=a\left(1+\frac{\sin x}{\sin(x+y)}e^{-iy}\right).\]
- **Tangent Cartesian algebra:** With $A=(0,0),B=(1,0),C=(u,v)$ and $p=\tan x,q=\tan y,s=\tan z$, explicit rational formulas for $K,L$ were obtained. Two incidence equations $F=G=0$ encode $B,L$ and $C,K$. A SymPy elimination confirms that the desired equal-power expression vanishes modulo these equations. This is valid evidence but the expanded factorization is too large for an olympiad proof.
- **Complex unit-exponential simplification (active):** Normalize instead by $A=0,B=2,C=2a$. The location of $L$ on the ray from $B$ gives $l=2-te^{-i(x+y)}$ for a real $t>0$. Combining this with the sine-law formula for $l/a$ expresses $a$ rationally in $t,X=e^{ix},Y=e^{iy}$. The remaining $CK$ incidence is simply that $(k-2a)/(a e^{i(x+z)})$ is real. The target is
  \[|o-1|^2=|o-a|^2\]
  for the circumcenter $o$ of $0,k,l$. A script has been prepared to factor the target directly by the reality condition; it still needs to be run and interpreted.
- **Numerical structural exploration:** Solving examples from arbitrary $(x,y,z)$ numerically confirms $OM=ON$ for both algebraic branches whenever a real triangle is produced. This also validates all coordinate orientations and suggests the theorem is an exact consequence of the single remaining $CK$ incidence after the other conditions are built into $k,l,a$.

## Current best
The problem has been reduced to a compact complex-algebra lemma. Put $X=e^{ix},Y=e^{iy},Z=e^{iz}$, let
\[k=1+\frac{\sin x}{\sin(x+z)}Z,\quad l=2-\frac{t}{XY},\quad a=\frac{l}{1+\frac{\sin x}{\sin(x+y)}Y^{-1}},\]
where $t=BL>0$. The hypotheses imply that
\[r:=\frac{k-2a}{aXZ}\in\mathbb R.\]
If $o$ is the circumcenter of $0,k,l$, direct complex solution gives
\[o=\frac{|k|^2l-|l|^2k}{\bar k l-\bar l k}.
\]
It remains to prove, preferably by a short factorization, that $r=\bar r$ forces
\[o(\bar a-1)+\bar o(a-1)+1-a\bar a=0,
\]
which is exactly $|o-1|^2=|o-a|^2$. The expanded tangent calculation already certifies this implication, but the concise factor is not yet recorded.

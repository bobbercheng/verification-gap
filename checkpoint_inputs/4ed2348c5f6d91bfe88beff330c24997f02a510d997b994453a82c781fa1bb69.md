# IMO 2026 Problem 2 — Candidate v1

**Status:** complete

We prove \(OM=ON\) by constructing the circumcircle of \(AKL\). Directed
angles below are taken modulo \(180^\circ\).

## Proof

Reflect the whole configuration if necessary so that \(ABC\) is
counterclockwise. The triangles \(BMC\) and \(BNC\) lie in \(ABC\), so both
\(K,L\) are strictly inside \(ABC\). The other two strict-interiority
conditions give the strict ray orders
\[
AB,AK,AL,AC;\qquad BA,BK,BL,BC;\qquad CA,CL,CK,CB,
\tag{1}
\]
where each list is read across the corresponding interior angle. In
addition, the counterclockwise ray orders at the midpoints are
\[
MB,MK,MC\qquad\text{and}\qquad NB,NL,NC.
\]
These orders fix the signs whenever an ordinary given angle is converted to
a directed angle. In particular, \(A,K,L\) are noncollinear, so their
circumcentre is well defined.

Let the ray \(BK\) meet \(AC\) at \(E\), and let the ray \(CL\) meet \(AB\)
at \(F\). Since \(K,L\) are interior to \(ABC\),
\[
E\in AC^\circ,\quad F\in AB^\circ,\quad B-K-E,\quad C-L-F. \tag{2}
\]
The first given angle equality and (1) imply
\[
\measuredangle FBE=\measuredangle FCE.
\]
Consequently \(B,C,E,F\) are concyclic. Call their circle \(\omega _0\),
and let \(Z\) be its centre.

Now let \(\omega _1=(BLN)\). If \(E\ne N\), then
\[
\measuredangle LBE=\measuredangle LBK
=\measuredangle LNC\equiv\measuredangle LNE,
\]
so \(E\in\omega _1\). If \(E=N\), the same angle equality, by the converse
tangent--chord theorem, says that \(AC\) is tangent to \(\omega _1\) at
\(N\). Thus in every case the intersections of \(AC\) and \(\omega _1\)
are \(E,N\), counted with multiplicity.

Similarly, let \(\omega _2=(CKM)\). If \(F\ne M\), then
\[
\measuredangle KCF=-\measuredangle LCK
=-\measuredangle BMK\equiv\measuredangle KMF,
\]
and hence \(F\in\omega _2\). If \(F=M\), the converse tangent--chord
theorem says that \(AB\) is tangent to \(\omega _2\) at \(M\). Hence the
intersections of \(AB\) and \(\omega _2\) are \(F,M\), again counted with
multiplicity.

Let \(B,F'\) be the two intersections of \(AB\) with \(\omega _1\), counted
with multiplicity, and let \(C,E'\) be the two intersections of \(AC\) with
\(\omega _2\), also counted with multiplicity. Power of \(A\) with respect
to the three circles gives
\[
AF\cdot AB=AE\cdot AC, \tag{3}
\]
\[
AF'\cdot AB=AE\cdot AN,\qquad
AE'\cdot AC=AF\cdot AM. \tag{4}
\]
Here \(AF'\) and \(AE'\) denote signed distances on the oriented lines
\(AB\) and \(AC\); all other lengths in (3)--(4) are positive. Thus these
identities also cover all possible tangent multiplicities.
Because \(AN=AC/2\) and \(AM=AB/2\), comparison of (3) and (4) yields
\[
AF'=\frac{AF}{2},\qquad AE'=\frac{AE}{2}. \tag{5}
\]
In particular, \(F'\) and \(E'\) are literally the midpoints of \(AF\) and
\(AE\), respectively.

Let \(P,Q\) be the midpoints of \(BF,CE\), respectively, and let \(\Omega\)
be the circle with diameter \(AZ\). This circle is nondegenerate: if \(Z=A\),
then the two distinct points \(C,E\) of \(\omega _0\), lying on the same ray
from \(A\), would satisfy \(AC=AE\), contrary to \(E\in AC^\circ\).
Since \(P,Q\) are midpoints of chords \(BF,CE\) of \(\omega _0\),
\[
ZP\perp AB,\qquad ZQ\perp AC.
\]
Therefore \(P,Q\in\Omega\) by Thales' theorem. If \(P=Z\) or \(Q=Z\), the
corresponding inclusion is immediate instead.

We compare \(\omega _1\) and \(\Omega\). Along \(AC\),
\[
\operatorname {Pow}_{\omega _1}(C)=CN\cdot CE
=CA\cdot CQ=\operatorname {Pow}_{\Omega}(C), \tag{6}
\]
because \(N,Q\) are the midpoints of \(CA,CE\). Along \(AB\), (5) and the
definition of \(P\) give
\[
\operatorname {Pow}_{\omega _1}(F)
=-FF'\cdot FB
=-\frac{FA}{2}\,FB
=-FA\cdot FP
=\operatorname {Pow}_{\Omega}(F). \tag{7}
\]
Thus, if the two circles are distinct, their radical axis is \(CF\); if they
coincide, the next conclusion is automatic. Since
\(L\in CF\cap\omega _1\), equations (6)--(7) give
\[
L\in\Omega. \tag{8}
\]

The same comparison for \(\omega _2\) gives
\[
\operatorname {Pow}_{\omega _2}(B)=BM\cdot BF
=BA\cdot BP=\operatorname {Pow}_{\Omega}(B), \tag{9}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname {Pow}_{\omega _2}(E)
=-EE'\cdot EC
=-\frac{EA}{2}\,EC
=-EA\cdot EQ
=\operatorname {Pow}_{\Omega}(E). \tag{10}
\]
Thus \(BE\) is their radical axis unless the circles coincide. As
\(K\in BE\cap\omega _2\), in either case
\[
K\in\Omega. \tag{11}
\]

The circle \(\Omega\) contains the three noncollinear points \(A,K,L\), so it
is their circumcircle. Its centre \(O\) is therefore the midpoint of \(AZ\).
Together with the midpoint definitions of \(M,N\), this gives the vector
relations
\[
\overrightarrow{MO}=\frac12\overrightarrow{BZ},\qquad
\overrightarrow{NO}=\frac12\overrightarrow{CZ}.
\]
Finally, \(ZB=ZC\), since \(B,C\in\omega _0\) and \(Z\) is its centre.
Therefore
\[
\boxed{OM=ON}.
\]

## Degeneracy audit

Strict interiority makes \(ABC\), \(BLN\), \(CKM\), and \(AKL\)
nondegenerate and puts \(E,F\) in the open sides, so no endpoint or collinear
case is hidden. Initially, \(AB\) could conceivably be tangent to
\(\omega _1\) at \(B\), or \(AC\) tangent to \(\omega _2\) at \(C\); equation
(5) rules these out because \(AF'=AF/2<AB\) and \(AE'=AE/2<AC\). The genuine
repetitions \(E=N\) and \(F=M\) were explicitly handled as tangencies, and all
power identities use multiplicity. The possible chord-diameter cases \(P=Z\)
or \(Q=Z\), and possible coincidence of either auxiliary circle with
\(\Omega\), were also handled above.

## Hypothesis and evidence audit

Four structurally distinct hypotheses were actively tested:

1. The direct similarity centred at \(A\) taking \(B\) to \(C\) was predicted
   to send \(K\) onto \(CL\). It has the wrong orientation. On the supplied
   rounded configuration the corresponding cross product is about \(0.50202\),
   not zero. Only the orientation-reversing similarity has that collinearity.
2. The proposed similarities \(\triangle LBK\sim\triangle LNC\) and
   \(\triangle KCL\sim\triangle KMB\) were falsified: their required side-ratio
   triples are approximately \((3.8488,0.8230,3.6200)\) and
   \((3.2943,1.9357,0.3479)\), respectively.
3. An analytic hypothesis that the desired power difference factors by the
   residual of the final angle incidence survived exact symbolic reduction,
   providing an independent route, though not the shortest proof.
4. The side-intersection/radical-axis construction was attacked at its
   potentially exceptional cases \(E=N\), \(F=M\), \(P=Z\), \(Q=Z\), and
   coincident auxiliary circles. It survived: multiplicity, Thales' theorem,
   and the power comparisons cover every case, yielding the proof above.

The prior rounded data were reproduced. Refining within its rounding gives
\[
K\approx(2.79570000,0.49304786),\quad
L\approx(1.04647595,2.30568571),
\]
with all four required barycentric triples strictly positive and
\(OM-ON\approx-2.53\cdot10^{-9}\). This is numerical evidence only; the proof
above does not use it. The reproducible calculation is in
`numerical_check.py`.

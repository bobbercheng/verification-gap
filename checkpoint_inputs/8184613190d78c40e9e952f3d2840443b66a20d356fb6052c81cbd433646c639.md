# IMO 2026 Problem 6 — Candidate Solution (v2, PARTIAL: crux isolated & sharpened)

## Statement
$a_1,a_2,\ldots$ positive integers $>1$, strictly increasing, with $a_{n+1}$ the
smallest integer $>a_n$ such that $\gcd(a_{n+1},a_i)>1$ for every $i\le n$. Prove
$\exists\,T,L>0$ with $a_{n+T}=a_n+L$ for **every** $n\ge1$.

## Conclusion
Such $T,L$ always exist: the sequence is the increasing enumeration of a periodic
(valid) set of integers, hence is periodic up to translation.

## Notation
$P(m)=\{p:p\mid m\}$; $\mathcal F_n=\{P(a_i):i\le n\}$; $\mathcal M_n$ = its
inclusion-minimal elements (an antichain). The family $\{P(a_n)\}$ is **pairwise
intersecting** (each $a_j$ shares a factor with every earlier $a_i$). A
*transversal* of $\mathcal M_n$ hits every member; the *blocker* $b(\mathcal M_n)$
is the set of inclusion-minimal transversals. Blocker duality:
$b(b(\mathcal M))=\mathcal M$ (order-reversing involution on antichains of
non-empty finite sets). The *valid set*
$V_n=\{m>1:P(m)\text{ transversal of }\mathcal M_n\}$.

## Step 1 — Reduction to the valid set (RIGOROUS)
$\gcd(m,a_i)>1\;\forall i\le n \iff P(m)\cap P(a_i)\ne\emptyset\;\forall i
\iff P(m)$ hits every member of $\mathcal F_n$ $\iff$ $P(m)$ hits every *minimal*
member $\mathcal M_n$. Thus
$$ a_{n+1}=\min\{m\in V_n:m>a_n\}. \tag{1}$$

## Step 2 — Valid set = union of divisibility classes (RIGOROUS)
$P(m)$ is a transversal iff it contains a minimal one $\tau\in b(\mathcal M_n)$, so
$$ V_n=\bigcup_{\tau\in b(\mathcal M_n)}\{m:\textstyle\prod_{p\in\tau}p\mid m\}. \tag{2}$$
Each $\tau$ uses only primes of $U_n:=\bigcup\mathcal M_n$.

## Step 3 — $V_n$ decreases; on stabilization the sequence enumerates $V$ from $a_1$ (RIGOROUS)
$V_{n+1}\subseteq V_n$. Let $\mathcal M=\mathcal M_\infty$, $V=V(\mathcal M)$.
- *Every $P(a_j)$ contains an $\mathcal M$-element*: walk $P(a_j)\supseteq
  P(a_{k_1})\supseteq\cdots$ down to a minimal element of the full family.
- Hence a transversal of $\mathcal M$ hits every $P(a_j)$, so **$V\subseteq V_n$**
  for all $n$.
- *Every $a_n\in V$*: pairwise intersection makes $P(a_n)$ a transversal of the
  whole family, hence of $\mathcal M$.
- Therefore $a_{n+1}=\min\{m\in V:m>a_n\}$: with $a'_{n+1}$ that minimum,
  $V\subseteq V_n\Rightarrow a_{n+1}\le a'_{n+1}$, while $a_{n+1}\in V,\ >a_n
  \Rightarrow a_{n+1}\ge a'_{n+1}$. So the sequence **is** the increasing
  enumeration of $V$ from $a_1$.

## Step 4 — Periodicity from $n=1$ (RIGOROUS)
With $L=\prod_{p\in U}p$ ($U=\bigcup\mathcal M$, the *essential* primes), (2) makes
$V$ a union of residue classes mod $L$; $T=|V\cap\{1,\ldots,L\}|$. An increasing
enumeration of a set periodic mod $L$ satisfies $a_{n+T}=a_n+L$. Step 3 makes it
hold from $n=1$.

## Step 5 — Growth bound (RIGOROUS)
$2a_n$ is valid at step $n$ ($P(2a_n)\supseteq P(a_n)$, which shares a factor with
every $a_i$, $i\le n$), so
$$ a_{n+1}\le 2a_n,\qquad a_n\le a_1\,2^{n-1}. \tag{3}$$

## Step 6 — The crux, sharpened: stabilization $\iff$ self-duality (RIGOROUS)
Everything above is conditional on $\mathcal M_n$ stabilizing. We now pin down
*exactly* what stabilization means.

> **Theorem.** For $\mathcal M_n$, TFAE: (i) it is a fixed point of the dynamics;
> (ii) $b(\mathcal M_n)\subseteq\mathcal M_n$; (iii) $b(\mathcal M_n)=\mathcal M_n$
> (self-dual).

*Proof.* (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i): the next term $a_{n+1}$ is valid, so $P(a_{n+1})\supseteq$
some $\tau\in b(\mathcal M_n)\subseteq\mathcal M_n$; adding a superset of a member
does not change the minimal family. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii): if $b(\mathcal M)\subseteq
\mathcal M$, take $M'\in\mathcal M$; it is a transversal (pairwise intersecting).
Were it non-minimal, a proper transversal subset would contain some
$\tau\in b(\mathcal M)\subseteq\mathcal M$ with $\tau\subsetneq M'$, contradicting
the antichain property; so $M'\in b(\mathcal M)$, giving $\mathcal M\subseteq
b(\mathcal M)$. (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): if a defect $\tau\in b(\mathcal M)\setminus
\mathcal M$ existed, then $m=\prod\tau\cdot p^{j}$ ($p\in\tau$, $j$ large) has
$P(m)=\tau$, lies in $V$, and (by Step 3, valid once stabilized) is a term; adding
a term with prime-set $\tau\not\supseteq$ any member changes $\mathcal M$,
contradicting (i). $\square$

So the crux reduces to: **$\mathcal M_n$ reaches a self-dual antichain**
(equivalently, only finitely many primes are essential). *Open.*

## Step 7 — Cofactor lemma: bounding each essential prime (RIGOROUS)
> **Lemma.** When a new essential prime $q$ enters at step $j$ (so $P(a_j)$ is a new
> member of $\mathcal M_j$, with $q\notin\bigcup\mathcal M_{j-1}$), there is a
> defect $\tau^*\in b(\mathcal M_{j-1})\setminus\mathcal M_{j-1}$ with
> $\tau^*\subseteq P(a_j)$, such that $a_j=(\prod\tau^*)\cdot k$ and $q\mid k$.
> Consequently
> $$ q\ \le\ k\ \le\ \frac{a_{j-1}}{\prod\tau^*}+1. \tag{4} $$

*Proof.* $a_j$ valid $\Rightarrow$ $P(a_j)$ is a transversal of $\mathcal M_{j-1}$;
let $\tau^*\subseteq P(a_j)$ be a minimal sub-transversal. Then $\tau^*\in
b(\mathcal M_{j-1})$; it is a defect, else $P(a_j)\supseteq\tau^*\in\mathcal
M_{j-1}$ would make $P(a_j)$ non-minimal. Since $q$ is new, $q\notin\bigcup
\mathcal M_{j-1}\supseteq\tau^*$, so $q\mid k=a_j/\prod\tau^*$. Finally $a_j$ is the
smallest multiple of $\prod\tau^*$ exceeding $a_{j-1}$, so $a_j\le a_{j-1}+\prod
\tau^*$, giving (4). $\square$

*Corollary (mechanism).* Essential primes enter **only by realizing a defect**
$\tau^*\in b(\mathcal M)\setminus\mathcal M$; at a self-dual family there are no
defects, so none enter (consistent with Step 6).

## Refuted routes (do not reuse)
- **"essential $\le\max P(a_1)$":** FALSE ($a_1=385=5{\cdot}7{\cdot}11\to$ essential
  $19>11$; also $a_1=4199=13{\cdot}17{\cdot}19\to$ essential $83>19$).
- **"termination by decreasing $|\mathcal M_n|$ or total size":** FALSE
  ($a_1=2310$ grows monotonically to $251$ sets before collapsing to $\{\{2\}\}$
  at step $893$).
- **"detect stabilization by no-new-prime-set":** wrong proxy; non-minimal supersets
  appear forever (e.g. $a_1=15$: factor $7$ in $42,84,\ldots$ is never essential).

## Evidence (computation, NOT proof — all independently reproduced here)
- **Self-duality of the stable family:** for every $a_1\in[2,250]$ and targeted hard
  cases ($2310,2730,385,7777,4199,14641,1024,6561,\ldots$) the stabilized
  $\mathcal M$ satisfies $b(\mathcal M)=\mathcal M$ exactly (0 failures).
- **Cofactor lemma:** holds for every essential-entry event in all tested cases.
- **Bound $Q\le a_1$** ($Q$=largest essential prime): 0 violations for $a_1\le250$
  and all targeted cases (tight: $Q=a_1$ iff $a_1$ is prime; prime powers collapse to
  $Q=$ that prime). Remains a *conjecture*.
- $a_1=15$: $\mathcal M=\{\{3,5\},\{2,3\},\{2,5\}\}$ (self-dual), $L=30$, $T=8$,
  $a_{n+8}=a_n+30$ from $n=1$.
- $a_1=385$: stabilizes step $37$ (7 sets, self-dual), $L=43890$, $T=5088$,
  $a_{n+5088}=a_n+43890$ for $n=1,\ldots,5$.
- $a_1=2310$: grows to $251$ sets, collapses to $\{\{2\}\}$ at step $893$;
  $T=1,L=2$.

## Open issues
1. **Prove $\mathcal M_n$ reaches a self-dual antichain** (≡ finitely many essential
   primes). This is the sole gap. The cofactor lemma (4) bounds each essential prime
   by the state at its entry; a bound on *when* defects stop being realized would
   close the proof. Best conjectured explicit bound: every essential prime $\le a_1$.
2. Turn (4) + the "defects are finite" intuition into a termination argument.

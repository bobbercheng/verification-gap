## Problem
Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu Bang marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang Yu marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu Bang going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces. For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu Bang may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang Yu's play.

## Approaches tried
- Modeling: claiming game with "pick any piece" — proved (and verified by brute force) that optimal play = greedy, so LB gets odd-position pieces (sorted decreasing). Game reduces to: LB picks ≤ n+1 pieces (composition of 1), XY subdivides with ≤ n cuts, payoff = alternating sum $D = p_1 - p_2 + p_3 - \cdots$; LB share $= (1+D)/2$.
- Grid computation (code/game.py): exact max-min on grids of $K$ atoms. n=1: $D^*=1/3$, $c=2/3$; n=2: $D^*=1/7$, $c=4/7$; n=3: $D^*=1/15$, $c=8/15$.
- Conjecture: $c_n = \dfrac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$, LB playing geometric pieces $\frac{2^{i-1}}{2^{n+1}-1}$, $i=1,\dots,n+1$.
- Parity reduction: deleting equal pairs from a multiset doesn't change $D$. XY effective ops: "halve a piece" (delete from pool) and "cancel $x\ge y$" (replace $x,y$ by $x-y$), each 1 cut.
- NEW THIS SESSION (not yet coded/verified — see below):
  - **Matching formula**: $D(M) = \min_{\mu}(\sum_{\{p,q\}\in\mu}|p-q| + \sum_{i\text{ unmatched}} p_i)$ over matchings $\mu$ of the multiset $M$. Proof: $D = S - 2\sum_k p_{2k}$ and $\sum_k p_{2k} = \max_\mu \sum\min(p,q)$ by an exchange argument.
  - **Part A proof (lower bound) SKETCH — looks complete**: family $a_1\le\cdots\le a_m$ super-increasing with margin $\delta$ ($a_1\ge\delta$, $a_{i+1}\ge\delta+a_1+\cdots+a_i$), subdivided with $\le m-1$ cuts ⇒ $D\ge\delta$. Proof: take optimal matching $\mu$; build multigraph on pieces (edge = matched pair of fragments, unmatched frag = mark). Components. Cyclic components (E≥V) have slack $\ge 1$ where slack(K) = frags(K) − 2·pieces(K) + 1; total slack = $c - m + \#\text{comp} \le \#\text{comp}-1$ (since cuts $c\le m-1$) ⇒ at least one TREE component. For a tree component on piece-set $I$: bipartite signs give $w_I := |\sum_{i\in I}\epsilon_i a_i| \le$ cost(component) (algebra: $\sum\epsilon_i a_i = \sum_{\text{edges}}(x_e - y_e) + \sum_i \epsilon_i U_i$, then triangle ineq). Then cost $\ge \sum_{\text{tree }K} w_K \ge$ alt-sum$(w_K) \ge \delta$ by **block lemma**: for disjoint blocks with $v_B = |\sum_{i\in B}\epsilon_i a_i|$, alt-sum$(v_B)\ge\delta$ (proof: block $B^*$ containing largest index $k^*$ has $v_{B^*}\ge a_{k^*} - \sum_{i<k^*}a_i \ge \delta + \sum_{B\ne B^*} v_B$, since $v_B \le \sum_{i\in B} a_i$ and all other blocks' indices $< k^*$; hence $v_{B^*}$ strictly largest, alt-sum $= v_{B^*} - \text{alt}(\text{rest}) \ge v_{B^*} - \sum\text{rest} \ge \delta$).
  - Subtle point handled: odd cycles in the matching multigraph break the per-component inequality $w\le$cost (checked numerically by hand: triangle counterexample), so cyclic components are DROPPED; slack counting ensures ≥1 tree component remains. Pieces are partitioned among components with all their fragments (component = connected component).
- Part B (upper bound): reduce to $m=n+1$ pieces (if LB uses $m\le n$ pieces, XY halves every piece: $m\le n$ cuts ⇒ $D=0$). Need: any $n+1$ positive numbers summing to $S$ admit an XY strategy (≤ n ops) with $D \le T_n S$, $T_n = 1/(2^{n+1}-1)$.
  - Pigeonhole: $2^{n+1}$ signed sums $\sum\epsilon_i a_i \in [-S,S]$ ⇒ two within $2S/(2^{n+1}-1)$ ⇒ difference gives $\delta\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}$ with $|\sum\delta_i a_i| \le T_n S$. BUT such a signed sum is NOT always realizable by cancel/chain ops (e.g. $\{1,2\}$: only $|2-1|$ realizable, not $2+1$). Realizability gap.
  - Empirics from prev session: min over subsets of greedy-chain seems $\le T_n$ (allchains 0 fails for n=2,3). Need to verify harder + prove.

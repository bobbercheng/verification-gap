## Problem
Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let points $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively. Let points $K$ and $L$ be chosen inside triangles $BMC$ and $BNC$, respectively, such that $K$ lies inside the angle $LBA$, $L$ lies inside the angle $ACK$, and $\angle KBA = \angle ACL$, $\angle LBK = \angle LNC$, $\angle LCK = \angle BMK$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AKL$. Prove that $OM = ON$.

## Approaches tried
- **Angle coordinates:** Set
  \[x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL,\quad y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC,\quad z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK.
  \]
  Normalize $A=(0,0),B=(2,0)$, so $M=(1,0)$, and put $C=2N$.
- **Raw Cartesian/complex elimination:** Verified the theorem but first gave unwieldy formulas.
- **Tangent/vector determinant factorization:** Found the compact relation between the target power determinant and the $CK$ incidence cross product.
- **Homogeneous trigonometric version (successful):** This removes every tangent/right-angle issue. Let $R_\theta$ denote rotation by $\theta$ and identify points with vectors. Sine law gives
  \[
  K=\left(1+\frac{\sin x\cos z}{\sin(x+z)},\frac{\sin x\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}\right).
  \]
  Parametrize the ray $BL$ by the actual distance $t=BL>0$:
  \[
  L=(2-t\cos(x+y),t\sin(x+y)).
  \]
  In triangle $CNL$, since $CN=N$ and the relevant angles are $x$ and $y$, one has
  \[
  L=\lambda N,
  \quad
  \lambda=1+\frac{\sin x}{\sin(x+y)}(\cos y,-\sin y).
  \]
  The last incidence $CK$ says
  \[
  Q:=(K-2N)\times R_{x+z}N=0.
  \]
  Define the equal-power determinant
  \[
  D=\det\begin{pmatrix}K_x&K_y&|K|^2\\L_x&L_y&|L|^2\\N_x-1&N_y&|N|^2-1\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Homogenizing and exactly simplifying the tangent identity gives the exceptionally clean factorization
  \[
  \boxed{
  D=\frac{\sin x\,(t\sin x+\sin y)}{\sin(x+y)\sin(x+z)}\,Q.}
  \]
  This identity has been verified directly from the three displayed vector formulas, with only $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$ used; no exceptional right-angle cases remain.

## Current best

The determinant $D$ is the difference-of-powers condition for $M=(1,0)$ and $N$ relative to $(AKL)$, so $OM=ON$.

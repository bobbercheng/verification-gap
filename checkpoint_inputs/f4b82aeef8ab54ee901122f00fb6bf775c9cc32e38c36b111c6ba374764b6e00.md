## Problem
Let $n$ be a positive integer. Liu Bang and Xiang Yu have a stick of length $1$ and want to divide it between themselves. Liu Bang marks at most $n$ points on the stick, and then Xiang Yu marks at most $n$ points on the stick. The marked points are distinct. Then, the stick is cut at all marked points, creating a number of pieces. Afterwards, they take turns claiming any unclaimed piece of the stick, with Liu Bang going first. Each player's goal is to maximise the total length of their own pieces. For each $n$, determine the largest value $c$ such that Liu Bang may guarantee a total length of at least $c$, regardless of Xiang Yu's play.

## Approaches tried
- Modeling: claiming game with "pick any piece" — optimal play = greedy (proved, brute-force verified). Game reduces to: LB picks ≤ n+1 pieces, XY subdivides with ≤ n cuts; payoff $D$ = alternating sum; LB share $=(1+D)/2$.
- Grid computation: $c_1=2/3, c_2=4/7, c_3=8/15$ ⇒ conjecture $c_n = \frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}$, LB plays geometric pieces $\frac{2^{i-1}}{2^{n+1}-1}$.
- Parity reduction: equal pairs cancel in $D$. XY ops: halve (delete piece), cancel $x\ge y$ (→ $x-y$), 1 cut each.
- **Matching formula** (verified, code/tests2.py): $D(M)=\min_\mu(\sum|p-q|+\sum_{\text{unm}}p)$.
- **Block lemma** (verified, 3000 trials): disjoint blocks of super-increasing family (margin δ), values $v_B=|\sum_{i\in B}\epsilon_i a_i|$ ⇒ alt-sum$(v_B)\ge\delta$. Proof: block with largest index has value $\ge a_{k^*}-\sum_{<k^*}a_i \ge \delta + \sum$other blocks.
- **Part A PROVED (modulo write-up)**: super-increasing family (margin δ, m pieces), subdivided ≤ m−1 cuts ⇒ $D\ge\delta$. Via matching formula + component decomposition (multigraph on pieces; cyclic comps have slack≥1; total slack ≤ #comp−1 ⇒ ≥1 tree comp) + tree inequality $|\sum_{i\in I}\epsilon_i a_i|\le$cost(comp) + block lemma. Verified 1500 random trials.
- Dominance "min-subdiv=min-h/c": FALSE on coarse grids (grid artifacts: can't halve 1-atom piece); not needed since Part A done via matchings.
- Part B: min over subsets of greedy-chain ≤ $T_n S$ ($T_n=1/(2^{n+1}-1)$): verified n=1..4 (2000 random each); adversarial max = $T_n$ exactly at geometric family. Interval chains also suffice empirically.

## Current best

# IMO 2026 Problem 6 – Candidate Solution (v1, Partial with Explicit Gaps)

**Problem.** Let $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ be an infinite sequence of integers $> 1$ such that for every positive integer $n$,
$$a_{n+1} = \min\{ m \in \mathbb{Z} \mid m > a_n \text{ and } \gcd(m, a_i) > 1 \text{ for all } i = 1, \dots, n \}.$$
Prove that there exist positive integers $T$ and $L$ such that
$$a_{n+T} = a_n + L \qquad \text{for every } n \ge 1.$$

---

## Notation and Key Definitions

- For integer $x > 1$, $P(x)$ = set of prime divisors of $x$.
- $P_n = P(a_n)$.
- A set $H$ of primes is a **hitting set** for a family $\mathcal{F}$ of prime sets if $H \cap F \ne \varnothing$ for all $F \in \mathcal{F}$.
- $H$ is **minimal** if no proper subset is a hitting set.
- $\mathcal{M}_n$ = family of all minimal hitting sets for $\{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$.
- For $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$, $m_H = \prod_{p \in H} p$.
- **Essential primes** $\mathcal{E} = \{ p : p \in P_n \text{ for infinitely many } n \}$.
- **Ubiquitous primes** $\mathcal{D} = \{ p : p \mid a_n \text{ for all sufficiently large } n \}$.
- Clearly $\mathcal{D} \subseteq \mathcal{E}$.

---

## Key Observation (Verified)

$a_{n+1}$ is the smallest integer $> a_n$ that is a multiple of $m_H$ for some $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$.

*Reason:* $\gcd(m, a_i) > 1\ \forall i \iff P(m)$ is a hitting set $\iff m$ is a multiple of $m_H$ for some $H \in \mathcal{M}_n$. Minimality of $a_{n+1}$ forces it to be the smallest such multiple.

**Important correction:** $P(a_{n+1})$ is a hitting set for $\{P_1,\dots,P_n\}$ but **not necessarily minimal**.
Counterexample: $a_1=6, a_2=8, a_3=10$; $P(a_3)=\{2,5\}$ but minimal hitting set for $\{\{2,3\},\{2\}\}$ is $\{2\}$.

---

## Part 1: Nonemptiness of Essential Primes (PROVED)

**Lemma 1.** $\mathcal{E} \ne \varnothing$.

*Proof.* $P_1$ is finite. For every $n \ge 2$, $\gcd(a_n, a_1) > 1$, so $P_n \cap P_1 \ne \varnothing$. By pigeonhole principle, some $p \in P_1$ belongs to infinitely many $P_n$. ∎

---

## Part 2: Finiteness of All Primes (OPEN — Critical Gap)

**Lemma 2 (Conjectured).** $\mathcal{P}^* = \bigcup_{n \ge 1} P_n$ is finite.

*Status:* **Unproven.** This is the core bottleneck of the problem.

*Prior failed attempts:* A CRT construction $x_k \equiv 0 \pmod{p_k}$, $x_k \equiv 1 \pmod{p_i}\ (i<k)$ produces a number **coprime** to all previous terms — the opposite of a valid candidate.

*Standard approach (not yet executed rigorously here):* Let $p_1 < p_2 < \dots$ be the distinct primes appearing, and $n_k$ the first index where $p_k$ appears. Then $P(a_{n_k})$ is a hitting set for $\mathcal{F}_{n_k-1}$ and must contain $p_k$. If infinitely many primes appear, one must construct a valid candidate using only the first $k-1$ primes that is smaller than $a_{n_k}$, contradicting minimality. The difficulty: $P(a_{n_k})$ need not be minimal, so the hitting set containing $p_k$ might not be minimal; one must work with the actual minimal hitting set $H \in \mathcal{M}_{n_k-1}$ that yields $a_{n_k}$ via its product $m_H$.

---

## Part 3: Stabilization of Minimal Hitting Sets (Conditional on Lemma 2)

Assume $\mathcal{P}^*$ is finite. There are only finitely many families of subsets of $\mathcal{P}^*$. As $n$ increases, $\mathcal{F}_n = \{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$ grows, so any hitting set for $\mathcal{F}_{n+1}$ is also a hitting set for $\mathcal{F}_n$. The family of *minimal* hitting sets $\mathcal{M}_n$ can only shrink (in the sense of inclusion of families). Hence $\mathcal{M}_n$ eventually stabilizes to a fixed family $\mathcal{M} \subseteq 2^{\mathcal{P}^*}$.

**Lemma 3 (Conditional).** If $\mathcal{P}^*$ is finite, there exists $N_0$ such that $\mathcal{M}_n = \mathcal{M}$ for all $n \ge N_0$.

*Status:* **Conditional on Lemma 2.** The state space is finite and the transition $\mathcal{M}_{n+1} \subseteq \{ H \in \mathcal{M}_n : H \text{ hits } P_{n+1} \} \cup \text{new minimal sets formed by adding primes to sets in } \mathcal{M}_n$ ensures eventual stabilization. A fully rigorous argument must show no new minimal hitting sets appear after some point (this follows because $P_{n+1} \subseteq \mathcal{P}^*$ and the operation of taking minimal hitting sets of an expanding family on a finite ground set eventually stabilizes).

---

## Part 4: Ubiquitous Prime Lemma (Correct Version) — Hypothesis to Verify

**Conjecture (Ubiquitous Prime Lemma).** Let $\mathcal{M}$ be the stabilized family of minimal hitting sets. Define $\mathcal{D} = \bigcap_{H \in \mathcal{M}} H$. Then:
1. $\mathcal{D} \ne \varnothing$.
2. Every $p \in \mathcal{D}$ divides $a_n$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
3. Every $H \in \mathcal{M}$ contains $\mathcal{D}$.
4. $\mathcal{E} \setminus \mathcal{D}$ are precisely the essential primes that do **not** eventually divide all terms.

*Status:* **Not yet verified experimentally across many $a_1$.** This lemma replaces the false "all essential primes are ubiquitous" claim.

*Experimental data from prior runs:*
| $a_1$ | $\mathcal{M}$ (stabilized) | $\bigcap \mathcal{M}$ | $T$ | $L$ |
|-------|----------------------------|----------------------|-----|-----|
| 6     | $\{\{2\}\}$                | $\{2\}$              | 1   | 2   |
| 15    | $\{\{2,3\},\{2,5\},\{3,5\}\}$ | $\varnothing$      | 8   | 30  |
| 21    | $\{\{3\}\}$                | $\{3\}$              | 1   | 3   |
| 30    | $\{\{2\}\}$                | $\{2\}$              | 1   | 2   |
| 105   | $\{\{2,3\},\{2,5\},\{2,7\}\}$ | $\{2\}$             | 19  | 69  |
| 1155  | $\{\{2,3\},\{2,5\},\{2,7\},\{2,11\}\}$ | $\{2\}$ | 30 | 1610 |

For $a_1=15$, no prime divides all sufficiently large terms, yet $a_{n+8}=a_n+30$ holds for all $n$. Periodicity comes from the merge-of-progressions structure, **not** from ubiquitous primes.

## Part 5: Periodicity of the Tail (Conditional on Lemmas 2–4)

Assume $\mathcal{P}^*$ finite, $\mathcal{M}_n$ stabilizes to $\mathcal{M}$, and $\mathcal{D} = \bigcap_{H \in \mathcal{M}} H$ is the set of ubiquitous primes.

Let $L = \mathrm{lcm}\{ m_H \mid H \in \mathcal{M} \}$. For $n \ge N_0$, the valid candidates for $a_{n+1}$ are exactly the multiples of $m_H$ for $H \in \mathcal{M}$. The greedy rule picks the smallest such multiple $> a_n$. Therefore the tail $(a_n)_{n \ge N_0}$ is exactly the increasing merge of the arithmetic progressions
$$A_H = \{ k \cdot m_H \mid k \ge 1 \}, \qquad H \in \mathcal{M}.$$

Since each $m_H \mid L$, membership in $\bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}} A_H$ depends only on the residue class modulo $L$. Let
$$R = \{ r \bmod L \mid r \in \bigcup_{H \in \mathcal{M}} A_H \}$$
be the set of covered residues, and let $T = |R|$. Listing the elements of the merge in increasing order visits the residues in $R$ cyclically; after $T$ steps the value increases by exactly $L$.

**Lemma 4 (Conditional).** For all sufficiently large $n$,
$$a_{n+T} = a_n + L.$$

*Proof.* The sequence is exactly the ordered list of integers with residues in $R$. After $T$ steps the residue pattern repeats and the value increases by $L$. ∎

## Part 6: Backward Extension to All $n$ (OPEN — Requires Correct Translation Invariance)

**Lemma 5 (Conjectured).** If $a_{n+T} = a_n + L$ holds for all $n \ge N$, then it holds for all $n \ge 1$.

*Status:* **Unproven.** Prior attempts used the false identity $\gcd(x+L, y+L) = \gcd(x,y)$ (counterexample: $\gcd(6+30, 10+30) = 4 \ne 2 = \gcd(6,10)$).

*Correct approach (sketch):* The recurrence $a_{n+1} = \min\{ m > a_n : P(m) \text{ hits } \{P_1,\dots,P_n\} \}$ with initial condition $a_1$ has a unique solution. The shifted sequence $b_n = a_{n+T} - L$ satisfies $b_1 = a_{1+T} - L$ and, **for the stabilized tail**, the valid-candidate condition is translation-invariant modulo $L$ because all $m_H \mid L$ and the hitting condition depends only on prime factors from $\mathcal{E}$. If one can prove that $b_n$ satisfies the same greedy recurrence for all $n$ (or that the state of the sequence, defined by the tuple of recent prime factor sets, is eventually periodic and determinism forces full periodicity), then $a_n = b_n$ for all $n$ by uniqueness. This requires rigorous justification of translation invariance of the hitting condition for early terms.

*Key sub-lemma needed:* Every early term $a_i$ ($i < N$) shares at least one essential prime with the tail. Otherwise all its prime factors are non-essential (appear only finitely often), so they must disappear from the sequence before $N$ — contradiction if $i < N$ and the prime appears at $i$.

## Open Issues Requiring Resolution (Priority Order)

1. **Prime Finiteness (Lemma 2):** Provide a correct minimality-contradiction proof. Key idea: at the first appearance $n_k$ of the $k$-th new prime $p_k$, all previous terms use only the first $k-1$ primes. Show that some multiple of a product of these $k-1$ primes must hit all previous terms and is smaller than $a_{n_k}$, contradicting minimality. Must handle the case where $P(a_{n_k})$ is not minimal.

2. **Ubiquitous Prime Lemma (Claim 4):** Prove $\mathcal{D} = \bigcap_{H \in \mathcal{M}} H$ is nonempty and exactly the set of primes eventually dividing all terms. Falsify or verify experimentally across many $a_1$.

3. **Stabilization Mechanism:** Prove $\mathcal{M}_n$ stabilizes without assuming $\mathcal{D}$ is ubiquitous (avoid circularity). Test monotone potential functions ($\Phi_1(n) = |\{H \in \mathcal{M}_n : H \not\subseteq \mathcal{E}\}|$, $\Phi_2(n) = \sum_{H \in \mathcal{M}_n} |H \setminus \mathcal{E}|$, $\Phi_3(n) = \text{sorted tuple of } m_H$) or the "active family" approach ($A_n = \{H \in \mathcal{M}_n : m_H \mid a_{n+1}\}$).

4. **Exact Minimal Hitting Sets:** Prove that eventually $P(a_n)$ equals a minimal hitting set exactly (no extra primes). Use the bounded-gap property of the merge: the distance between consecutive multiples of any $m_H$ is at most $m_H \le L$. Extra prime factors would force skipping valid candidates.

5. **Backward Extension (Lemma 5):** Establish translation invariance of the hitting condition modulo $L$ for the full sequence. Every early prime is either essential (periodic mod $L$) or non-essential (disappears before stabilization). Uniqueness of the greedy sequence given $a_1$ then forces the periodic pattern to propagate backward.

6. **Exact Period Parameters:** Prove $T = |R|$ and $L = \mathrm{lcm}\{m_H \mid H \in \mathcal{M}\}$ (not $\prod_{p \in \mathcal{E}} p$, which may be larger — e.g., $a_1=105$ has $\mathcal{E}=\{2,3,5,7\}$, $\prod\mathcal{E}=210$ but $L=69$).

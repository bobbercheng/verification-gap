# Candidate v1 — complete

The exact optimal guarantee is
\[
\boxed{c_n=\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}}.
\]

Liu attains it by making the initial pieces proportional to
\(1,2,4,\ldots,2^n\). The proof below gives both bounds and also accounts for Xiang using fewer than \(n\) marks.

## 1. The drafting discrepancy

Let the final piece lengths, in nonincreasing order, be
\[
x_1\ge x_2\ge\cdots\ge x_r>0,
\]
and put
\[
D(x_1,\ldots,x_r)=x_1-x_2+x_3-x_4+\cdots.
\]

**Drafting lemma.** Under optimal play, the player moving first takes total length
\[
x_1+x_3+x_5+\cdots=\frac{1+D}{2}.
\]

Indeed, let \(V(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\) denote the moving player's final advantage (their total minus the opponent's). Backward induction gives
\[
V(x_1,\ldots,x_r)=\max_i\bigl(x_i-V(x_1,\ldots,\widehat{x_i},\ldots,x_r)\bigr).
\]
Assume inductively that \(V\) on every smaller multiset is its alternating sorted sum. Choosing \(x_1\) gives exactly \(D\). If \(x_i\) is chosen instead, subtracting the resulting advantage from \(D\) gives
\[
2\sum_{j<i}(-1)^{j+1}x_j
\bigl((-1)^{i+1}-1\bigr)x_i.
\]
For odd \(i\), this is a sum of adjacent nonnegative gaps. For even \(i\), it is twice
\[
(x_1-x_2)+\cdots +(x_{i-3}-x_{i-2})+(x_{i-1}-x_i),
\]
also nonnegative. Thus choosing a largest piece is optimal and \(V=D\). Since the two totals sum to \(1\), the first player's total is \((1+D)/2\).

It remains to optimize \(D\) after the two marking stages.

## 2. A refinement lemma

Suppose Liu's first-stage cuts produce \(q\) positive intervals of lengths
\(a_1,\ldots,a_q\), with sum \(1\). Define their minimum subset-sum gap by
\[
\Gamma(a_1,\ldots,a_q)
=\min_{A\ne B}
\left|\sum_{i\in A}a_i-\sum_{i\in B}a_i\right|,
\tag{1}
\]
where \(A,B\) range over all subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,q\}\). The value may be zero.

**Refinement lemma.** If Xiang may add at most \(q-1\) cuts, then the least possible final drafting discrepancy is exactly \(\Gamma(a_1,\ldots,a_q)\).

### Lower bound in the refinement lemma

Suppose Xiang adds \(k\le q-1\) cuts, producing \(r=q+k\le2q-1\) final pieces. Sort them, and if \(r\) is odd append one formal piece of length \(0\). Pair consecutive entries:
\[
(x_1,x_2),(x_3,x_4),\ldots.
\]

Construct a multigraph as follows. Its real vertices are Liu's \(q\) original intervals. A pair of final pieces gives an edge between the two original intervals containing them; loops and parallel edges are allowed. If a formal zero was appended, add one dummy vertex of weight \(0\) and join it to the parent of the last real piece. Every vertex is incident with an edge.

Let \(\varepsilon=1\) when the dummy is present and \(0\) otherwise. The graph has
\[
V=q+\varepsilon,
\qquad
E=\frac{r+\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
If \(r\) is even, then \(E\le q-1<V\); if \(r\) is odd, then \(E\le q<V=q+1\). Hence \(E<V\). Consequently, at least one connected component is bipartite: if every component were non-bipartite, each would contain a cycle and hence have at least as many edges as vertices.

Take a bipartition \(U,W\) of such a component. The absolute imbalance of its vertex weights is a difference of two distinct subset sums, so it is at least \(\Gamma\). On each edge, the two endpoint contributions are the lengths of the corresponding paired pieces. Since every edge crosses from \(U\) to \(W\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
\Gamma
\le
\left|\sum_{i\in U}a_i-\sum_{j\in W}a_j\right|
\le
\sum_{\text{edges in the component}}|\text{endpoint lengths' difference}|
\le D.
\tag{2}
\]
A loop cannot lie in the chosen bipartite component, and the formal zero causes no difficulty. Thus every permitted refinement has \(D\ge\Gamma\).

### Upper bound in the refinement lemma

Choose distinct subsets attaining (1), cancel their intersection, and interchange them if necessary. We obtain disjoint sets \(A,B\) such that
\[
S_A:=\sum_{i\in A}a_i\ge S_B:=\sum_{j\in B}a_j,
\qquad S_A-S_B=\Gamma.
\tag{3}
\]

First suppose \(B\ne\varnothing\). Virtually concatenate the intervals indexed by \(A\) into \([0,S_A]\), and those indexed by \(B\) into \([0,S_B]\). Overlay the two sets of existing endpoints on \([0,S_B]\), cutting an original interval whenever an endpoint from the other concatenation lies in its interior. Every cell in \([0,S_B]\) now occurs twice, once on each side, so these cells form equal pairs. The unpaired tail on the \(A\)-side has total length \(S_A-S_B=\Gamma\).

At most \(|B|\) new cuts are made on the \(A\)-side (the \(|B|-1\) internal endpoints of the \(B\)-concatenation and possibly \(S_B\)), and at most \(|A|-1\) on the \(B\)-side. For every index outside \(A\cup B\), Xiang bisects that interval, producing one further equal pair. The total number of cuts is at most
\[
|A|+|B|-1+q-|A|-|B|=q-1.
\]

If \(B=\varnothing\), leave the \(A\)-intervals as the residual pieces and bisect every interval outside \(A\). This uses \(q-|A|\le q-1\) cuts, and the residual total is again \(S_A=\Gamma\).

Thus, in all cases, the final multiset consists of equal pairs together with residual pieces of total length \(\Gamma\). Removing two equal entries from a sorted alternating sum does not change \(D\), while the discrepancy of the residual multiset is at most its total. Hence \(D\le\Gamma\), completing the refinement lemma.

## 3. Xiang's upper bound

Let \(q\) be the number of positive intervals after Liu's marks, so \(1\le q\le n+1\).

If \(q\le n\), Xiang bisects all \(q\) intervals. Every final length then occurs in an equal pair, so \(D=0\) and Liu receives exactly \(1/2\), already no more than the claimed bound.

It remains to consider \(q=n+1\). There are \(2^q\) subset sums of \(a_1,\ldots,a_q\), all in \([0,1]\), including \(0\) and \(1\). After sorting these sums (with multiplicity), two consecutive ones differ by at most
\[
\frac1{2^q-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Gamma(a_1,\ldots,a_q)\le\frac1{2^q-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
By the refinement lemma, Xiang can use at most \(q-1=n\) cuts and ensure
\[
D\le\frac1{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]
The drafting lemma then gives
\[
\text{Liu's total}
\le\frac12\left(1+\frac1{2^{n+1}-1}\right)
=\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\tag{5}
\]

## 4. Liu's matching lower bound

Set
\[
\delta=\frac1{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]
Liu makes exactly \(n\) cuts so that the \(n+1\) initial interval lengths are
\[
\delta,2\delta,4\delta,\ldots,2^n\delta.
\tag{6}
\]
For example, in this order the marks are at \((2^k-1)\delta\), \(1\le k\le n\).

Every subset sum of (6) is a distinct integer multiple of \(\delta\), and binary expansion gives every multiple
\[
0,\delta,2\delta,\ldots,(2^{n+1}-1)\delta=1.
\]
Consequently \(\Gamma=\delta\). The lower half of the refinement lemma says that every placement of at most \(n=(n+1)-1\) Xiang cuts leaves \(D\ge\delta\). Therefore Liu always receives at least
\[
\frac{1+\delta}{2}
=\boxed{\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}}.
\]
Together with (5), this proves optimality.

## 5. Boundary cases and assumptions

- The problem states \(n\ge1\). For reference, the same formula would give \(1\) at \(n=0\), which is the uncut game.
- Only effective interior marks matter. If endpoint marks are permitted by the wording, they create no cut and may be discarded. All constructed marks lie in interiors; coincident virtual overlay boundaries require no new mark, so the count can only decrease.
- Ties among final lengths do not affect the alternating sum. Equal copies may be placed adjacently within a tie class.
- Xiang is allowed *at most* \(n\) marks. Both the graph lower bound and the construction explicitly allow fewer.

The first values are
\[
c_1=\frac23,\qquad c_2=\frac47,\qquad c_3=\frac8{15}.
\]

## 6. Hypotheses and falsification record

1. **Universal fairness hypothesis: \(c_n=1/2\).** This is suggested by bisecting pieces into equal pairs. It already fails for \(n=1\): Liu's lengths \(1/3,2/3\) have subset-sum gap \(1/3\), and the graph argument forces \(D\ge1/3\), hence a total at least \(2/3\).
2. **Equal-interval hypothesis:** equal initial pieces, with Xiang splitting all but one in half, suggests \((n+2)/(2(n+1))\). It fails because Xiang may use fewer cuts. If \(n+1\) is even, the equal pieces already cancel in pairs. If \(n+1\) is odd, Xiang bisects just one interval; the remaining full pieces and the two half-pieces all cancel in equal pairs. In both cases Liu gets only \(1/2\).
3. **Binary subset-spacing hypothesis: \(c_n=2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)\).** It was attacked by allowing every allocation of Xiang's cuts, odd and even final piece counts, and arbitrary interlacing of descendants of different initial intervals. The refinement lemma handles all these attacks. The subset-sum pigeonhole bound proves no initial partition can do better, while binary lengths attain equality.

A small-case linear-program enumeration of all final rank/parent patterns for \(n=2\) also selected the ratio \(4:2:1\) and value \(4/7\). This was used only as hypothesis-generating evidence; the proof above is independent of the computation.
